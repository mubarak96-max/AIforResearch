# Shared content for the three UI style mockups

The three style proposals (A, B, C) show the same product, the same navigation names and the same sample data, so they can be compared on look and layout alone. Everything marked *sample* is illustration and must be replaced before launch.

## Product

- Name: **AI for Research** (aiforresearch.app). Wordmark in text; mark: a simple drawn symbol from the name or its object (a citation marker), never a letter in a black square.
- What it is: an AI research workspace. Ask a question, get an answer built from real papers with citations you can open, then review, analyse, write and present, from first question to thesis defence.
- Who: students (undergraduate, honours, master's, PhD), researchers, academic staff, clinicians, industry analysts. Every field: medicine, life sciences, data and computing, engineering, environmental studies, social sciences, humanities, business.
- Real facts that may be stated: 250M+ scholarly works (OpenAlex catalogue); 10,000+ citation styles; Word, PDF and LaTeX in and out; Zotero and Mendeley import. No invented customers, quotes, logos, ratings or user counts.

## Landing page (in this order)

1. **Hero.** One h1, one supporting sentence, and the working **Ask box**:
   - a text field ("Ask a research question"), sample question *Does sleep after learning improve memory consolidation?*
   - mode switch: **Summary** (a short cited answer, 1 credit) · **Deep** (a structured report from 20–40 papers, 5 credits) · **Detailed** (a long report with tables from 50+ papers, 20 credits)

   - "Attach PDF" (also .doc, .docx, .md, .txt), and the Ask button
   - Headline options (pick or write one, two lines at most): "Research with real sources" · "Ask. Read. Write. Defend."
   - Supporting sentence: "Ask a question and get an answer built from 250M+ papers, every claim cited. Then review, analyse and write, up to your thesis defence."
   - Primary action: "Ask a question" (free, no card).
2. **Three callouts** directly under the Ask box:
   - "Every claim cites a real paper you can open."
   - "Your PDFs, Zotero and Mendeley in one Library."
   - "From the answer to a thesis: review, analyse, write, present."
3. **How it works** (the illustration): Ask → Read the evidence → Build on it.
   - First, ask: the question with mode and sources.
   - Then read: the cited answer, with a consensus line ("14 of 18 studies report a benefit") and the source list with filters (year, study type, journal quality).
   - Then build: one click turns the answer into a literature review, a slide deck, a gap analysis or a draft in the editor.
4. **Who it is for** (each links to its own landing page): Undergraduates `/for/undergraduates` · Master's students `/for/masters` · PhD candidates `/for/phd` · Academic staff and supervisors `/for/academics` · Clinicians and medical researchers `/for/medicine` · Industry and policy analysts `/for/industry`. 5. **Features** (capabilities, five):

   - Filter by year, study type, journal quality and citations.
   - Research in chat, or write in the editor with suggestions and citations.
   - Share a project with your supervisor or co-authors.
6. **Tools** (nine, each opens its page): Deep Research · Find Papers · Chat with PDF · Library · Thesis Suite (AI thesis) · Proposal Studio · Thesis to Manuscript · Citation Generator · Text Humanizer (natural-style rewrite). Link: "All 40+ tools".
7. **Pricing** (see below).
8. **FAQ** (seven): Where do the sources come from? · Can it make up a citation? · Is it allowed at my university? · What do I get for free? · What is a credit? · Can I use my own PDFs and Zotero library? · Can I cancel any time?
9. **Footer.**

### Screen 2: Deep Research result (`#research`)

Question it answers: "What does the evidence say, and can I trust it?"
Finding (*sample figure*): "14 of 18 studies report better recall after sleep than after the same time awake."
Most frequent job: turn the answer into the next piece of work (the follow-on tools).

Regions:
- The question as the title; under it mode "Deep", sources "All papers", "32 papers read · 18 cited · 2 min 40 s" (*sample*).
- Filters on the sources: Year (2000–2026) · Study type (RCT, meta-analysis, review, cohort, qualitative) · Journal quality (Q1–Q4) · Citations · Open access · "Exclude retracted" on.
- The answer, with headings (Summary · What the evidence shows · Where studies disagree · Limits · Open questions) and numbered citation markers [1]–[5] that link to the source rows. Sample summary: "Sleep in the hours after learning improves later recall of declarative material compared with an equal period awake [1][2]. The effect is linked to slow-wave sleep and hippocampal replay [2][3], and is larger for material learned with intent to remember [4]. The idea is old: forgetting was shown to be slower across sleep than across waking in 1924 [5]."
- A consensus strip (*sample*): Supports 14 · Mixed 3 · Does not support 1 (of 18 cited studies; the parts sum to the total).
- Sources (real papers; show type and year; citation counts are *sample* and may be left out):
  1. Diekelmann S, Born J. The memory function of sleep. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 2010. Review.
  2. Rasch B, Born J. About sleep's role in memory. *Physiological Reviews*, 2013. Review.
  3. Stickgold R. Sleep-dependent memory consolidation. *Nature*, 2005. Review.
  4. Walker MP, Stickgold R. Sleep, memory, and plasticity. *Annual Review of Psychology*, 2006. Review.
  5. Jenkins JG, Dallenbach KM. Obliviscence during sleep and waking. *American Journal of Psychology*, 1924. Experiment.
- Follow-on tools: Write a literature review · Find gaps · Check citations · Make slides · Open in editor · Save all to Library.
- A follow-up question field ("Ask a follow-up").

### Screen 3: Reviewer setup (`#reviewer`)

Question it answers: "What is being reviewed, at what level, and what will it cost?"
Most frequent job: start the review.

- Document type: **Thesis** · Proposal · Manuscript.
- Level: Undergraduate · Honours · **Master's** · PhD.
- Quality: **Standard** (comments and a scorecard, 10 credits) · High quality (deeper, with tables and graphs, 30 credits).
- Document: *Chapter 2: Literature review.docx*, 9,840 words, 41 references (*sample*). Options: upload, pick from documents, add from Library.
- Checks included (switches): Structure and argument · Method and statistics · Citations exist and support the claim · Clarity and style · Formatting against the university template.
- Summary: "Standard review · 10 credits · about 4 minutes · 412 credits left". Primary action: "Start review".
- Last review (*sample*): Chapter 2, yesterday: 3 major, 7 minor comments; scores Structure 7/10, Method 6/10, Citations 8/10, Clarity 7/10.
