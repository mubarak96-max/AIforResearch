# WriteOrbit messaging

The reference for all site copy. When you change a page, check it against this file first.

## Audience

1. **Primary:** students, researchers and academics writing papers, theses, literature reviews, essays and proposals.
2. **Secondary:** anyone else who writes with sources: journalists and news editors, analysts and report writers, non-academic researchers. Reach them with the "writing with sources" angle and with dedicated pages, not on the homepage hero.

## Core promise

**Write faster, with a real source for every claim.**

Short form (taglines, footer, manifest): *The AI writing editor that cites real sources.*

Why this promise: general AI chat invents references. WriteOrbit's difference is that every suggestion, answer and citation points to a real paper you can open, and the editor checks your claims before you submit.

## Voice

Formal and friendly. Use plain, direct words.

- Say what the feature does in one sentence. Lead with the verb ("Find", "Cite", "Check", "Export").
- Talk to the reader as "you". Talk about the product as "WriteOrbit", not "we", except in the help and auth flows.
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- No hype words: *revolutionary, supercharge, seamless, unlock, effortless, game-changing, cutting-edge*.
- Don't promise what the product doesn't do (see "Not yet" below).

## Not yet (don't put these in product copy)

These are in the keyword list, but the product doesn't do them yet. Cover them in blog posts, or build the feature before you write a landing page for it.

- **AP style checker** (dates, numbers, state abbreviations, percentages, times). The blog covers AP style; the editor has no AP check.
- **Screenwriting / script writing software, script coverage, Final Draft alternative, YouTube script generator.** The editor has no screenplay format.
- **Website citation from a URL.** Custom citations accept a URL, but nothing reads the page for you. Don't say "cite any website automatically".
- **"AI fact checker" as a product name.** Claim check flags unsupported and overstated claims and shows where to cite. It doesn't check facts against the news. Say "check your claims", not "fact-check your article".
