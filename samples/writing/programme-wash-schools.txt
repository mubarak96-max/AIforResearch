PROGRAMME DESCRIPTION (test sample; the programme is invented for testing)

Clean Schools, Healthy Learners: a water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) programme in 12 rural primary schools in one county, January 2023 to December 2025. Budget: USD 480,000 from a private foundation.
Aim: reduce absenteeism caused by diarrhoeal illness and improve girls' attendance during menstruation.
Activities: drilled boreholes or rainwater tanks in 12 schools; built gender-separated latrines with handwashing stations; trained 2 teachers per school as hygiene champions; formed school health clubs; supplied menstrual pads and built a private changing room in each school; quarterly refresher training.
Target group: about 6,200 pupils aged 6 to 14, and their teachers.
Logframe outcome indicators: (1) pupil absenteeism rate; (2) girls' attendance during menstruation; (3) handwashing with soap after latrine use (observed); (4) functional water point in every school.

EVALUATION FINDINGS (end-line, November 2025)
Methods used: school attendance registers (all 12 schools, 2022 baseline and 2025); observation of handwashing at 12 schools on 2 days each; survey of 640 pupils in grades 4 to 7; 12 focus groups with girls; interviews with 12 head teachers and 3 county officials; review of budget and spending records.
Indicator results:
- Absenteeism: baseline 14.2% of school days, target 9%, achieved 10.1%.
- Girls' attendance during menstruation (self-report, days missed per month): baseline 2.6, target 1.0, achieved 0.9.
- Handwashing with soap after latrine use (observed): baseline 18%, target 60%, achieved 47%. Soap was missing at 5 of 12 schools on at least one observation day.
- Functional water point: target 12 of 12, achieved 10 of 12 (two boreholes broke down in 2025; repairs not funded).
Qualitative: girls said the changing rooms and pads were the main reason they stayed in school; some said pads ran out in term 3. Head teachers valued the health clubs but said refresher training stopped after mid-2024 when the trainer left. County officials had not yet budgeted for maintenance after the programme ends.
Spending: 96% of the budget spent; latrine construction cost 22% more than planned because of material prices.
Evaluation questions: Did the programme reduce absenteeism? Is it likely to last after funding ends? What should a second phase change?
