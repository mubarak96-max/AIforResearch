The results of this study shows that students who slept more in the night after the lecture remembered more of the lecture one week after. This is in line with what alot of previous research have found, for example Diekelmann and Born (2010) who said that sleep is very important for memory. So basically our findings agrees with the literature and it is therefore clear that sleep is good for students.

Another thing that was found is that the nap group did better then the rest group but the difference was not that big and it was not significant (p = 0.08). This might be because the naps where too short or because some students did not actually fall asleep during the nap, we did not measure this which is a limitation. Mednick et al. (2003) found that naps are as good as a night of sleep so maybe if the naps were longer it would of worked.

It is important to note that there are many limitations in this study. The sample was small (n = 64) and all from one university. The students also reported there sleep themselves using a sleep diary, which may not be accurate because people often think they sleep more than they really do. Also the lecture material was only from one course (introductory psychology) so we dont know if it works for other subjects like maths or engineering.

In terms of implications, universities should maybe think about not having exams early in the morning and also should teach students about sleep. Students should also be told to not stay up all night before an exam because it is bad for memory and attention as Lim and Dinges (2010) showed in there meta-analysis.

In conclusion this study adds to the research on sleep and memory by showing that it also works in a real university in Kenya with real lecture material and not just word lists in a lab. Future research should use actigraphy to measure sleep more objectively and should have a bigger sample from more universities.
