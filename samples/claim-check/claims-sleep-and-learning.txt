Sleep after learning improves the recall of word lists compared with the same time spent awake. A 60 to 90 minute nap can improve perceptual learning as much as a full night of sleep. During sleep, the brain clears metabolic waste such as beta-amyloid faster than during waking. Short-term sleep deprivation has no measurable effect on simple attention or vigilance. Every adult needs exactly eight hours of sleep a night to learn effectively. Drinking coffee before an exam completely reverses the memory effects of an all-night study session.
