# How to write a press release (AP style format, template and examples)

*Last updated: 6 November 2026*

A press release is a short news story an organization writes for journalists. Write it the way a reporter would: a headline, a dateline, a one-sentence lead with the news, supporting facts, one or two quotes, a boilerplate paragraph and a media contact, ending with `###`. Keep it to about one page, in AP style.

This guide is part of our [AP style guide](/blog/ap-style-guide). It gives you the 9 parts, a copyable template and three complete examples.

## The 9 parts of a press release

| # | Part | What it does | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Release line | Says when it can be published | FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE / EMBARGOED UNTIL 12:01 A.M. EST, NOV. 12, 2026 |
| 2 | Headline | States the news in under about 12 words | City library extends weekend hours at all five branches |
| 3 | Subheadline (optional) | Adds the second most important fact | Longer hours start Dec. 1 after a 9% budget increase |
| 4 | Dateline | Where the news comes from | SPOKANE, Wash. — |
| 5 | Lead | The news in one sentence: who, what, when, where | 25 to 35 words |
| 6 | Body | Facts, numbers, details in order of importance | 3 to 5 short paragraphs |
| 7 | Quotes | A person explains why it matters | One or two, attributed with "said" |
| 8 | Boilerplate | A standard "About [organization]" paragraph | 50 to 80 words |
| 9 | Media contact | Who a reporter calls | Name, title, email, phone |

End with `###` centered below the last line. It tells the reader there is no more text. (Some older releases use `-30-`.)

## Press release format, part by part

### Release line and embargo

Most releases say FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE. Use an embargo only when there is a real reason, such as a study publishing at a set time, and give the exact time and time zone: EMBARGOED UNTIL 11 A.M. EST, TUESDAY, NOV. 10, 2026. An embargo is a request, not a contract, so send embargoed material only to reporters you trust.

### Headline

Write it like a news headline: subject, active verb, key fact. Present tense, sentence case, no period, no exclamation point.

| Wrong | Right |
| --- | --- |
| ACME Corp. Is Thrilled To Announce An Exciting New Partnership! | Acme and Riverside Clinic team up to offer free hearing tests |
| Introducing the Future of Payroll | Ledgerly cuts payroll setup for small businesses to 15 minutes |

(Both are fictional examples.)

### Dateline

A dateline is the place name at the start of a story that tells readers where the news comes from. In AP style the city is in capital letters, followed by the state in AP abbreviation and a dash: `SPOKANE, Wash. —`. AP abbreviations are not postal codes (Wash., not WA), eight states are never abbreviated (Alaska, Hawaii, Idaho, Iowa, Maine, Ohio, Texas, Utah), and major cities on AP's standalone list, such as CHICAGO and BOSTON, take no state. [1] Wire-distributed releases usually add the date: `CHICAGO, Nov. 6, 2026 —`.

### Lead

The lead is the first sentence, and it carries the news. Use the inverted pyramid: most important fact first, everything after in order of importance. A reporter who reads only the lead should be able to write a brief.

| Wrong | Right |
| --- | --- |
| Riverside Clinic, a leading provider of innovative, patient-centered care since 1987, is proud to announce... | Riverside Clinic will offer free hearing tests to adults over 60 every Saturday in January, starting Jan. 3. |

### Quotes

A quote should say something a fact cannot: why the organization did this, what it means for people. "We are thrilled to announce this exciting partnership" says nothing, and reporters will not use it. Attribute with "said," name first: `"...," Okafor said.`

### Boilerplate and contact

The boilerplate is a short, factual paragraph about the organization: what it does, where, how big, a website. Keep it free of slogans. The media contact must be a person who answers the phone the day the release goes out.

## AP style rules that matter most in releases

These are the rules reporters fix most often when they adapt a release. Full lists are in our [AP style guide](/blog/ap-style-guide) and the [AP style numbers and dates cheat sheet](/blog/ap-style-numbers-dates-times).

| Rule | Wrong | Right |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Months abbreviated with a date (Jan., Feb., Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov., Dec.); March to July never | November 6th, 2026 | Nov. 6, 2026 |
| Times | 10:00 AM | 10 a.m. |
| Noon and midnight | 12 p.m. | noon |
| Titles: capitalize before a name, lowercase after | Jane Ruiz, Chief Executive Officer | CEO Jane Ruiz / Jane Ruiz, chief executive officer |
| Numbers: spell out one to nine, figures for 10 and up | 5 new branches | five new branches |
| Percent | 12 percent | 12% |
| Money | $5,000,000 | $5 million |
| States in body text spelled out | Spokane, WA | Spokane, Washington |
| Company names | ACME, INC. | Acme Inc. |
| No exclamation points, no trademark symbols needed | Ledgerly™ launches! | Ledgerly launches |

AP updates its stylebook every year; check the current edition for changes. [2]

## Press release template (copy and paste)

```markdown
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE

# [Headline: subject + active verb + key fact]
## [Optional subheadline: the second most important fact]

[CITY], [State AP abbreviation], [Mon. D, YYYY] — [Lead: who is doing what,
when and where, and why it matters to the reader. One sentence, 25 to 35 words.]

[Paragraph 2: the most important supporting fact. Numbers with their source.]

"[Quote: why this matters, in plain words]," said [Full Name], [title] of
[Organization].

[Paragraph 4: details: dates, prices, eligibility, location, how to take part.]

[Paragraph 5: second quote from a partner, customer or expert (optional).]

[Paragraph 6: what happens next or where to learn more, with a link.]

About [Organization]
[50 to 80 words: what it does, where, size, founding year, website.]

Media contact
[Name], [title]
[email] | [phone]

###
```

## Three AP style press release examples

All three examples below are fictional. The organizations, people, numbers and study are invented.

### Example 1: product launch

```
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE

Ledgerly launches payroll tool that sets up small businesses in 15 minutes

PORTLAND, Ore., Nov. 6, 2026 — Ledgerly, a Portland accounting software
company, on Thursday launched Ledgerly Payroll, a tool that lets businesses
with fewer than 50 employees set up payroll in about 15 minutes and run it
for $6 per employee a month.

The tool files federal and state payroll taxes automatically in all 50
states and sends year-end W-2 forms to employees by email.

"Most of our customers were doing payroll in a spreadsheet because the
alternatives took a week to set up," said Priya Nair, Ledgerly's chief
executive officer.

Ledgerly Payroll is available now at ledgerly.example.com. Existing Ledgerly
customers can add it from their account settings at no setup cost.

About Ledgerly
Ledgerly makes accounting software for small businesses. Founded in 2019,
it has 80 employees and serves about 12,000 businesses in the United States.

Media contact
Sam Ortiz, communications manager
press@ledgerly.example.com | 503-555-0142

###
```

### Example 2: event

```
FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE

Spokane library to host free tax help every Saturday in February

SPOKANE, Wash., Jan. 15, 2027 — The Spokane Free Library will offer free
tax filing help for households earning under $60,000 every Saturday in
February, from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m., at its Central branch, 900 W. Main Ave.

Trained volunteers will help residents file federal returns at no cost. No appointment is needed; residents should bring photo ID,
Social Security cards and all W-2 and 1099 forms.

"Paying $200 to file a simple return is a real cost for many families
here," said Ana Brooks, the library's director.

The library expects to help about 400 households over the four Saturdays,
based on demand last year.

About the Spokane Free Library
The Spokane Free Library runs five branches and serves about 230,000
residents.

Media contact
Leo Grant, outreach coordinator
lgrant@spokanefreelib.example.org | 509-555-0177

###
```

The event release puts the date, time, place and cost in the lead. Those are the four things a reporter or listings editor needs.

### Example 3: research findings

```
EMBARGOED UNTIL 11 A.M. EST, TUESDAY, NOV. 10, 2026

Teens slept about 30 minutes more after later school start, study finds

COLUMBUS, Ohio, Nov. 10, 2026 — High school students in three Ohio
districts slept about 30 minutes more on school nights after their schools
moved the start time from 7:30 a.m. to 8:30 a.m., according to a study
published Tuesday in the Journal of Adolescent Sleep.

Researchers at Midstate University tracked 2,000 students with wrist
sensors for one school year before the change and one year after. The
study is observational: it shows a change after the new start time, but
it cannot prove that the start time alone caused it.

"The extra sleep came almost entirely from later wake-up times; bedtimes
barely moved," said Dr. Helen Cho, an associate professor of pediatrics at
Midstate and the study's lead author. "We did not measure grades, so we
can't say whether the extra sleep helped students in class."

The study was funded by a grant from the Midstate Health Foundation. The
paper is available at https://doi.org/[DOI].

About Midstate University
Midstate University is a public research university in Columbus, Ohio,
with 31,000 students.

Media contact
Rachel Kim, research communications
rkim@midstate.example.edu | 614-555-0109

###
```

## Press releases about research and reports: citing the study correctly

Releases about studies are where accuracy matters most, because reporters often copy their numbers. Before sending, check the release against the paper, not against your memory of it.

**The research release checklist:**

- [ ] The headline and lead state the finding the paper actually reports, in plain language.
- [ ] The study type is named: randomized trial, observational study, survey, review, modeling.
- [ ] Correlation is not written as cause ("linked to," not "causes," for an observational study).
- [ ] Sample size, population and time frame are stated.
- [ ] The journal, publication date and a working DOI link are included. If it is a preprint, say so.
- [ ] The main limitation is in the body, ideally in the researcher's own words.
- [ ] Funding is disclosed.
- [ ] Every number matches the paper's tables, including units and rounding.
- [ ] A researcher who can explain the work is available on the embargo date.

This also covers releases about your own reports and white papers: link the report, name the method (survey of how many people, run by whom, when), and do not quote a number the report does not contain.

## What journalists delete

| What you wrote | What a reporter sees | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Leading," "innovative," "world-class," "cutting-edge" | Unverifiable adjectives | Replace with a fact: "the first in the state," "used by 12,000 businesses" |
| "We are thrilled to announce" | A quote with no information | A quote that explains why or what changes |
| Jargon and acronyms | Work they have to translate | Plain words; spell out acronyms on first use |
| The news in paragraph four | A release they stop reading | News in the lead |
| "Significant growth" | A missing number | "Revenue rose 22% to $4.1 million" |
| A study claim with no link | A claim they cannot check | DOI link and the study type |
| A contact who doesn't answer | A story that runs without you | A named person, reachable all day |

## FAQ

### How long should a press release be?

Most press releases run 300 to 500 words, about one page. That is enough for a headline, a one-sentence lead, the key facts, one or two quotes, a short boilerplate and a contact. If you need more, link to a fact sheet, report or study instead of adding paragraphs. Journalists read the top and decide in seconds.

### Do press releases use AP style?

Yes, most do. Releases are written for journalists, and most U.S. newsrooms use AP style, so a release that already follows AP rules on dates, times, titles, numbers and datelines is easier to use. Follow your organization's house style where it differs, but keep the news structure: dateline, lead, facts, quotes, boilerplate.

### How do you write a dateline for a press release in AP style?

Write the city in capital letters, then the state in AP abbreviation (not the postal code), then a dash, and start the lead: SPOKANE, Wash. — Major cities on AP's standalone list, such as CHICAGO, need no state. Many releases add the date after the city, for example CHICAGO, Nov. 6, 2026 —, which wire services expect.

### What are the parts of a press release?

A press release has 9 parts: a release line (FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE or an embargo time), a headline, an optional subheadline, a dateline, a lead with the news in one sentence, a body with supporting facts, one or two quotes, a boilerplate paragraph about the organization, and a media contact. It ends with ### centered below the text.

### How do you write a press release about a research study?

Lead with the finding in plain language, then give the study type, sample size, journal, publication date and a working DOI link. Quote the lead researcher on what the result means and does not mean, state the main limitation, disclose funding, and never claim cause when the study shows correlation. Check every number against the paper.
