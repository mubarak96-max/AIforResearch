# Dissertation helper: the tools and AI workflow that get you to submission

*Last updated: 12 November 2026*

A good dissertation helper is not one app but a small toolkit matched to the four places dissertations stall: the literature review, the method, the writing and the formatting. Use a reference manager from day one, scholarly search, a PDF reader that keeps notes, an editor your supervisor can comment in, and a weekly plan. AI can speed up search and checking, within your university's rules.

This guide is for master's and PhD students who want thesis writing assistance that keeps them in control of the work. It is part of our series on planning a study and thesis; the hub is [how to write a research question](/blog/how-to-write-a-research-question).

## Where do dissertations get stuck?

Most dissertations stall at one of four bottlenecks. Name yours, then pick tools for it rather than collecting dissertation apps for every stage.

| Bottleneck | What it looks like | What helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Literature review | 200 PDFs, no structure, the same papers read twice | A reference manager, citation chaining, a synthesis matrix, PDF notes you can search |
| Method | A design that does not answer the question; analysis chosen after data collection | Writing the method chapter early, a methods checklist, a stats test chooser |
| Writing | Blank pages, chapters that never feel finished | A word budget per chapter, short daily sessions, chapter-by-chapter supervisor feedback |
| Formatting | Broken citations, figure numbers and table of contents in the last week | Citations inserted from a reference manager, styles and captions set up early, Word or LaTeX templates |

## What software do PhD students use?

PhD research tools fall into five groups. You need one of each, not five of each.

| Stage | Tool type | Common choices | Good at |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Collect and cite | Reference manager | Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote | Zotero is free and open source, cites inside Word, LibreOffice and Google Docs, and includes 300 MB of free cloud storage [1][2] |
| Find | Scholarly search | Library databases, PubMed, Google Scholar, AI search tools | Library databases give subject indexing; AI search tools find papers phrased differently from your keywords |
| Read | PDF reader with notes, PDF chat | Zotero's reader, your PDF app, chat-with-PDF tools | Notes linked to the page; questions answered with page numbers |
| Write | Editor | Microsoft Word, LaTeX in Overleaf | Word: tracked changes and comments for supervisors [3]. Overleaf: browser LaTeX; its free plan allows 1 collaborator per project and paid plans add track changes [4] |
| Analyze | Statistics and qualitative software | R, SPSS, Stata, jamovi, JASP, NVivo | Use what your department teaches and your supervisor can check |

Prices and plans change; the competitor facts above were checked on the vendors' pages in November 2026.

### Word or LaTeX for dissertation writing software?

Choose what your field and supervisor use, and decide in the first month.

- **Word** suits most humanities and social science theses, and supervisors who comment with tracked changes. Use built-in heading styles, captions and a reference manager plug-in from the start.
- **LaTeX** suits equations, many cross-references and very long documents, common in math, physics and computer science. Many universities publish a LaTeX thesis template.
- **Switching late** costs days of formatting repairs. If you must convert, do it between chapters, not in the final month.

## How do you work with your supervisor?

Your supervisor is the most important dissertation helper you have. Make their feedback easy to give.

1. **Agree the format early:** Word with tracked changes, comments in a shared document, or PDF annotations. One format, used every time.
2. **Send chapters, not the whole thesis.** A 5,000-word chapter gets detailed comments; an 80,000-word draft gets general ones.
3. **Ask specific questions** in the email: "Is the sampling justification in 3.2 enough?" gets a better answer than "Any thoughts?"
4. **Keep a feedback log:** comment, your response, and the version where you fixed it. You will need it in the defense.
5. **Set the next meeting before you leave this one,** with what you will send and when.

## How do you stay within the rules on AI?

Read your university's AI policy and your program handbook before you use any AI tool, and ask your supervisor in writing. Rules differ between universities, departments and even modules, and they change. In the UK, the 24 Russell Group universities agreed principles that support AI literacy while upholding academic rigor and integrity, and each university sets its own rules [5].

A pattern many policies share (check yours):

| Usually allowed, often with disclosure | Often restricted | Usually not allowed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Searching for papers, summarizing a paper you then read | Paraphrasing or rewriting your text with AI | Submitting AI-generated text, analysis or results as your own |
| Grammar and spelling checks | AI help with analysis code | Inventing or AI-generating data or references |
| Brainstorming a structure or questions | Translation of your own drafts | Uploading confidential or participant data to tools without approval |

Keep a simple log: date, tool, what you asked, and what you used. If your university asks for an AI statement, the log writes it for you.

## A 12-week dissertation writing plan

This plan is a worked example for a 15,000-word master's dissertation whose data are already collected. The chapter budget is 1,500 (introduction), 4,000 (literature review), 2,500 (method), 3,500 (results) and 3,500 (discussion and conclusion), which adds up to 15,000. Adjust weeks and words to your own limit; see [how to write a thesis outline](/blog/how-to-write-a-thesis-outline) for budgets by field.

| Week | Main task | Words drafted | Running total | Send to supervisor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Confirm outline, word budget and AI rules; set up reference manager and template | 0 | 0 | Outline |
| 2 | Draft method chapter | 2,500 | 2,500 | |
| 3 | Run and write up analysis, part 1 | 1,750 | 4,250 | Method |
| 4 | Results, part 2; tables and figures | 1,750 | 6,000 | |
| 5 | Literature review, themes 1 and 2 | 2,000 | 8,000 | Results |
| 6 | Literature review, themes 3 and 4 | 2,000 | 10,000 | |
| 7 | Discussion | 2,500 | 12,500 | Literature review |
| 8 | Conclusion and introduction | 2,500 | 15,000 | |
| 9 | Revise method and results from feedback | 0 | 15,000 | Discussion, conclusion, introduction |
| 10 | Revise literature review and discussion | 0 | 15,000 | |
| 11 | Check citations, figures, tables, abstract; full read-through | 0 | 15,000 | Full draft |
| 12 | Final corrections, formatting, declarations, submit | 0 | 15,000 | |

Why this order: the method is the easiest chapter to write because you know what you did. The introduction is written near the end, when you know what the dissertation argues. Feedback arrives while you draft the next chapter, so you are never waiting.

## How do you finish a dissertation faster?

Most time is lost to rework and waiting, not slow typing. These decision rules save the most.

- **Write every working day,** in one or two focused sessions, rather than in rare long blocks.
- **Cite as you write** from your reference manager. Never leave "(ref)" placeholders for later.
- **Cut scope, not quality.** If a chapter runs 30% over budget, remove a sub-question rather than compressing everything.
- **Stop reading when new papers stop changing your argument.** Note new papers for the discussion and move on.
- **Use templates** for tables, figure captions and the front matter in week 1.

## FAQ

### What software do PhD students use?

Most PhD students combine five kinds of software: a reference manager such as Zotero or Mendeley, a scholarly search tool, a PDF reader for notes, a writing tool (Word, or LaTeX in Overleaf for math-heavy fields), and analysis software such as R, SPSS, jamovi, JASP or NVivo. Add cloud backup. The best set is the one your supervisor can also open and comment on.

### How can I finish my dissertation faster?

Fix the structure and word budget first, then write every working day in short sessions, starting with the methods chapter because it is the easiest to describe. Send chapters to your supervisor with specific questions, not whole drafts at the end. Keep citations in a reference manager from day one so formatting takes hours, not weeks. Cut scope before you cut quality.

### Is it allowed to use AI for a dissertation?

It depends on your university, department and supervisor, and the rules change often. Many universities allow AI for searching, brainstorming and language checks, require you to disclose it, and ban submitting AI-generated text as your own work. Read your university's current AI policy and your program handbook, ask your supervisor in writing, and keep a log of how you used each tool.

### Should I write my dissertation in Word or LaTeX?

Use what your field and supervisor use. Word is easiest for tracked changes and comments with supervisors who do not use LaTeX, and most humanities and social science theses are written in it. LaTeX handles equations, cross-references and long documents more reliably, which is why it is common in math, physics and computer science. Converting late is painful, so decide early.

### What is a dissertation helper?

A dissertation helper is any tool or service that helps you plan, research, write or format a dissertation. Software helpers include reference managers, scholarly search, PDF readers, writing editors and planners. Human helpers include your supervisor, writing centers and peers. Be careful with services that offer to write the dissertation for you: submitting that work as your own breaks academic integrity rules.
