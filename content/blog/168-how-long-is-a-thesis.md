# How long is a thesis or dissertation? Word counts by degree, field and chapter

*Last updated: 14 November 2026*

A thesis or dissertation is usually 8,000 to 15,000 words for an undergraduate degree, 10,000 to 50,000 words for a master's, and 70,000 to 100,000 words for a PhD, with science and engineering theses often shorter than those in the humanities. These are typical ranges, not rules. The only length that counts is the one in your own university's regulations, which also say what is included in the count.

This guide gives the typical ranges, explains why they vary, shows how a word count splits across chapters, and walks you through finding and reading your own program's limit. If you came here for the length of a thesis *statement* (one sentence in an essay), see [what is a thesis statement](/blog/what-is-a-thesis-statement) instead.

## How long is a thesis or dissertation?

**Length depends on the degree, the field and the university, and most programs set a maximum word count rather than a target.** The table gives ranges commonly stated in university handbooks. Use it to sense-check your plan, then replace it with your program's figures.

<!-- VERIFY: these ranges are typical figures drawn from many university handbooks, not from one named source. Before publishing, spot-check against three to five current university regulations (for example a UK, an Australian and a US graduate school) and adjust if they differ. -->

| Degree | Typical length (words) | Typical length (pages, double spaced) | Usually called |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Undergraduate (honors, final-year project) | 8,000 to 15,000 | 30 to 60 | Dissertation (UK), honors thesis (US, Australia) |
| Taught master's (MA, MSc, MBA) | 10,000 to 20,000 | 40 to 80 | Dissertation (UK), thesis or capstone (US) |
| Research master's (MPhil, MRes, MS by research) | 20,000 to 60,000 | 80 to 200 | Thesis |
| PhD, humanities and social sciences | 80,000 to 100,000 | 200 to 350 | Thesis (UK, Australia), dissertation (US) |
| PhD, sciences and engineering | 40,000 to 80,000 | 120 to 250 | Thesis or dissertation |
| Professional doctorate (EdD, DBA, DNP) | 40,000 to 80,000, or a project plus a shorter report | Varies | Thesis, dissertation or portfolio |

Two naming notes. In the United Kingdom and Australia, a PhD produces a *thesis* and a master's produces a *dissertation*. In the United States it is usually the other way round [2]. The words matter less than the regulations they point to.

### Why does thesis length vary so much?

**Fields carry their argument in different forms, and universities set limits for their own examination systems.** Four things drive most of the difference:

| Factor | Effect on length | Example (illustrative) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How the field argues | Text-based fields need more words to make the same point | A history PhD builds its case from archival quotation and interpretation; a chemistry PhD from spectra, tables and short methods sections |
| Data in figures and tables | Figures carry content but add few words | A materials science thesis with 60 figures may be 50,000 words and 250 pages |
| Thesis format | A thesis by publication can be shorter in new text | Three published papers plus an introduction and discussion chapter |
| National tradition | Some systems examine by a long written thesis, others put more weight on a defense or coursework | US PhDs include years of coursework; many UK and Australian PhDs are research only |

Doctoral frameworks also recognize several formats, including the thesis by publication and practice-based theses, which affects how much new text is expected [3].

US dissertations are less often capped by word count. Many US graduate schools specify format (margins, fonts, page numbering) and leave length to the committee, so students and supervisors talk in pages. In the UK, Australia, Ireland and many other systems, a maximum word count in the regulations is the norm. <!-- VERIFY: confirm that most US graduate school dissertation guidelines set format rather than a maximum length, by checking three or four graduate school formatting guides. -->

## How do I find my program's thesis word limit?

**Look in your program's regulations first, then the graduate school's thesis guidelines, then ask your supervisor; and get anything unclear confirmed in writing.** The limit is almost never in a general web search result about "average thesis length".

1. **Find the right document.** Search your university site for "thesis regulations", "code of practice for research degrees", "dissertation handbook" or "examination regulations" plus your degree. For taught degrees the limit is usually in the module or program handbook.
2. **Check the version.** Regulations change. Use the version for the year you started, or the current one if your university applies it to everyone. The document usually says which.
3. **Read what counts.** Look for a sentence that lists inclusions and exclusions. Note each item in a table like the one below.
4. **Look for a minimum, a tolerance and a penalty.** Some programs allow 10 percent over; some reject a thesis over the limit without examination; some have a minimum.
5. **Check for field variation.** Large universities often let each faculty or department set its own limit. A university-wide figure may be overridden by your faculty.
6. **Ask your supervisor what is normal.** The maximum is not the target. Your supervisor knows what recent theses in your group looked like. Your library's repository holds them, so you can open three recent theses from your department and check their length yourself.
7. **Confirm edge cases in writing.** If the wording is unclear about footnotes or appendices, email the graduate school or examinations office and keep the reply.

### What usually counts toward the word count?

**The main text almost always counts; the reference list almost never does; footnotes, tables and appendices vary.** Fill this in for your own program:

| Part of the thesis | Commonly counted? | Your program |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main text of chapters | Yes | |
| In-text citations | Usually yes | |
| Footnotes or endnotes | Varies: often yes in the humanities | |
| Tables and figure captions | Varies | |
| Abstract | Usually no, and often has its own limit (often 300 words or fewer) | |
| Title page, contents, acknowledgments | No | |
| Reference list or bibliography | Usually no | |
| Appendices | Usually no, but examiners need not read them | |
| Published papers included in a thesis by publication | Varies; check the specific rules | |

Do not move core content into appendices to fit the limit. Examiners are not obliged to read appendices, and anything your argument depends on belongs in the chapters.

## How long should each thesis chapter be?

**A common split for an empirical thesis gives about a tenth to the introduction, a quarter to the literature review, a sixth to methods, a quarter to results, and a fifth to discussion and conclusion.** These proportions are a planning tool, not a rule. Adjust them to your design and your supervisor's advice.

| Chapter | Share (typical) | In a 12,000-word master's | In an 80,000-word PhD |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | 10% | 1,200 | 8,000 |
| Literature review | 20 to 25% | 2,500 to 3,000 | 16,000 to 20,000 |
| Methodology | 15% | 1,800 | 12,000 |
| Results or findings | 25% | 3,000 | 20,000 |
| Discussion | 15 to 20% | 1,800 to 2,400 | 12,000 to 16,000 |
| Conclusion | 5 to 10% | 600 to 1,200 | 4,000 to 8,000 |

Qualitative theses often merge findings and discussion into thematic chapters. Humanities theses often have no separate methods chapter and are organized as four to six argument chapters between an introduction and a conclusion. A thesis by publication has an introduction, the papers as chapters, and a general discussion. For a longer treatment of planning and drafting a doctoral thesis chapter by chapter, see Dunleavy [1]. Our [thesis outline guide](/blog/how-to-write-a-thesis-outline) has templates for each structure, and the [methodology chapter guide](/blog/how-to-write-a-methodology-chapter) covers what the methods chapter must contain.

### Worked example: planning a 15,000-word master's dissertation (illustrative)

An education student has a 15,000-word limit, references and appendices excluded, a 10 percent tolerance, and a mixed-methods design.

- Introduction: 1,500 words
- Literature review: 3,500 words
- Methodology: 2,500 words
- Findings (survey and interviews): 4,000 words
- Discussion: 2,500 words
- Conclusion: 1,000 words
- **Total: 15,000 words.** The 10 percent tolerance is kept as a buffer for revisions, not planned into the draft.

At this stage the student also notes that the abstract (300-word limit) is outside the count, and that tables of survey results will count, because the handbook says so.

## How many pages is a thesis?

**Divide your word count by about 250 for double-spaced pages or about 500 for single-spaced pages, then add room for figures, tables and front matter.** This is a rough rule of thumb for 12-point text on A4 or US Letter paper.

| Words | Double spaced (about 250 per page) | Single spaced (about 500 per page) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10,000 | 40 | 20 |
| 20,000 | 80 | 40 |
| 50,000 | 200 | 100 |
| 80,000 | 320 | 160 |
| 100,000 | 400 | 200 |

Real theses vary from these figures. Headings, block quotations, equations, tables and figures change the count per page, and many universities ask for 1.5 line spacing. If your program states a page limit, format to the required template from the start, because changing spacing late will move every page reference.

## How do you write to a word limit?

**Plan by chapter, draft past the limit if you need to, then cut by removing whole points rather than trimming sentences.** Most students end up over the limit, not under it.

- **Track words per chapter, not just the total.** A literature review that grows to 40 percent of the thesis is a common reason for a weak results chapter.
- **Cut summaries of every paper.** A review should synthesize and compare, not describe each study. A [literature review matrix](/tools/literature-review-matrix-builder) helps you group studies by theme so you write about patterns.
- **Move detail, not content.** Full survey instruments, codebooks and extra robustness tables can go in appendices. The finding itself stays in the chapter.
- **Remove repetition between chapters.** Introductions often restate the literature review; discussions often restate results.
- **Check figure and table text.** If they count, a long caption is expensive.

Leave the final edit until the chapters are stable, and set a deadline for it. The [thesis defence timeline planner](/tools/thesis-defence-timeline-planner) works backward from your defense or submission date so you can see when cutting and formatting must be done.

## Common mistakes with thesis length

- **Using a figure from the internet.** "The average dissertation is X pages" says nothing about your program's limit.
- **Ignoring what counts.** Students lose marks, or have theses returned, for counting footnotes they should have included.
- **Writing to the maximum.** A focused 70,000-word PhD thesis is better than a padded 100,000-word one.
- **Pushing core content into appendices.** Examiners may not read it.
- **Formatting at the end.** Switching to the required template late changes page counts and can break a page limit.

## FAQ

### How long is a PhD thesis?

A PhD thesis is commonly 70,000 to 100,000 words in the humanities and social sciences, and often shorter in the sciences and engineering, where figures and tables carry much of the content. In page terms that is roughly 150 to 350 pages. Your university's regulations set the actual maximum, usually with a stated list of what counts toward it, so check them before you plan chapters.

### How long is a master's thesis?

A taught master's dissertation is often 10,000 to 20,000 words, and a research master's thesis can run 20,000 to 50,000 words or more. In the United States, master's theses are more often described in pages, commonly 40 to 100. The range is wide because programs differ in how much original research they expect, so your handbook is the only figure that counts.

### How long is an undergraduate dissertation?

An undergraduate dissertation or honors thesis is usually 8,000 to 15,000 words, or about 30 to 60 double-spaced pages. Shorter capstone projects can be 5,000 words. Your department sets the limit and usually says whether references, appendices and tables count. Many also allow a margin, such as 10 percent over, before marks are deducted.

### Do references count toward the thesis word count?

Usually not. Most universities exclude the reference list or bibliography, and many also exclude appendices, the abstract, acknowledgments and front matter. Rules on footnotes, tables and figure captions vary more: some count them, some do not. Read the exact wording in your regulations, and when it is unclear, ask your graduate school or examinations office in writing.

### How many pages is 80,000 words?

About 320 pages double spaced in a 12-point font, or about 160 single spaced, using the rough rule of 250 words per double-spaced page and 500 per single-spaced page. A real thesis will have fewer pages of text than this suggests, because headings, tables, figures and block quotes take space. Treat any page estimate as a guide and track words.

### Is it bad to go under the word limit?

Not in itself. Examiners judge whether you answered your question, not whether you used every word. A thesis well under the typical range for your field can raise doubts about depth, though, so check that each chapter does its job. Some programs also set a minimum length, so read both limits in your regulations.
