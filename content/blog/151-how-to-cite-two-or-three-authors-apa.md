# How to cite two or three authors in APA 7

*Last updated: 14 November 2026*

In APA 7, cite two authors by naming both every time, joined by "&" in parentheses (Braun & Clarke, 2006) and by "and" in a sentence: Braun and Clarke (2006). Cite three or more authors with the first author's surname and "et al." from the first citation: (Simmons et al., 2011). The reference list names every author up to 20 [1][2].

This guide covers in-text citations by number of authors, plus the matching reference entries. It belongs to our citation styles series; the pillar, [references in APA](/blog/apa-reference-list), covers the reference list itself.

## APA author rules at a glance

**The number of authors decides the in-text form; the reference list names everyone up to 20.** Every example uses a real paper you can look up.

| Authors | Parenthetical | Narrative | Reference list starts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | (Cohen, 1992) | Cohen (1992) | Cohen, J. (1992). |
| 2 | (Braun & Clarke, 2006) | Braun and Clarke (2006) | Braun, V., & Clarke, V. (2006). |
| 3 | (Simmons et al., 2011) | Simmons et al. (2011) | Simmons, J. P., Nelson, L. D., & Simonsohn, U. (2011). |
| 5 | (Dunlosky et al., 2013) | Dunlosky et al. (2013) | Dunlosky, J., Rawson, K. A., Marsh, E. J., Nathan, M. J., & Willingham, D. T. (2013). |
| 21 or more | (Wilkinson et al., 2016) | Wilkinson et al. (2016) | First 19 authors, . . . , last author |
| Group | (Open Science Collaboration, 2015) | Open Science Collaboration (2015) | Open Science Collaboration. (2015). |

## What is the difference between narrative and parenthetical citations?

**A parenthetical citation puts the author and year in parentheses; a narrative citation puts the author in your sentence and the year in parentheses after the name** [1][2].

- Parenthetical: Retrieval practice improved recall a week later (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006).
- Narrative: Roediger and Karpicke (2006) found that retrieval practice improved recall a week later.

Both are correct. Use narrative citations when the authors matter to your point (you are comparing researchers, or one study is central), and parenthetical citations when the finding matters more than who found it. Mixing both keeps a literature review readable.

A rare third form puts both author and year in the sentence: "In 2006, Roediger and Karpicke showed..." No parentheses are needed then.

## How do you cite one author in APA?

**Write the surname and the year: (Cohen, 1992) or Cohen (1992).** Never add initials or first names unless two first authors share a surname (see below).

> Cohen, J. (1992). A power primer. *Psychological Bulletin*, *112*(1), 155–159. https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.112.1.155

Add a page number for a direct quote: (Cohen, 1992, p. 156). Use "pp." for a range: (Cohen, 1992, pp. 156–157). Page numbers are optional, but encouraged, for paraphrases [1].

Suffixes such as Jr. or III appear in the reference list only. Henry L. Roediger III is "Roediger, H. L., III" in the list and plain "Roediger" in the text:

> Roediger, H. L., III, & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention. *Psychological Science*, *17*(3), 249–255. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x

## How do you cite two authors in APA?

**Name both authors in every citation, never "et al.", with "&" inside parentheses and "and" in running text** [1][2].

> Braun, V., & Clarke, V. (2006). Using thematic analysis in psychology. *Qualitative Research in Psychology*, *3*(2), 77–101. https://doi.org/10.1191/1478088706qp063oa

| Incorrect | Correct | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (Braun and Clarke, 2006) | (Braun & Clarke, 2006) | Ampersand inside parentheses |
| Braun & Clarke (2006) argue | Braun and Clarke (2006) argue | "and" in a sentence |
| (Braun et al., 2006) | (Braun & Clarke, 2006) | Two authors are never shortened |
| (Clarke & Braun, 2006) | (Braun & Clarke, 2006) | Keep the author order of the paper |
| Braun, V. & Clarke, V. (2006). | Braun, V., & Clarke, V. (2006). | Reference list has a comma before & |

The comma before the ampersand in the reference list surprises people. APA uses it even with two authors, because the comma separates each author's initials from the next name.

Author order is not alphabetical. Kahneman and Tversky (1979) and Tversky and Kahneman (1974) are two different papers by the same pair, and the citations must keep the order printed on each:

- (Kahneman & Tversky, 1979) for prospect theory
- (Tversky & Kahneman, 1974) for heuristics and biases

## How do you cite three or more authors in APA?

**Use the first author's surname and "et al." in every citation, including the first one** [1][2].

> Simmons, J. P., Nelson, L. D., & Simonsohn, U. (2011). False-positive psychology: Undisclosed flexibility in data collection and analysis allows presenting anything as significant. *Psychological Science*, *22*(11), 1359–1366. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611417632

- Parenthetical: (Simmons et al., 2011)
- Narrative: Simmons et al. (2011) showed that undisclosed choices in analysis can make false results look significant.
- With a page: (Simmons et al., 2011, p. 1359)

This is one of the biggest changes from APA 6, which wrote out three to five authors in full the first time, then used et al. If your instructor still wants "Simmons, Nelson, and Simonsohn (2011)" at first mention, they are using APA 6. Ask which edition they mark against.

Punctuation details:

- "et" has no period; "al." always has one.
- et al. is not italic in APA.
- In parentheses, a comma goes between et al. and the year: (Simmons et al., 2011).
- At the end of a sentence, the period of "al." does not double up: "...as Simmons et al. found." not "found.."
- "et al." is plural, so the verb is too: "Simmons et al. show", not "shows".

The reference list still names all three. The same goes for five authors: Dunlosky et al. (2013) in the text, and all five names in the list.

## How do you cite 21 or more authors in APA?

**In the text, treat it like any work with three or more authors: (Wilkinson et al., 2016). In the reference list, give the first 19 authors, an ellipsis, then the last author** [1].

> Wilkinson, M. D., Dumontier, M., Aalbersberg, I. J., Appleton, G., Axton, M., Baak, A., Blomberg, N., Boiten, J.-W., da Silva Santos, L. B., Bourne, P. E., Bouwman, J., Brookes, A. J., Clark, T., Crosas, M., Dillo, I., Dumon, O., Edmunds, S., Evelo, C. T., Finkers, R., . . . Mons, B. (2016). The FAIR Guiding Principles for scientific data management and stewardship. *Scientific Data*, *3*, Article 160018. https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2016.18

The paper has 53 authors. There is no ampersand before "Mons, B." because the ellipsis replaces it. A paper with exactly 20 authors lists all 20, with an ampersand before the twentieth.

## How do you cite a group author in APA?

**Use the full name of the organization as the author; introduce an abbreviation in square brackets only if it is well known or you will use it several times** [1][3].

> Open Science Collaboration. (2015). Estimating the reproducibility of psychological science. *Science*, *349*(6251), Article aac4716. https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aac4716

- Parenthetical: (Open Science Collaboration, 2015)
- Narrative: The Open Science Collaboration (2015) repeated 100 published studies.

With an abbreviation:

| Citation | Parenthetical | Narrative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | (American Psychological Association [APA], 2020) | American Psychological Association (APA, 2020) |
| Later | (APA, 2020) | APA (2020) |

The reference list uses the full name only:

> American Psychological Association. (2020). *Publication manual of the American Psychological Association* (7th ed.). https://doi.org/10.1037/0000165-000

A group author is not the same as a list of people who belong to a consortium. If the paper's byline is a group name, use the group. Large genome and physics papers often print a consortium name and then individual names; cite the byline exactly as the published article shows it.

## How do you avoid ambiguous et al. citations?

**If two works would shorten to the same "Name et al., Year", write out as many authors as you need to tell them apart, then et al.** [1].

The cases below use invented names, because the rule matters only when two real papers collide in your own list. *Example (illustrative).*

| Your two references | Both shorten to | Write instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kapoor, Bloom, Montez, Warner, & Hill (2017) and Kapoor, Bloom, Zucker, Tang, & Mandel (2017) | Kapoor et al. (2017) | (Kapoor, Bloom, Montez, et al., 2017) and (Kapoor, Bloom, Zucker, et al., 2017) |
| Lee, Park, & Chen (2020) and Lee, Park, & Gomez (2020) | Lee et al. (2020) | (Lee, Park, & Chen, 2020) and (Lee, Park, & Gomez, 2020): only the last author differs, so write all names |
| Two works by Lee, Park, & Chen (2020), same author list | Lee et al. (2020) | (Lee et al., 2020a) and (Lee et al., 2020b), with a and b in the reference list too |

Note the comma before et al. once two or more names come before it. That is the only time APA puts a comma there.

A related case: two different first authors with the same surname, such as two researchers called Taylor. Add initials to each citation, even when the years differ: (J. M. Taylor & Neimeyer, 2015) and (T. Taylor, 2014). *Example (illustrative).*

## How do you cite several works at once?

**Put them in one pair of parentheses, in the order they appear in the reference list, separated by semicolons** [1].

- Different authors: (Braun & Clarke, 2006; Cohen, 1992; Simmons et al., 2011)
- Same authors, different years: (Tversky & Kahneman, 1974, 1981), oldest first
- Same authors, same year: (Lee et al., 2020a, 2020b)

In narrative form, cite each one in the sentence: "Cohen (1992) and Simmons et al. (2011) both..."

## Can you leave out the year after the first narrative citation?

**Yes, within one paragraph you may drop the year from later narrative citations of the same work, but parenthetical citations always keep it** [1].

> Roediger and Karpicke (2006) tested students five minutes, two days or one week after study. Roediger and Karpicke found that the benefit of testing appeared at the longer delays, and a later meta-analysis also found a reliable testing effect (Rowland, 2014).

The second mention of Roediger and Karpicke drops the year. A parenthetical citation, such as (Rowland, 2014), always keeps it. Start each new paragraph with the year again.

## Common mistakes

- **"et al." for two authors.** Never. Both names, every time.
- **"and" inside parentheses, "&" in a sentence.** Swap them.
- **Writing all three authors at first citation.** That was APA 6.
- **"et. al." or "et al" without a period.** Only "al." takes a period.
- **"et al." in the reference list.** List up to 20 authors.
- **Initials in the text.** Only for two first authors with the same surname.
- **Year mismatches.** (Cohen, 1988) in the text and Cohen (1992) in the list. The [citation coverage checker](/tools/citation-coverage-checker) catches these.

## FAQ

### How do you cite two authors in APA 7?

Name both authors every time you cite the work. In a parenthetical citation, join them with an ampersand: (Braun & Clarke, 2006). In a narrative citation, where the names are part of your sentence, write "and": Braun and Clarke (2006) describe six phases. In the reference list, write both surnames with initials and an ampersand: Braun, V., & Clarke, V. (2006).

### How do you cite three authors in APA 7?

From the first citation, write the first author's surname followed by et al. and the year: (Simmons et al., 2011) or Simmons et al. (2011). APA 7 changed this rule: APA 6 named three to five authors in full the first time. In the reference list, list all three authors, with an ampersand before the last: Simmons, J. P., Nelson, L. D., & Simonsohn, U. (2011).

### When do you use & and when do you use 'and' in APA?

Use the ampersand (&) inside parentheses, in tables and figures, and in the reference list. Use the word "and" in running text, when the authors' names are part of your sentence. So write (Kahneman & Tversky, 1979) but Kahneman and Tversky (1979) proposed. Never mix the two in one citation, and never use & in a sentence outside parentheses.

### Is there a comma before et al. in APA?

Not when only one name comes before it: (Simmons et al., 2011). Use a comma before et al. only when you have to write two or more names to tell two works apart, as in (Kapoor, Bloom, Montez, et al., 2017). There is always a comma between et al. and the year in a parenthetical citation, and et al. always ends with a period.

### How do you cite a group author like an organization in APA?

Write the organization's full name as the author: (Open Science Collaboration, 2015). If the name has a well-known abbreviation that you will use at least a few times, give it in square brackets the first time, (American Psychological Association [APA], 2020), then use (APA, 2020) after that. The reference list always uses the full name, never the abbreviation.

### How do you cite a paper with 21 or more authors in APA?

In the text, treat it like any work with three or more authors: first author and et al., such as (Wilkinson et al., 2016). In the reference list, list the first 19 authors, then an ellipsis, then the final author, with no ampersand before the last name. Works with up to 20 authors list every author.
