# Site initiation visit checklist: what to confirm before the first participant

*Last updated: 13 November 2026*

A site initiation visit checklist lists everything a clinical trial site must have in place before it can enroll: approvals and agreements, a complete investigator site file, a signed delegation log, protocol and GCP training, investigational product and pharmacy set-up, laboratory and equipment, data capture access, safety reporting and the consent process. Work through it with the sponsor's monitor at the site initiation visit (SIV), assign every open point an owner and a date, and activate the site only when they are closed.

This guide is for study coordinators, research nurses, investigators and new monitors (CRAs) in hospitals, universities and contract research organizations. It is the pillar of our series on clinical studies and reporting. It is general guidance, not regulatory advice: your sponsor's SOPs, the protocol, your institution's rules and ICH GCP govern what you must do. <!-- VERIFY: ICH E6(R3) was adopted by ICH in January 2025 and has since come into effect in the EU and been issued by FDA as guidance; confirm the current status and dates in your region before citing it -->

## What a site initiation visit is (and what it is not)

**A site initiation visit is the set-up and training visit that happens after a site has been selected and approved, and before it enrolls its first participant.** The sponsor, usually through its monitor, confirms that the site is ready and trains the site team on the protocol and the sponsor's procedures.

It is one of four visits in the life of a trial site, and they are easy to confuse:

| Visit | When | Its question | Main output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pre-study or site qualification visit** (also site selection or feasibility visit) | Before the site is chosen | Can this site run the study: patients, staff, facilities, experience? | A selection decision |
| **Site initiation visit (SIV)** | After approvals and contracts, before enrollment | Is the site ready to start, and does the team know the protocol? | An SIV report, an action list, then site activation |
| **Routine monitoring visit (interim)** | During enrollment and follow-up | Is the study being run to protocol and GCP, and are the data right? | A monitoring report, source data verification, findings |
| **Close-out visit (COV)** | After the last participant's last visit | Is everything finished, reconciled and archived? | Close-out report, IP reconciliation, archiving |

An SIV is not the time to discover that the ethics approval is missing or the contract is unsigned. Those belong before the visit is booked. The SIV confirms and trains; it should not be the first time anyone checks.

## Who attends

The rule of thumb: anyone whose name will be on the delegation log should attend the parts that concern their tasks.

| Role | Why they are there |
| --- | --- |
| Principal investigator (PI) | Responsible for the trial at the site; signs the delegation log; must understand eligibility, safety reporting and their own duties |
| Sub-investigators | Eligibility decisions, medical assessments, adverse event assessment |
| Study coordinator or research nurse | Day-to-day running: visits, consent, data entry, logs |
| Pharmacist or IP manager | Receipt, storage, dispensing and accountability of investigational product |
| Laboratory staff | Sample collection, processing, storage and shipping |
| Other delegated staff | Imaging, ECG, specific assessments named in the protocol |
| Sponsor's monitor (CRA) | Runs the visit, checks documents, trains on sponsor procedures, writes the report |
| Sponsor medical monitor or project manager (sometimes) | Protocol science, safety questions, complex procedures |

Send the agenda and the slides ahead, and ask staff who cannot attend to complete training before they take on any task. Everyone signs the training log.

## The site initiation visit checklist

Use this as a working list. Tick only what you have seen, not what someone says is done. Sponsors have their own forms; map this onto theirs rather than replacing them.

### 1. Approvals and agreements

- [ ] Ethics committee or IRB approval of the current protocol version, consent form and participant materials (with version numbers and dates matching the documents in use)
- [ ] Regulatory authority approval or notification, as required in your country
- [ ] Local institutional approvals (research governance, radiation, biosafety, data protection) where required
- [ ] Signed clinical trial agreement and budget
- [ ] Insurance or indemnity documents, where required

### 2. Investigator site file (regulatory binder)

The investigator site file (ISF), called the regulatory binder in many US sites, holds the site's essential documents. Check it against the sponsor's index.

- [ ] Signed protocol and any amendments, with the PI's signature page
- [ ] Investigator's brochure or product information, current version
- [ ] Approved consent form and participant materials, every version
- [ ] Approval letters and correspondence with the ethics committee and regulator
- [ ] CVs (signed and dated), GCP training certificates and professional licenses for everyone on the delegation log
- [ ] Financial disclosure forms, where required <!-- VERIFY: financial disclosure is a US FDA requirement (21 CFR Part 54); other regions differ -->
- [ ] Form FDA 1572 for US IND studies <!-- VERIFY: applies to studies under a US IND -->
- [ ] Laboratory certification or accreditation and normal ranges
- [ ] Logs ready: screening, enrollment, delegation, training, monitoring visits, temperature, sample, IP accountability
- [ ] A place for the SIV report and future monitoring correspondence

### 3. Delegation log

- [ ] Every person who does trial tasks is on it, with the tasks delegated to them
- [ ] Each task matches that person's training, qualifications and license (only a qualified person assesses eligibility or adverse events)
- [ ] Signed and dated by the PI, with start dates; no one acts before their entry is signed
- [ ] Signature and initials specimens, so entries in source and CRFs can be traced

### 4. Training

- [ ] Protocol training: objectives, design, eligibility, visit schedule, procedures, prohibited medication, endpoints and how they are measured
- [ ] Study-specific procedures and manuals (lab manual, pharmacy manual, imaging manual)
- [ ] GCP training current for all staff, per the sponsor's requirement
- [ ] Training log signed by every attendee, with the date and the topics
- [ ] A plan to train staff who join later

### 5. Informed consent process

- [ ] The current approved version is the one on file and in use; superseded versions are marked and removed from use
- [ ] Who may take consent (and it matches the delegation log)
- [ ] How consent is documented in the source notes (date, time, version, who took it, that questions were answered and a copy given)
- [ ] Translated versions, interpreters and impartial witnesses, where needed
- [ ] Assent and parental permission for children, and what happens when a child reaches adulthood
- [ ] The re-consent process when the consent form is amended

### 6. Investigational product (IP) and pharmacy

- [ ] Shipment received (or the date it will be), and the receipt recorded
- [ ] Secure storage with restricted access, at the labeled temperature
- [ ] Temperature monitoring and the excursion procedure (who to call, quarantine)
- [ ] Accountability log: dispensed, returned, destroyed; reconcilable to the unit
- [ ] Dispensing and preparation instructions; pharmacy staff trained
- [ ] Randomization system access (IRT/IWRS) and the emergency unblinding procedure

### 7. Laboratory and samples

- [ ] Lab kits in date and in enough quantity
- [ ] Collection, processing, storage and shipping procedures understood
- [ ] Centrifuge, freezers and fridges available, with temperature logs
- [ ] Courier accounts, shipping materials and dangerous goods training where needed
- [ ] Central or local lab: who receives results and how they are reviewed and signed

### 8. Equipment and facilities

- [ ] Equipment the protocol relies on (ECG, scales, blood pressure monitors, spirometers) is calibrated or maintained, with records
- [ ] Space for visits, confidential storage for records, and secure archiving
- [ ] Emergency equipment where the protocol requires it (for example, for first dosing)

### 9. Data collection: eCRF and source

- [ ] Accounts for the electronic data capture (EDC) system for everyone who enters or signs data, with training completed
- [ ] Agreement on what counts as source documents for each data point (and documented in a source document agreement or list)
- [ ] How queries are raised, answered and closed, and the timelines
- [ ] Access for the monitor to medical records for source data verification, in line with consent and local rules

### 10. Safety reporting

- [ ] Definitions: adverse event, serious adverse event, and any adverse events of special interest in the protocol
- [ ] Reporting timelines: serious adverse events to the sponsor within the protocol's deadline (commonly 24 hours of the site becoming aware) <!-- VERIFY: use the protocol's stated timeline; 24 hours is common but not universal -->
- [ ] Forms, the reporting route (EDC or paper), and the sponsor's safety contacts, including out of hours
- [ ] How the investigator assesses seriousness, severity and causality, and who may do it
- [ ] Pregnancy reporting and follow-up, where relevant
- [ ] Reporting to the ethics committee, as local rules require

### 11. Monitoring and recruitment

- [ ] The monitoring plan: visit frequency, on-site or remote, source data verification and the first visit after first enrollment
- [ ] Screening and enrollment logs set up
- [ ] Recruitment plan and target, and the approved recruitment materials
- [ ] Protocol deviations: how to record and report them

### 12. Close of the visit

- [ ] Open questions answered or logged
- [ ] Every action has an owner and a due date
- [ ] Date for the SIV report and the site's response
- [ ] The condition for site activation (often written confirmation from the sponsor once actions close)

## After the visit

1. **The monitor writes the SIV report** and a follow-up letter listing what was reviewed, who attended and the open actions. File both in the ISF.
2. **The site closes the actions** and sends evidence: a signed log, a missing CV, a corrected temperature record.
3. **The sponsor activates the site** in writing. Enrollment starts only after activation, not after the visit itself.
4. **The first monitoring visit** is usually scheduled soon after the first participant, to catch early errors in consent, eligibility and data entry.
5. **Keep the file current.** New staff are trained and added to the delegation log before they work; amendments are approved, trained and filed before use.

## Common mistakes

- **Version mismatch.** The consent form in the clinic is not the version the ethics committee approved. Check the version and date on every copy.
- **Delegation before signature.** A nurse takes consent before the PI signed her delegation entry. Every task done before the entry is a deviation.
- **Training only at the SIV.** Staff who joined a month later never signed the training log.
- **IP arrives before it can be stored correctly.** No continuous temperature record, or an uncalibrated fridge.
- **Unclear source.** Nobody agreed whether the eCRF or the hospital record is the source for vital signs, and the monitor finds two different values.
- **Safety contacts that do not answer at night.** Test the out-of-hours route.

## A worked example (invented)

*Example (invented for illustration).* A university hospital is joining a phase 3 trial of an inhaled therapy. Two weeks before the SIV, the coordinator works through the checklist with the monitor by email. Three points are open: the pharmacist's GCP certificate expired last month, the spirometer's calibration record is from last year, and the Spanish consent form is approved but the translator's attestation is not in the ISF. At the SIV the PI, two sub-investigators, two nurses, the pharmacist and the lab manager attend; the training log is signed; the pharmacy tour confirms storage and temperature logging. The SIV report lists the three open items, each with an owner and date. They close within ten days, and the sponsor sends the activation letter.
