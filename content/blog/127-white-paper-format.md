# White paper format: structure, length, layout and citations

*Last updated: 13 November 2026*

A white paper follows a predictable format: a title page, an executive summary, the problem, the evidence, the approach, implementation, a conclusion and references. Most business white papers are 6 to 12 pages. The format changes a little by type: scientific white papers add the strength of evidence, technical ones add architecture and trade-offs, policy ones add options and consultation questions.

This guide gives you the sections in order with a length for each, the page layout and typography, how to cite, and the variations for scientific, technical, policy, military and APA-style papers. If you need the background first, read [what is a white paper](/blog/what-is-a-white-paper). For the writing process itself, see [how to write a white paper](/blog/how-to-write-a-white-paper).

## The standard white paper structure

Here is the format most business and general white papers use, with a length guide for a paper of about 3,500 words.

| # | Section | What it does | Length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Title page | Title that names the problem or outcome, subtitle that names the approach, publisher, date | 1 page |
| 2 | Executive summary | The problem, why it matters now, the approach, the main recommendation | 150–250 words, ideally one page |
| 3 | Introduction | Who the paper is for, what it covers, what the reader will be able to decide | 150–300 words |
| 4 | The problem | What is wrong or changing, who it affects, what it costs, with cited figures | 500–800 words |
| 5 | Why current approaches fall short | A fair account of the alternatives and their limits | 300–500 words |
| 6 | The evidence | Research, data and examples, with how strong each finding is | 600–900 words |
| 7 | The approach | The principles first, then the method, product or policy | 500–800 words |
| 8 | Implementation | Steps, timeline, costs, risks, what is needed to start | 300–500 words |
| 9 | Recommendations and conclusion | Numbered actions and one clear next step | 200–400 words |
| 10 | About the author or organisation | Who published it and why they are credible; the product goes here | 50–150 words |
| 11 | References | Every source cited, in one style | As needed |

Two rules apply to every white paper format:

- **The executive summary must stand alone.** Many readers read only the title page and summary. If they cannot see the problem, the evidence in brief and the recommendation there, the rest will not be read.
- **The argument must work without the product.** In a problem–solution paper, keep sections 4 to 6 free of your brand. The product belongs in section 7 at the earliest, and often only in section 10.

### Sample white paper format (outline)

```text
[TITLE: the problem or the outcome, 6 to 12 words]
[Subtitle: the approach, in plain words]
[Organisation] | [Date] | [Version]

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The problem in one sentence, with one sourced number.
Why it matters now.
The approach this paper recommends.
The main recommendation and the first step.

1. INTRODUCTION
2. THE PROBLEM
3. WHY CURRENT APPROACHES FALL SHORT
4. WHAT THE EVIDENCE SAYS
5. A BETTER APPROACH
6. IMPLEMENTATION
7. RECOMMENDATIONS
8. CONCLUSION AND NEXT STEP
ABOUT [ORGANISATION]
REFERENCES
```

## Format by type of white paper

The core stays the same, but each type has sections its readers expect.

| Type | Sections to add or change | Length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem–solution (business) | The standard structure above | 6–12 pages |
| Backgrounder | Replace "why current approaches fall short" with "how it works" and a feature, function, benefit table; add use cases and getting started | 6–10 pages |
| Numbered list | Introduction, then 7 to 10 numbered points, each a heading with its explanation and evidence; conclusion | 4–8 pages |
| Scientific or research | Scope; state of the science by theme; where the evidence agrees and where it is uncertain; implications; recommendations; how the paper was prepared; glossary | 10–40 pages |
| Technical | Requirements and design goals; architecture (with a diagram); performance and evaluation; security, reliability and limits; comparison with alternatives; integration; glossary | 8–30 pages |
| Policy or government | Case for change; objectives; numbered proposals with cost and owner; options considered (table); implementation and timeline; impact; consultation questions | 15 pages to book length |
| Industry or market report | The field today; key trends; challenges; outlook (projections labelled as such); what it means for the reader; methodology note | 10–30 pages |

### Scientific white paper format

A scientific white paper is judged by its evidence, so the format makes the evidence visible:

- **State the strength of evidence** for each main finding (for example strong, moderate, limited) and say why.
- **Separate consensus from uncertainty** in a section of its own.
- **Add a "How this paper was prepared" note**: which kinds of studies it draws on, how they were found and the main limits.
- **Use the field's citation style** and cite primary studies, not other summaries, wherever you can.

### Technical white paper format

Engineers read for design and trade-offs. Include:

- **Requirements and design goals** before the design, so the reader can judge the design against them.
- **An architecture diagram** with a numbered walk-through of the data flow.
- **Performance figures with their conditions**: hardware, data size, configuration and date.
- **A comparison table**: approach, strengths, weaknesses, when to use it.
- **Limits and failure modes.** Engineers trust a paper that says when not to use the thing.

### Policy white paper format

Policy readers need options and consequences:

- **Numbered proposals**, each with its rationale, expected effect, cost and who must act.
- **An options table**: option, benefits, costs and risks, strength of evidence, why chosen or not.
- **Implementation and timeline**, with measures of progress.
- **Consultation questions** if you are inviting responses.

### Military white paper format (army and navy)

In the US armed forces, "white paper" usually means something much shorter than a business white paper: a memo-style paper of one or two pages, written for a commander or decision maker, that states an issue or proposal, the background, the discussion and a recommendation. Many follow a bottom-line-up-front (BLUF) style, with the recommendation in the opening lines. White papers are also requested in some defence research solicitations as a short first-stage proposal before a full one. <!-- VERIFY: confirm typical length and BLUF convention against current Army and Navy correspondence guidance (AR 25-50; SECNAV M-5216.5) and a recent defence BAA -->

Each service, command and solicitation sets its own format, page limit and markings. Use the template you were given; if there is none, a common pattern is:

```text
SUBJECT: [the issue, in one line]
1. PURPOSE. [What decision or information this paper supports.]
2. BOTTOM LINE. [The recommendation in one or two sentences.]
3. BACKGROUND. [Only what the reader needs.]
4. DISCUSSION. [Options, evidence, risks.]
5. RECOMMENDATION. [What to approve, by whom, by when.]
Prepared by: [name, office, contact] | Date
```

## Page layout and typography

Design cannot rescue weak evidence, but poor layout can bury strong evidence. Keep it plain.

| Element | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Page size | US Letter or A4, portrait. Landscape only for slide-style papers |
| Columns | One column, or a wide main column with a narrow sidebar for pull quotes and key figures |
| Body text | One readable font, 10–12 pt, line spacing about 1.3–1.5 |
| Headings | Two levels at most in the body; numbered sections help in technical and policy papers |
| Margins | At least 2 cm (0.75 in); more white space reads as more confident |
| Charts and tables | One on most spreads, each with a numbered caption, a one-line takeaway and its source underneath |
| Pull quotes | From your evidence (a number with its source), not slogans |
| Colour | Brand colours for the cover, headings and charts; black or dark grey text |
| Cover | Title, subtitle, publisher, date. A version number helps if you will update it |
| File format | PDF for distribution; keep the editable Word or design file for revisions |

A few checks before you publish the PDF:

1. Every link works, including those in the references.
2. Every chart is readable on a phone screen.
3. The document has a title and author in its PDF properties (search engines and readers show them).
4. Headings are real heading styles, so the PDF has a navigable outline and is accessible to screen readers.

## How to cite sources in a white paper

A white paper's credibility rests on its sources, so the citation format matters more than in most marketing content.

- **Pick one style and keep it.** Business and policy papers often use numbered endnotes or an author-date style such as APA. Scientific and technical papers should follow the journal style of their field (for example Vancouver in medicine, IEEE in engineering).
- **Source every number.** In the sentence ("a 2024 survey of 1,200 hospitals by…") or with a note marker.
- **Print the source under every chart.**
- **Prefer primary sources.** Trace a statistic quoted in a blog post back to the original study or dataset.
- **Describe your own data.** If a figure comes from your customers or a pilot, say how it was measured, the sample and the period.

### APA white paper format

APA does not have a special layout for white papers. To write a white paper in APA style, use APA heading levels, author-date in-text citations, and an APA 7 reference list.

To **cite** a white paper in APA 7, treat it as a report and add a description in square brackets:

> Organisation Name. (Year). *Title of the white paper* [White paper]. Publisher (omit if same as author). URL

For example:

> Nakamoto, S. (2008). *Bitcoin: A peer-to-peer electronic cash system* [White paper]. https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf

## Common white paper format mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| The summary describes the paper ("This paper discusses…") | Make it state the problem, the finding and the recommendation |
| The product appears on page one | Move it after the evidence, or to "About" |
| Numbers without sources | Add a citation for each, or remove it |
| Wall-of-text pages | Add a table, chart or list on most spreads |
| Too many heading levels | Use two in the body |
| No date or version | Add both to the cover |
| A conclusion that only repeats the summary | End with a specific next step |

## FAQ

### What is the format of a white paper?

A standard white paper has a title page, an executive summary, an introduction, a section on the problem, a section on the evidence, a section on the approach or solution, implementation or next steps, a conclusion, a short note about the author or organisation and a reference list. Scientific, technical and policy white papers add sections of their own, such as strength of evidence, architecture or options considered.

### How many pages should a white paper be?

Most business white papers are 6 to 12 pages including the cover and references, with a main body of about 2,500 to 5,000 words. Technical and scientific white papers often run 10 to 30 pages. Military and short community white papers can be one or two pages. If a call or client sets a limit, that limit wins.

### What font and layout should a white paper use?

Use one readable font family at 10 to 12 points for body text, generous margins, a single column or a wide main column with a narrow sidebar, and headings that let a reader scan the argument. Put one chart or table on most spreads, each with a caption and its source. Keep the brand in the cover and colours, not in the text.

### How do you cite sources in a white paper?

Use one citation style throughout: numbered notes or an author-date style such as APA for business and policy papers, the field's journal style for scientific and technical papers. Put the source of every statistic in the sentence or in a note, print sources under every chart, and end with a full reference list.

### Is there an APA white paper format?

APA does not define a special layout for white papers. You can write a white paper in APA style by using APA headings, in-text citations and a reference list. To cite a white paper in APA 7, treat it as a report: Author or Organisation. (Year). Title in italics [White paper]. Publisher. URL.

### What is the army white paper format?

In the US military, a white paper is usually a short memo-style document, often one or two pages, that states a problem or proposal and a recommendation for a decision maker, frequently with a bottom-line-up-front opening. Each service and command sets its own format, so follow the template your unit or the solicitation provides.
