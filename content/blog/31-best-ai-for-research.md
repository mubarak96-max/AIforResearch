# The best AI for research in 2026, by research stage

*Last updated: 12 November 2026*

The best AI for research depends on the stage you are at. Use a scholarly search tool (Semantic Scholar, Consensus, Elicit or AI for Research) to find papers, a PDF chat tool to read them, a citation map to see how a field connects, and an editor with real citations to write. No tool does all of it well, so pick by job.

This guide is for students, PhD candidates and researchers choosing AI research tools. It is the hub of our series on AI research tools; for tools that write long cited reports, see the [best AI for deep research](/blog/best-ai-for-deep-research).

## How we compared the tools

**We compared each tool on the same five criteria, using the vendor's own pages, checked November 2026.** This edition has no scored head-to-head test, so there are no scores below: you get a documented comparison plus a protocol you can run yourself.

| Criterion | The question we asked | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Sources | Does it search a scholarly index, the web, or your own PDFs? | Every answer is only as good as what was searched |
| 2. Citations you can open | Does each claim link to a specific paper? | You must confirm the paper says what the sentence says |
| 3. Full text | Does it read full texts, or only abstracts? | Methods, effect sizes and limits are in the full text |
| 4. Filters | Year, study type, peer review, field? | Lets you ask for trials or reviews, not opinion pieces |
| 5. Export | RIS, BibTeX, Word, a reference manager? | Your sources must reach your thesis, not stay in a chat |

**A scholarly index is a database of records of academic works (title, authors, journal, year, DOI, abstract) that you search directly.** Tools built on one return papers that exist; web assistants return pages, which may or may not be papers.

### The test protocol (for a scored edition)

1. Pick three questions from different fields, for example: "Does vitamin D supplementation reduce falls in adults over 65?", "Does remote work affect employee turnover?" and "How accurate are AI models at protein structure prediction?"
2. For each, list three papers an expert says must be found.
3. Run each question in every tool on the same day, with default settings.
4. Score known papers found (0 to 3), the share of peer-reviewed sources, and 10 claims per answer checked against the cited paper.
5. Ask five questions about the same three PDFs in every tool that reads PDFs, and score whether each answer cites the right page.

## The 12 tools, one by one

Facts come from each vendor's own pages, checked November 2026; prices change often.

### 1. AI for Research

**Good at:** the whole path from question to cited draft in one workspace: search across 250M+ scholarly works with study-type filters, cited Deep Research answers, PDF questions answered with page citations, and an editor with live citations in 10,000+ styles.

**Limits:** it reads abstracts when no open full text exists, and it does not search Google Scholar, Scopus or Web of Science. Costs are in the product section below.

### 2. Elicit

**Good at:** structured reviews: it screens against your criteria and extracts data into a table. The free Basic plan includes unlimited search across more than 138 million papers, unlimited summaries, chat with papers and Zotero import. Pro is $49 a user a month ($588 billed annually) and adds a systematic review workflow that screens up to 5,000 papers [1].

**Limits:** a smaller index than some rivals, and no writing editor. Full review: [Elicit AI](/blog/elicit-ai-review).

### 3. Consensus

**Good at:** yes-or-no research questions answered from papers only. Consensus searches 400M+ academic papers, and its Consensus Meter shows how much the top studies agree [2][3]. The free plan includes 10 Pro messages and 3 Deep Searches a month; Pro gives unlimited Pro messages and 15 Deep Searches, and students get 40% off for 12 months with a verified student email [2].

**Limits:** no web or policy sources, and the meter counts studies without weighing their design. Full review: [Consensus AI](/blog/consensus-ai-review).

### 4. SciSpace

**Good at:** reading one hard paper. Highlight a passage, table or equation and ask what it means. It also offers search, comparison tables and an agent for multi-step tasks [18]. <!-- VERIFY: SciSpace feature names and free plan on scispace.com (the site refused automated access in November 2026) -->

**Limits:** check every page reference. Full review: [SciSpace](/blog/scispace-review).

### 5. Scite

**Good at:** showing whether a finding held up: Smart Citations label later papers as supporting or contrasting a study. Scite claims 1.6B+ citations from 300M+ sources. Basic is $12 a month and Pro $40 a month, both billed yearly, after a 7-day free trial [4].

**Limits:** no free plan beyond the trial.

### 6. ResearchRabbit

**Good at:** browsing outward from papers you trust, collection by collection. Free Forever includes unlimited searches across 310+ million articles, up to 50 seed articles, and shared collections. RR+ is $10 a month billed annually or $12.50 monthly [5].

**Limits:** it is easy to wander; record why you kept each paper. Comparison: [Connected Papers vs ResearchRabbit vs Litmaps](/blog/connected-papers-vs-researchrabbit-vs-litmaps).

### 7. Semantic Scholar

**Good at:** free, no-nonsense paper search. Semantic Scholar is run by Ai2, a non-profit, and describes itself as a free, AI-powered research tool indexing over 200 million academic papers [6].

**Limits:** it finds papers and leaves the synthesis to you.

### 8. Perplexity

**Good at:** fast orientation with sources shown up front. The free plan includes 3 Pro Searches a day and 1 Research query a month; Education Pro is $10 a month for verified students and educators [7]. Since May 2026, Perplexity can draw on EBSCOhost research databases, though you need an institutional EBSCOhost login to open the full text [8].

**Limits:** results come from web ranking; check that each source is the paper itself. <!-- VERIFY: whether Perplexity's consumer app offers an Academic source setting and what it is called -->

### 9. ChatGPT

**Good at:** explaining concepts, suggesting search terms, and deep research reports that mix papers with policy and data. Deep research can be limited to sites you choose and downloaded as Word or PDF [9]. OpenAI lists deep research on every plan, "limited" on Free [10]. <!-- VERIFY: OpenAI help and pricing pages refused automated access in November 2026; confirm deep research limits per plan -->

**Limits:** it searches the web, often reads only abstracts of paywalled papers, and has no study-type filters. Guide: [ChatGPT deep research for academic work](/blog/chatgpt-deep-research-for-academic-work).

### 10. Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM)

**Good at:** answering questions only from sources you upload. Google renamed NotebookLM to Gemini Notebook on 16 July 2026 and says it is the same standalone product [11]. The free plan allows 100 notebooks and 50 sources per notebook [12].

**Limits:** it will not find the paper you are missing.

### 11. Zotero, with AI plugins

**Good at:** storing, organizing and citing references, free and open source. Zotero gives 300 MB of free sync storage; 2 GB costs $20 a year and unlimited $120 a year [13]. AI features come from third-party plugins such as Beaver [14]. <!-- VERIFY: that Zotero still has no built-in AI features -->

**Limits:** read a plugin's privacy terms before you send it your library. See [Zotero alternatives](/blog/zotero-alternatives).

### 12. Connected Papers

**Good at:** a one-shot overview of a field from a single paper. It builds a graph of similar papers from shared citations and references, using the Semantic Scholar corpus [15]. <!-- VERIFY: Connected Papers free plan (reported as 5 graphs a month) on connectedpapers.com/pricing -->

## Red flags: how to catch fabricated citations

**A fabricated citation is a reference that looks real but points to a paper that does not exist, or to a real paper that does not say what the sentence claims.** It is the most common failure of AI tools for research.

In a 2023 study of 636 references in literature reviews written by ChatGPT, 55% of GPT-3.5 citations and 18% of GPT-4 citations were fabricated [16]. In 2025, the Tow Center tested eight AI search engines on 1,600 queries and found they gave incorrect answers to more than 60% of them, often with broken or wrong links [17].

Check any AI research answer in four steps:

1. **Look up every DOI** at doi.org, and search the exact title in a scholarly index. No match means the paper probably does not exist.
2. **Open the paper and find the claim.** Read the paragraph around it, not just the abstract.
3. **Check the numbers digit by digit.** Effect sizes and sample sizes are where summaries go wrong.
4. **Check for retractions** before the reference goes into your thesis.

More on step 2: [how to check a claim against the research](/blog/how-to-check-a-claim-against-the-research).

## FAQ

### Which AI is best for research?

No single tool wins every stage. For finding papers, use a tool built on a scholarly index, such as Semantic Scholar, Consensus, Elicit or AI for Research. For reading one paper closely, use a PDF chat tool that cites pages. For mapping a field, use a citation map. For writing, use an editor that keeps citations attached to real papers. Most researchers combine two or three.

### Is ChatGPT good for research?

ChatGPT is good for orientation: explaining a concept, suggesting search terms, or a deep research report that mixes papers with policy and data pages. It is weaker for academic citation because it searches the web, often reads only abstracts of paywalled papers, and has no study-type filters. A 2023 study found 18% of GPT-4 citations were fabricated. Check every reference before you use it.

### What is the best free AI for research?

Semantic Scholar is free with no paid tier and covers 200M+ papers. ResearchRabbit's free plan includes unlimited searches and collections. Elicit's free plan includes unlimited search and summaries. Consensus gives 10 Pro messages and 3 Deep Searches a month free. AI for Research gives 45 credits a month, and its paper search, citation map and citation check cost 0 credits.

### Can AI tools for research make up sources?

Yes. General chatbots can produce references that look real but do not exist, and AI search engines can link to the wrong page. Tools built on a scholarly index return records that exist, but they can still misstate what a paper found. Look up every DOI, open the paper, and confirm that the cited passage supports your sentence.

### How should I choose an AI research tool?

Decide which stage you need help with, then judge tools on five criteria: what they search, whether each claim links to a source you can open, whether they read full text or abstracts, which filters they offer, and what they export. Run the same question in two tools and check ten claims from each before you pay for either.
