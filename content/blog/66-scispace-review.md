# SciSpace review: an honest look for students and researchers

*Last updated: 12 November 2026*

SciSpace is an AI research platform that combines paper search, chat with PDF, literature review tables, data extraction and a research Agent. Its strength is reading: it explains dense passages, tables and equations beside the paper. It has a free plan with limits. Like every AI paper summarizer, its summaries need checking against the page before you cite them.

This review is for students and researchers deciding whether SciSpace belongs in their workflow. It is part of our guide to [the best AI for deep research](/blog/best-ai-for-deep-research).

**A note on sources.** SciSpace's own pricing and feature pages refused automated access when we checked them in November 2026 [1], [2]. Facts below come from independent reviews dated June and September 2026 and are marked for verification. Check SciSpace's site before you buy.

## What is SciSpace?

**SciSpace is a web-based research assistant that lets you search a large index of papers, chat with PDFs, compare papers in tables and draft text with AI.** It was previously called Typeset. Reviews in 2026 put its index at about 280 million papers [3] <!-- VERIFY: index size on scispace.com -->.

### What are SciSpace's main tools?

| Tool | What it does | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Literature Review | Search by question or keywords; results in a table you can filter and export (CSV, XLSX, BibTeX) | [3] |
| Chat with PDF (Copilot) | Explains passages, math and tables in an uploaded or found paper; answers follow-up questions with references to the text | [3], [4] |
| Extract Data | Pulls chosen fields (method, sample, findings) from several PDFs into a table; exports CSV, Excel, RIS, BibTeX, XML | [3] |
| Deep Review | A longer, structured synthesis; one guide describes PICO question suggestions, screening criteria and a PRISMA diagram | [3], [4] |
| Agent | Multi-step tasks: plans, searches, extracts and drafts; uses monthly credits | [3] |
| Citation Generator | Formats references in 9,000+ styles | [3], [4] |
| AI Writer and Paraphraser | Drafting and rewording tools | [3], [4] |

## What does SciSpace do well?

SciSpace is at its best when you have a paper open and don't fully understand it.

- **Explaining hard passages.** Highlight a paragraph, a regression table or an equation and ask what it means. For a first-year PhD student reading outside their field, that saves real time. Library reviewers single out the PDF assistant as its most useful feature [4].
- **Many tools in one place.** Search, reading, comparison tables and citation formatting sit in one account, so you don't move files between four apps.
- **Side-by-side comparison.** The extraction table lets you see five studies' samples, methods and outcomes in one view, which is the raw material of a synthesis.
- **Exports that fit a workflow.** BibTeX and RIS exports move papers into Zotero or Mendeley [3].
- **Language support.** One review says Extract Data works across 75+ languages [3] <!-- VERIFY -->.

## Is SciSpace free? Plans and prices

Yes, there is a free Basic plan. Paid plans are built around monthly Agent credits. These figures come from a review dated 27 September 2026 [3]; SciSpace's pricing page [1] could not be loaded by our checker <!-- VERIFY: all prices and credit counts on scispace.com/pricing -->.

| Plan | Annual billing (per month) | Monthly billing | Agent credits a month | Agent tasks at once |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basic | Free | Free | 100 | 1 |
| Premium | $12 | $20 | 1,200 | 2 |
| Advanced | $70 | $90 | 10,000 | 4 |
| Max | $160 | $200 | 40,000 | 4 |

The same review reports that unused credits expire at the end of each billing cycle, and that standalone tools outside the Agent don't use credits, though other usage limits apply [3]. A UBC library guide notes conflicting descriptions of what the free tier includes, which is another reason to check the live page [4].

### Which plan fits?

- **Basic:** an undergraduate who reads a few papers a week and wants explanations.
- **Premium:** a master's or PhD student who uses chat with PDF and extraction tables most days.
- **Advanced or Max:** heavy Agent users, such as a team producing several evidence summaries a month. Compare the cost with your library's institutional tools first.

## Is SciSpace reliable and safe?

SciSpace is useful for orientation and not safe to quote unchecked. The UBC library guide, updated June 2026, collects reported problems: inaccurate claims about papers, poor paraphrasing, invented references, results skewed toward older literature, and limited access to full texts of paywalled papers [4]. These are user reports, not our test results, but they match the failure modes of every AI paper summarizer.

### A four-step check for any SciSpace answer

1. **Open the page it points to.** If the answer cites a passage, find it in the PDF and read the paragraph around it.
2. **Check numbers digit by digit.** Effect sizes, sample sizes and p-values are where summaries go wrong most often.
3. **Confirm every reference exists.** Look up the DOI in Crossref or the publisher's site. A reference that looks right but has no DOI is a warning sign.
4. **Check scope.** If a Deep Review or Agent run gives a conclusion, ask how many papers it read and whether paywalled papers were read in full or only by abstract.

*Example (invented, for illustration).* You ask SciSpace for the effect of a reading intervention in a trial of 412 pupils. The answer gives "d = 0.45". On page 9 of the PDF, 0.45 is the effect in the subgroup of 118 pupils with low baseline scores; the whole-sample effect is 0.21. Both numbers are in the paper. Only one answers your question.

### Uploading your own files

Read SciSpace's privacy policy before you upload unpublished work. The UBC guide notes it does not cover data handling, so check the vendor's terms directly [4]. Many journals and funders forbid uploading manuscripts under review to AI tools.

## SciSpace vs Elicit

Both search papers and build tables. They lean different ways.

| | SciSpace | Elicit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Best at | Reading and explaining single papers; many tools in one account | Structured extraction and systematic review screening |
| Index (vendor or review figure) | About 280M papers [3] | 138M+ papers [5] |
| Free plan | Basic, 100 Agent credits a month [3] | Unlimited search and summaries, limited reports [5] |
| Entry paid plan | Premium about $12/month billed annually [3] | Pro $49/user/month billed annually [5] |
| Review workflow | Deep Review | Pro screens up to 5,000 papers [5] |

**Decision rule:** if you spend most of your time inside one PDF, try SciSpace. If you spend it comparing 50 studies in a grid, try Elicit. For a yes-or-no question across many studies, see our [Consensus AI review](/blog/consensus-ai-review).

## Where does SciSpace stop?

As described in the 2026 reviews, SciSpace gives you search, reading help and drafts. These jobs still fall to you or another tool:

- **Judging study quality.** A summary can tell you a study was an RCT; it can't tell you whether randomization was done well.
- **A defensible systematic review.** Journals expect dual independent screening, recorded reasons for exclusion and a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram from real counts [6]. Check whether Deep Review records these in the form your journal wants.
- **Full texts behind paywalls.** If the tool can't read the full text, it summarizes from the abstract, and abstracts leave out limitations.

## FAQ

### Is SciSpace free?

SciSpace has a free Basic plan. A September 2026 review of its pricing lists 100 Agent credits a month and one Agent task at a time on Basic, with chat with PDF available but subject to usage limits. Paid plans start with Premium at about $12 a month billed annually or $20 monthly. Confirm current limits on SciSpace's pricing page before you rely on them.

### What is SciSpace used for?

SciSpace is used to find papers, read them with an AI assistant that explains passages and answers questions, compare several papers in a table, and draft review text. Students mostly use it to understand hard papers faster. Researchers use its literature review and data extraction tables to compare studies side by side before writing.

### Is SciSpace reliable?

It is reliable for orientation and less reliable for anything you will quote. A University of British Columbia library guide records user reports of inaccurate claims about papers and invented references. Treat every summary as a pointer: open the cited page, check the number or quote, and confirm that each reference exists before it goes into your work.

### SciSpace vs Elicit: which is better?

SciSpace is the better fit if your day is spent reading individual PDFs and you want explanations next to the text. Elicit is the better fit if your deliverable is a structured extraction table or a screened set of papers: its Pro plan, $49 a user a month billed annually as checked in November 2026, includes a systematic review workflow that screens up to 5,000 papers.

### Is it safe to upload unpublished work to SciSpace?

Read SciSpace's privacy policy and terms before uploading anything confidential, such as an unpublished manuscript, patient data or a grant you are reviewing. Many journals and funders forbid putting material under review into third-party AI tools. If you are unsure, ask your supervisor or research office, and upload only published papers.
