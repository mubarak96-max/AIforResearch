# What is a white paper? Meaning, types and examples

*Last updated: 13 November 2026*

A white paper is an authoritative report that explains a problem and argues for an approach to it, using evidence rather than claims. Governments publish white papers to set out policy proposals. Companies publish them to educate buyers. Scientists and engineers write them to explain the state of a field or how a technology works. Whatever the setting, the reader is about to make a decision, and the white paper is there to inform it.

This guide explains what the term means in each setting, where it comes from, the main kinds, how long a white paper is and who writes one. It is the hub of our white paper series. For a step-by-step writing method, read [how to write a white paper](/blog/how-to-write-a-white-paper). For real documents to learn from, see [white paper examples](/blog/white-paper-examples).

## White paper meaning

**A white paper is a document that presents a problem, the evidence about it, and a recommended approach, for readers who must decide what to do.** Three features set it apart from other documents:

1. **It is built on evidence.** Statistics, studies, data and worked examples carry the argument. A white paper without sources is an opinion piece.
2. **It recommends something.** A policy, a method, a standard, a technology or a research priority. It does not just describe.
3. **It is written for decision makers outside the specialist core.** Officials, executives, buyers, funders or the informed public. It explains what a specialist would take for granted.

What "white paper" means depends a little on who publishes it:

| Setting | What "white paper" means | Typical reader | What it asks the reader to do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Government | A statement of policy proposals, often before legislation | Parliament, officials, the public | Support or respond to the proposals |
| Business and marketing | A persuasive report on an industry problem and an approach to it | Buyers evaluating a purchase | Consider an approach, then a vendor |
| Science and research | A summary of the state of the evidence on a question, or a community statement of priorities | Funders, agencies, policy makers, other fields | Fund, prioritise or act on the evidence |
| Technology | An explanation of how a system, protocol or architecture works and why it was designed that way | Engineers, technical buyers, developers | Adopt, build on or evaluate the technology |
| Crypto and blockchain projects | The founding description of a protocol or token: its problem, design and economics | Developers, users, investors | Understand and join the project |

## Where the term "white paper" comes from

The name comes from British government publishing. Official reports were graded partly by their covers. Long, formal reports were bound in blue and known as **blue books**. Shorter statements of government policy were issued in plain white covers and called **white papers**. The Churchill White Paper of 1922, which set out British policy in Mandatory Palestine, is often cited as an early, well-known example.

The UK still uses the term in this sense, alongside the **green paper**, a consultation document that invites views before the government settles on a policy. A white paper usually comes later and states what the government intends to do. Other governments use the same name: Australia, for example, publishes defence and foreign policy white papers, and the European Commission has issued white papers on topics such as artificial intelligence (2020).

From the 1990s onward, technology companies borrowed the term for long-form reports that explain a technical problem and the vendor's approach to it. Business-to-business (B2B) marketing then adopted it widely. In 2008, Satoshi Nakamoto published "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System" as a short white paper, and since then most blockchain projects have launched with one.

## The main kinds of white paper

Different fields use different kinds. The names below are the ones writers and editors use most often.

### Business white papers

Business white papers are marketing documents, but good ones read as education. The three classic types, as described by Gordon Graham in *White Papers For Dummies*, are:

- **Problem–solution:** describes an industry-wide problem and argues for a new approach to it. Used early in a buying cycle, often for lead generation.
- **Backgrounder:** explains how one product, service or method works and what it offers. Used late in a buying cycle, when buyers compare options.
- **Numbered list:** a set of tips, questions or points about an issue, such as "7 questions to ask before you replace your billing system". Quick to read and easy to share.

A fourth kind is common in consulting and analyst work: the **industry or market report**, which describes the state of a sector, its trends and its outlook.

### Scientific and research white papers

In science, "white paper" has two common meanings.

The first is a **state-of-the-evidence paper**: a synthesis of what research shows on a question, written for people who must act on it, such as a health agency, an industry body or a funder. It cites peer-reviewed studies, says where they agree and disagree, and ends with recommendations. It differs from a review article mainly in its audience and its purpose: a review informs researchers, a white paper informs a decision.

The second is a **community white paper**: a short paper in which a group of scientists sets out the case for a research direction, an instrument or a programme. Large planning exercises collect these. The US astronomy decadal survey (Astro2020) and the Snowmass process in particle physics both invited science white papers from the community to inform their priorities. <!-- VERIFY: confirm Astro2020 and Snowmass 2021 both solicited community "white papers" under that name -->

### Technical white papers

A technical white paper explains how a technology works: its architecture, its design choices, its performance and its limits. Cloud providers, chip makers and security vendors publish many of them. The AWS Well-Architected Framework, for example, is published as a whitepaper that sets out design principles for cloud systems. The reader is an engineer or technical buyer, so the paper can use technical language, but it must still be explicit about trade-offs. See the [technical white paper template](/templates/technical-white-paper).

### Policy and government white papers

A government white paper states policy proposals and the reasons for them, often before a bill. Think tanks, NGOs and industry bodies also write policy white papers to propose changes to law or regulation. These usually include the case for change, the options considered, the proposal, its costs and benefits, and how it would be implemented. A **policy brief** is the short, decision-ready cousin: two to four pages of key messages and recommendations. See the [policy white paper template](/templates/policy-white-paper).

### Crypto and project white papers

A blockchain white paper describes a protocol: the problem it solves, how the system works, its security assumptions and, for tokens, its economics. The Bitcoin paper (2008) is about nine pages and reads much like a short computer science paper. Ethereum's white paper, by Vitalik Buterin, followed in late 2013. Because many token white papers have been used to promote weak or fraudulent projects, readers now look hard at whether the claims are specific and testable.

## What a white paper is not

A white paper is often confused with neighbouring formats. The differences matter, because readers expect different things from each.

| Document | How it differs from a white paper |
| --- | --- |
| Research paper | Reports new, original findings; peer reviewed; written for specialists. A white paper usually synthesises existing evidence for decision makers. See [white paper vs research paper](/blog/white-paper-vs-research-paper). |
| Brochure or sales sheet | Lists features and benefits with little evidence. A white paper leads with the problem and the evidence. |
| Case study | Tells the story of one customer or project. A white paper may contain case studies as evidence. |
| E-book | Broader, lighter, more visual; often a guide to a whole topic. A white paper is narrower and more formal. |
| Policy brief | Two to four pages, decision-ready. A policy white paper is longer and sets out options and the reasoning in full. |
| Report | Can be purely descriptive. A white paper always argues for something. See [how to write a research report](/blog/how-to-write-a-research-report). |

## How long is a white paper?

There is no fixed length, but each kind has a typical range.

| Kind | Typical length |
| --- | --- |
| Business problem–solution or backgrounder | 6 to 12 pages, about 2,500 to 5,000 words |
| Numbered list | 4 to 8 pages |
| Technical white paper | 8 to 30 pages |
| Scientific state-of-the-evidence paper | 10 to 40 pages |
| Community science white paper | Often capped by the call, commonly a few pages |
| Government white paper | 30 to several hundred pages |
| Crypto project white paper | 9 to 50 pages |
| Military white paper | Often 1 to 2 pages |

Gordon Graham puts the main body of most business white papers at 6 to 8 pages, or about 10 to 12 pages including front and back matter. If you have less than about 1,500 words of substance, an article or a brief is usually the better format.

## Who writes white papers, and who reads them

- **Government departments** write policy white papers, with ministers and civil servants. Parliaments, journalists, interest groups and the public read them.
- **Product marketers and specialist freelance writers** write business white papers, usually with a subject expert from the company. Buyers, analysts and procurement teams read them.
- **Scientists, research groups and learned societies** write scientific white papers. Funders, agencies, industry and other researchers read them.
- **Engineers and solution architects** write technical white papers. Engineers and technical evaluators read them.
- **Think tanks, NGOs and consultancies** write policy and industry white papers. Officials, donors and executives read them.

In every case, the reader is busy and sceptical. Most will read the title, the executive summary and the recommendations, and will look at the evidence only if those pages earn their trust.

## What makes a good white paper

Across all kinds, good white papers share five habits:

1. **One problem, stated precisely.** "Hospitals readmit too many heart-failure patients within 30 days" beats "healthcare faces challenges".
2. **Evidence for every claim.** Each statistic has a source the reader can open. If a number is your own, say how you measured it.
3. **Fair treatment of alternatives.** Describing other approaches honestly, including their strengths, makes your recommendation believable.
4. **An executive summary that stands alone.** Many readers stop there.
5. **A specific next step.** What should the reader do on Monday?

## FAQ

### What is a white paper in simple terms?

A white paper is an authoritative report that explains a problem and sets out an approach to it, backed by evidence. Governments use white papers to announce policy proposals, companies use them to educate buyers before a purchase, and scientists and engineers use them to explain the state of a field or how a technology works. The common thread is that the argument rests on evidence, not on claims.

### Why is it called a white paper?

The name comes from British government publishing. Short policy statements were issued with plain white covers, while longer official reports were bound in blue and called blue books. The Churchill White Paper of 1922 on British policy in Palestine is often cited as an early, well-known example. Business and technology borrowed the term in the late twentieth century for any authoritative report on a problem and its solution.

### What is the difference between a white paper and a research paper?

A research paper reports new, original findings to other researchers and goes through peer review in a journal. A white paper usually reports no new study: it gathers existing evidence to inform a decision, for readers outside the specialist field, and it is published by an organisation without peer review. A white paper may cite research papers heavily, but its job is to recommend, not to discover.

### How long is a white paper?

Most business white papers run 6 to 12 pages, roughly 2,500 to 5,000 words with a cover, executive summary and references. Technical and scientific white papers can be longer, and government white papers are often book-length. Some formats are short: the white paper in the Bitcoin tradition is about 9 pages, and military white papers are often one or two pages.

### Is a white paper marketing?

In business, yes: a white paper is a content-marketing asset that attracts and informs buyers, often in exchange for their contact details. It works only when it reads as education rather than advertising. A white paper that leads with the problem and the evidence, and mentions the product only at the end, is far more persuasive than one that reads like a brochure.

### Can AI write a white paper?

AI can draft the structure and the prose quickly, but it must not supply the facts. General chatbots can invent statistics and sources, which destroys a white paper's credibility. Use a tool that searches real research first and cites it, give it your own data, and check every number against its source before you publish.
