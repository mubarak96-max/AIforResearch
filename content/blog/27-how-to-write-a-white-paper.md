# How to write a white paper (with AI, without making things up)

*Last updated: 6 November 2026*

A white paper is a persuasive, research-based report that explains a problem and argues for an approach to solving it. The three common types are the backgrounder, the problem and solution paper, and the numbered list. Its power comes from evidence, so cite real data, check every claim, and never let AI supply the statistics.

This white paper writing guide is for B2B marketers, consultants and policy teams. It covers structure, a fill-in template, research, persuasion without overclaiming, AI drafting, design and cost. It is part of our series on [citations that point to real sources](/blog/ai-citation-generator).

## What white paper writing is

**A white paper is a persuasive, research-based report that explains a problem and argues for an approach to solving it.** It reads like a report and works like an argument. Readers are usually evaluating a decision: a purchase, a policy, a standard. They download a white paper because they want to understand the problem before they talk to a vendor.

That is why credibility is the whole game. A white paper that reads like a brochure gets closed. One that teaches the reader something, with sources they can check, gets forwarded to their boss.

## The 3 types of white papers

Gordon Graham, author of *White Papers For Dummies*, describes three main types. [1][2]

| Type | What it does | Best for | Example title (fictional) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Backgrounder** | Explains the features and benefits of one product, service or method in depth | Late-stage buyers comparing options; product launches | "How the FlowGrid sensor network works" |
| **Problem and solution** | Describes an industry-wide problem and argues for a new approach, without selling hard | Early-stage readers; lead generation | "Why mid-size water utilities lose 20% of treated water, and how to find it" |
| **Numbered list** | A set of tips, questions or points about an issue | Quick reads; drawing attention | "7 questions to ask before you replace your meter network" |

Graham's guidance is that a numbered list can be combined with either of the other two, but a backgrounder and a problem and solution paper don't mix well. [2] Pick one main type before you outline.

## White paper structure and template

A problem and solution white paper usually follows this order. Graham puts the main body of most white papers at 6 to 8 pages, with 10 to 12 pages total once you add front and back matter. [2]

| Section | Purpose | Approx. length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title and subtitle | Name the problem, not the product | One line each |
| Executive summary | The problem, the stakes, the approach, in one paragraph | 100 to 200 words |
| The problem | Who it affects, how much it costs, why it's getting worse | 1 to 2 pages |
| Why existing approaches fall short | Fair description of current options | 1 page |
| A better approach | The method or principles, not the product | 1 to 2 pages |
| Evidence | Data, studies, case results | 1 to 2 pages |
| How to get started | Practical next steps, criteria for choosing | Half a page |
| About the publisher | Short company description; the product belongs here | A paragraph |
| References | Every source cited | As needed |

### Fill-in white paper writing template

```markdown
# [Title: name the problem the reader has]
## [Subtitle: the approach, in plain words]

## Executive summary
[One paragraph. Who has the problem. What it costs them, with one number
and its source. Why current approaches don't fix it. The approach this paper
recommends. What the reader will learn.]

## 1. The problem
[Who is affected and how. Quantify it: cost, time, risk.]
[Evidence: 2 to 3 cited data points. Name each source in the sentence.]
[Why it's getting worse now: regulation, cost, technology, demand.]

## 2. Why current approaches fall short
[Describe each common approach fairly, with its real strengths.]
[Name the limit each one hits, with evidence where you have it.]

## 3. A better approach
[The principles or method. Not your product name.]
[How it addresses each limit from section 2.]

## 4. Evidence that it works
[Studies, pilot data, customer results you have permission to use.]
[State sample size and conditions for every result.]

## 5. How to get started
[Steps, a checklist, or criteria for evaluating any solution.]

## About [organization]
[Two to four sentences. This is where the product goes.]

## References
[One consistent style. Every number in the paper traces here.]
```

## Research: finding studies and data you can cite

Every number in your white paper will be read by someone looking for a reason to doubt it. Good sources, in rough order of persuasive weight:

1. **Government and agency data:** statistical agencies, regulators, central banks. Free, neutral and hard to argue with.
2. **Peer-reviewed research:** studies in your field. Search a scholarly index and read beyond the abstract.
3. **Industry and association reports:** useful, but check who paid for them and how they sampled.
4. **Your own data:** customer results, internal benchmarks, original surveys. The most distinctive source you have. Describe the method.
5. **Expert interviews:** quotes from named practitioners add authority. Get permission and approval of quotes.

Avoid citing statistics you found quoted in a blog post. Trace them to the original source; often the number has changed, lost context, or never existed. If you are reviewing a body of research to build the evidence section, see [AI literature review tools](/blog/ai-literature-review-tools).

## Writing persuasively without overclaiming

Overclaiming is the fastest way to lose a skeptical reader. Rules:

**Wrong:** "Studies show predictive maintenance eliminates downtime."
**Right:** "In a 2024 study of 40 plants, predictive maintenance cut unplanned downtime by 23% on average (fictional example)."

**Wrong:** "The leading solution trusted by thousands."
**Right:** "Used by 140 utilities in North America (as of September 2026)." Use only figures you can document.

**Wrong:** "Traditional approaches are outdated and fail."
**Right:** "Manual audits work well for small networks but take about three weeks per 100 miles of pipe (fictional example)."

More rules:

- **Match the verb to the evidence.** "Suggests" for one study, "shows" for several consistent ones, "proves" almost never.
- **Give the denominator.** "Reduced costs by $2 million" means nothing without "across 12 sites over three years."
- **Be fair to alternatives.** Describing a competitor's real strength makes your comparison believable.
- **Keep the product out of sections 1 to 4** in a problem and solution paper. The argument should stand without it.

## Using AI to draft: where it helps, where it invents

White paper writing AI tools can save days, if you keep them away from the facts.

| Task | AI is good at it | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outline from your notes and goals | Yes | Generic structure; edit for your argument |
| First draft from sources you provide | Yes | Paraphrase can shift meaning; compare with the source |
| Summarizing studies you've read | Yes, with PDFs attached | Misread numbers; check each one on the page |
| Generating statistics or "studies show" | No | Invented figures and sources |
| Writing citations from memory | No | Fabricated or mismatched references |
| Tightening and tone | Yes | Can blur technical precision |
| Title and subtitle options | Yes | Check they promise only what the paper delivers |

The risk is documented. In a 2023 study, ChatGPT-3.5 wrote 30 short medical papers with 115 references: 47% were fabricated and 46% were real but inaccurate. [3] In 2023 a US federal judge sanctioned lawyers who filed a brief citing court decisions ChatGPT had invented. [4] A white paper with one invented statistic loses the trust that every real one was supposed to build.

A safe workflow:

1. **Collect sources first.** Studies, datasets, interviews, internal data.
2. **Draft from them,** with a tool that cites a real index or the PDFs you supplied.
3. **Run a claim check** on the full draft. List every factual claim and confirm each against its source. See [AI fact checker](/blog/ai-fact-checker) for a method.
4. **Have a subject expert review** the evidence and approach sections.

## Design and export

Design signals seriousness, but it can't fix weak evidence. Keep it simple:

- **Use your brand template** in Word or your design tool. A clean single column with generous margins beats a dense brochure layout.
- **One chart per key number,** with the source printed under it.
- **Pull quotes from your data,** not slogans.
- **Export to PDF** for distribution, and keep a Word version for the next revision.
- **Check the PDF:** links work, references are complete, charts are readable on a phone.

## Writer vs. service: costs and when to outsource

Published fee estimates vary widely. Graham reports that most B2B marketers expect to pay $5,000 to $8,000 for an experienced writer, roughly $1,000 per page of main body, with design and graphics extra. [2] An AWAI overview cites estimates of $3,000 to $6,000 for a decently produced white paper, with high-end papers above $10,000. [5] Treat these as rough ranges and ask for a quote.

| Option | Typical cost | Choose it when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In-house writer | Staff time | You have a subject expert who writes well and has two to four weeks |
| Freelance specialist | Several thousand dollars per paper (see above) | You need it done well and have the research material ready |
| White paper writing service or agency | Usually higher than a freelancer | You need research, interviews, design and promotion handled together |

Questions to ask any writer or service:

1. Does the price include research and expert interviews, or only writing?
2. How many revision rounds?
3. How will you source and cite statistics? Will every number have a reference?
4. Do you use AI, and how do you check its output?
5. Is design included?

## FAQ

### What is white paper writing?

White paper writing is the work of producing a persuasive, research-based report that explains a problem and argues for an approach to solving it. In business it is a marketing and sales asset aimed at people evaluating a purchase or policy. Its persuasive power comes from evidence: data, cited research and specific examples rather than product claims.

### How long is a white paper?

Most commercial white papers have a main body of about 6 to 8 pages, or roughly 2,500 to 4,000 words, plus a cover, summary and references. Gordon Graham, author of White Papers For Dummies, puts the total with front and back matter at about 10 to 12 pages. Shorter pieces work better as articles or briefs.

### How much does a white paper writing service cost?

Published estimates for an experienced freelance writer cluster around $3,000 to $8,000 for the text, with design often billed separately. Agencies and specialist technical writers charge more, and marketplace writers charge much less. Ask what the price includes: research, interviews, revision rounds, citations and design are each common add-ons.

### Can AI write a white paper?

AI can draft structure, sections and summaries quickly, but it shouldn't supply the facts. Chatbots asked for statistics or sources can invent numbers or attach citations to papers that don't exist, which ruins a document whose value is credibility. Use AI with sources you provide or a tool that cites from a real index, then check every claim.

### What is the best AI tool for white paper writing?

The best tool is one that ties every claim to a source you can open. Look for citations from a real index of published research, the ability to chat with your own source PDFs, claim checking, collaboration with tracked changes, and Word and PDF export. General chatbots are fine for outlines and editing, but not for statistics.
