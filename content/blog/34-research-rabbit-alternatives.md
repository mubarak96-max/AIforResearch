# Research Rabbit alternatives: 7 tools to discover papers

*Last updated: 12 November 2026*

The best Research Rabbit alternatives are Connected Papers for a quick similarity map, Litmaps for saved maps with alerts, Local Citation Network and Inciteful for free maps built from open data, Semantic Scholar Research Feeds for new-paper recommendations, Elicit for question-based search, and AI for Research's Citation Map for reportable snowballing next to your writing. Most have free plans.

This list is for students and researchers who searched "rabbit research" or the Research Rabbit app and want to see what else does the job. It belongs to our guide to [the best AI for deep research](/blog/best-ai-for-deep-research). For a detailed three-way comparison of the best-known options, read [Connected Papers vs ResearchRabbit vs Litmaps](/blog/connected-papers-vs-researchrabbit-vs-litmaps); this page doesn't repeat it.

## What is Rabbit Research (ResearchRabbit)?

**ResearchRabbit is a literature discovery app: you add seed papers to a collection and it recommends related, earlier and later work, so your map grows as you browse.** People search for it as "rabbit research", "Research Rabbit" or "research rabbit app". It is good at turning a handful of papers into a reading list, and its free plan is generous.

As checked in November 2026, ResearchRabbit's Free Forever plan includes unlimited searches across 310+ million articles, an unlimited library and collections, shared collections and up to 50 seed articles [1]. ResearchRabbit+ costs $10 a month on the annual plan or $12.50 monthly, with up to 300 seeds, advanced search controls, multiple projects and Signals alerts [1]. A rebuilt version launched on 20 October 2025, "thanks to our partnership with Litmaps" [2]. Litmaps had announced earlier in 2025 that it was acquiring ResearchRabbit [10].

### Why look for a Research Rabbit alternative?

Common reasons:

1. **You hit the 50-seed cap** on a large topic and don't want to pay.
2. **You need output you can report**, such as a CSV or RIS of every candidate for a systematic review.
3. **You want a different kind of link**: similarity rather than direct citation, or a path between two papers.
4. **You want alerts or a feed** without building collections.
5. **You start from a question**, not a paper.

## 1. Connected Papers: best for a one-paper overview

**Good at:** speed. Paste one paper and you get a graph of a few dozen similar papers, grouped by shared references and citations, so sub-topics show as clusters. It finds papers that sit in the same conversation even when they don't cite each other. Its free plan caps the number of graphs a month; its pricing page did not load for our checker, so confirm the cap there [8]. <!-- VERIFY: free graph cap and paid plan prices on connectedpapers.com/pricing -->

**Choose it over ResearchRabbit when** you want an overview in one minute, not a collection. Full details are in our [three-way comparison](/blog/connected-papers-vs-researchrabbit-vs-litmaps).

## 2. Litmaps: best for a long project with alerts

**Good at:** keeping a living map of a topic. Pro allows unlimited inputs, articles and maps, with daily or configurable alerts, for $10 a month or $120 a year; the annual price requires an academic email [3]. The free plan gives 100 articles, 1 map, up to 20 inputs and a monthly summary [3]. Litmaps now also develops ResearchRabbit [2], so the two are converging.

**Choose it over ResearchRabbit when** you want alerts across a three-year PhD and many seeds on one map.

## 3. Local Citation Network: best free option for a list of papers

**Local Citation Network is a free, open-source web app that builds a citation network from a set of seed articles and suggests the most-cited and most-citing papers missing from your set.** It uses data from OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Crossref or Zotero, and its developer says it was built "for free and for fun" under a GPL-3 licence [4].

**Good at:**

- **Starting from many papers at once.** Paste a list of DOIs, one per row, or scan a plain-text file such as a reference manager export for DOIs [4].
- **Ranking what's missing.** "Top Cited" and "Top Citing" lists show the locally most relevant articles not yet in your set [4].
- **Reportable output.** It retrieves all cited or citing articles for direct citation searching in systematic reviews, and exports CSV and RIS [4].

**Limits:** citing articles come only from OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar, because Crossref doesn't provide citations [4]. There are no accounts, alerts or collections; you save a network as a JSON file.

## 4. Inciteful: best for connecting two papers

**Good at:** two jobs other tools don't combine. Paper Discovery expands one or more seed papers into a ranked list and a graph. Literature Connector finds the papers that link two papers you name, which helps when you are bridging two fields. It is free and builds on OpenAlex data [9]. <!-- VERIFY: inciteful.xyz now redirects to incitefulmed.com/academic; confirm the Paper Discovery and Literature Connector tools, OpenAlex as source, free use and export options on the current site -->

**Choose it over ResearchRabbit when** your question sits between two literatures, such as "sleep research" and "educational assessment".

## 5. Semantic Scholar Research Feeds: best for staying current

**Semantic Scholar is a free, AI-powered search and discovery tool; its Research Feeds are "an adaptive research recommender" that learns which papers you care about** [5]. Feeds refresh daily and recommend papers from Semantic Scholar's corpus published in the last 3 months [5]. You can also set email alerts for authors, papers, topics and feeds, and keep papers in a Library with folders and bulk export [5].

**Limits:** it is a feed, not a map. You won't see how papers connect. Its AI summaries (TLDRs) are limited to computer science and biomedicine [5].

**Choose it over ResearchRabbit when** you already have your core papers and want to hear about new ones without maintaining a map.

## 6. Elicit: best when you start from a question

**Good at:** finding papers by meaning when you don't have a seed. Elicit searches over 138 million papers from Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex and PubMed, with unlimited search and summaries on its free plan [6], and its Pro plan adds a systematic review workflow. It is not a citation map: it won't show you who cites whom. Read our [Elicit AI review](/blog/elicit-ai-review) for plans and limits.

**Choose it over ResearchRabbit when** you have a question and no starting papers yet.

## 7. AI for Research: best for snowballing you will report

AI for Research's **[Citation Map](/features/citation-map)** shows the references and citing papers of a seed paper (OpenAlex, with Semantic Scholar as fallback) as a graph and a list. You can run snowballing rounds across a whole set, see candidates ranked by how many set papers link to them, find reviews and meta-analyses that cite your set, and export RIS or CSV. **Similar papers** in the Thesis Suite recommends papers from a title or abstract. Both cost 0 credits.

**Limits:** Citation Map draws direct citation links, not a similarity layout like Connected Papers, and it has no new-paper alerts. The method behind it is in [citation snowballing](/blog/citation-snowballing).

## How do you find similar research papers? A 6-step workflow

**To find similar research papers, combine a keyword search with citation links from your best papers, then screen every candidate the same way.** One tool rarely covers both.

1. **Search first.** Run your keyword or database search and pick 3 to 5 papers that sit at the center of your question.
2. **Map from the set.** Put those seeds into one map tool. Use a similarity tool (Connected Papers) for a new field, or a citation tool (Local Citation Network, Citation Map) when you must report the search.
3. **Bridge if needed.** If your topic spans two fields, run a two-paper path search.
4. **Export the candidates** as RIS or CSV before you screen, so the list is fixed and countable.
5. **Screen against your criteria**, the same ones you used for database results.
6. **Set a feed or alert** on the final set so new papers reach you.

### Worked example (invented, for illustration)

A master's student is writing on "peer tutoring and exam anxiety in first-year students". A database search gives 4 central papers.

- Citation Map, one round from the 4 seeds: 212 references and citing papers. Ranked by links into the set, 31 are linked to 2 or more seeds.
- Screening the 31 titles and abstracts: 11 are relevant; 3 were already in her database results, so 8 are new.
- A Semantic Scholar feed on the final 15 papers brings 2 new relevant papers over the next two months.

Her methods line: "Forward and backward citation searching from 4 seed papers (November 2026) identified 8 additional studies." That is the sentence a supervisor or reviewer wants. Counting these records in the PRISMA "other methods" column is covered in [how to make a PRISMA flow diagram](/blog/how-to-make-a-prisma-flow-diagram).

## FAQ

### Is Research Rabbit free?

Yes. As checked on its pricing page in November 2026, ResearchRabbit's Free Forever plan includes unlimited searches across 310+ million articles, an unlimited library and collections, sharing, and up to 50 seed articles. ResearchRabbit+ costs $10 a month on the annual plan or $12.50 monthly and raises the limit to 300 seed articles, with multiple projects and Signals alerts.

### What replaced Research Rabbit?

Nothing replaced it. ResearchRabbit was rebuilt: a new version launched on 20 October 2025 through a partnership with Litmaps, which had announced it was acquiring ResearchRabbit earlier that year. It now has a paid ResearchRabbit+ tier. If you liked the old free browsing experience, the Free Forever plan still exists, with a 50-seed limit.

### What is the best free Research Rabbit alternative?

For a quick map from one paper, Connected Papers. For a free, open-source map from a whole list of DOIs with CSV and RIS export, Local Citation Network. For a daily feed of new papers like the ones you save, Semantic Scholar Research Feeds. Each is free to use, though Connected Papers caps graphs a month.

### How do I find similar research papers to one I already have?

Start from the paper's DOI. Look at its reference list (backward) and at papers that cite it (forward), then use a similarity tool that compares shared references and citations to find papers in the same conversation that don't cite each other. Save candidates to one list, screen them against your criteria, and record which tool and seed you used.

### Is a citation map enough for a literature review?

No. A citation map finds papers linked to the ones you already have, so it inherits their blind spots: new papers with few citations, work in other languages and studies no one in your set cites. Run a keyword or database search first, then use the map to fill gaps, and report it as citation searching.
