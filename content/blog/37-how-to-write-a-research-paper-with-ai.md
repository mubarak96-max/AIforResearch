# How to use AI for writing research papers and stay within the rules

*Last updated: 12 November 2026*

You can use AI for writing research papers if you stay the author: you set the question, supply the data, check every citation, and disclose substantive AI use the way your journal asks. Use AI to search, outline, draft from your own notes and review. Do not let it invent results or references, and do not list it as an author.

This guide is for students and researchers who want a workflow that holds up with a supervisor, an editor or an integrity office. It belongs to our series on [the structure of a research paper](/blog/how-to-write-a-research-paper), which covers each section in detail.

## Is it allowed to use AI to write a research paper?

Usually yes, with conditions. Most large publishers allow AI assistance in preparing a manuscript, require disclosure of substantive use, and say AI tools cannot be authors because they cannot take responsibility for the work [1][2][4][5].

**An AI disclosure statement is a short passage in a manuscript that names the AI tools used, what they were used for, and confirms the authors reviewed the output and take responsibility for it.**

Three ground rules come out of every policy we checked in November 2026:

1. **You are the author; AI is an assistant.** Accountability for every sentence, number and reference stays with you.
2. **Disclose substantive use.** Grammar and spelling fixes are often exempt; drafting, restructuring, literature search and analysis help are not.
3. **No fabrication.** AI-generated data, images of results or references to papers that don't exist count as misconduct regardless of how they were made.

Course and university rules can be stricter than journal rules. A class may ban AI drafting entirely. Read your syllabus or graduate school policy before you start. Our [guide to AI policies in academia](/blog/ai-in-academic-research-policies) summarizes publisher, funder and university positions.

## A 6-step workflow: AI for writing research papers

The safest way to use AI for writing research papers is to split each step into what AI does, what you do, and what you keep for your records.

| Step | AI does | You do | Keep a record of |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Outline | Proposes headings and a word budget | Choose the argument and order | The outline you approved |
| 2. Sources | Searches and summarizes abstracts | Read the key papers in full | Search terms, dates, databases |
| 3. Draft | Writes paragraphs from your notes with citations | Rewrite in your voice; check citations | Which sections were AI-drafted |
| 4. Revise | Flags weak claims and unclear text | Accept or reject each change | Review comments |
| 5. Check | Confirms references exist, flags retractions | Fix and re-verify | The check report |
| 6. Export | Formats references and file | Final read and disclosure statement | The final statement |

### Step 1: Outline with AI

Give the AI your research question, your study design and the journal's section limits. Ask for an IMRaD outline with two or three points per section and a word budget that adds up to the limit.

Example prompt (invented): "Outline a 4,500-word IMRaD paper. Question: is remote work associated with sleep duration in call-center staff? Data: survey of 412 staff, two groups, sleep in hours. Journal limit: 4,500 words excluding references."

Then edit the outline yourself. The argument of the paper is the part reviewers judge, and it should be yours.

### Step 2: Find and read sources

Use AI search to find papers that use different words for your concepts, then screen abstracts. AI summaries are a filter, not a substitute for reading: for the 15 to 30 papers your argument depends on, read the methods and results yourself.

Search more than one source and note what you searched. Our guides on [how to find sources for a research paper](/blog/how-to-find-sources-for-a-research-paper) and [citation snowballing](/blog/citation-snowballing) cover the method.

### Step 3: Draft section by section with cited autocomplete

Draft one section at a time, starting with methods and results, which come straight from your records. Feed the AI your notes, tables and numbers, not just a topic. A draft built from your own material is easier to defend and closer to your voice.

**Cited autocomplete is an editor feature that suggests the next sentence as you type, with a citation to a real paper you can accept, change or ignore.** It keeps you writing while the tool suggests sources, but each suggestion is a lead to check, not a finished citation.

Rules for this step:

- Never let AI write numbers you did not give it. Results come from your analysis.
- Rewrite every AI paragraph so it says exactly what you mean.
- Open each suggested citation. Confirm the paper exists and supports the sentence.

### Step 4: Revise with AI review

Ask an AI reviewer to read the draft as a peer reviewer would: is the gap clear, does the method match the question, are the claims proportionate, are limitations named? Treat the comments like a colleague's: some will be wrong.

Then check individual claims. For each key statement in your introduction and discussion, see whether the studies you cite support it. Our guide on [how to check a claim against the research](/blog/how-to-check-a-claim-against-the-research) explains how.

### Step 5: Check every reference

**A hallucinated citation is a reference produced by an AI tool that points to a paper that does not exist, or attaches real details to the wrong paper.** It is the most common way AI-assisted papers get caught, and editors and reviewers increasingly look for it. See [AI-generated research papers](/blog/ai-generated-research-papers) for reported cases.

1. Confirm each reference resolves: DOI, title, authors, year, journal.
2. Check for retractions and corrections.
3. Confirm the cited paper says what your sentence claims.
4. Replace preprints with the published version where one exists.

### Step 6: Format and export

Apply the journal's reference style, headings and file format (Word or LaTeX). Add the declarations the journal asks for, including the AI statement. Read the final version once in full, start to end.

## What to disclose and how

Disclose in the place your journal specifies. These are the positions we checked in November 2026:

| Publisher or body | Where to disclose | Exempt use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Elsevier | Separate AI declaration section before the references [1] | Basic grammar, spelling and punctuation checks |
| Wiley | Acknowledgments; figure captions for AI-generated or edited visuals [2] | Simple grammar correction and word choice suggestions, typically |
| IEEE | Acknowledgments, naming the AI system and the sections affected [3] | Editing and grammar enhancement: disclosure recommended, not required |
| Springer Nature | Methods section, or a suitable alternative section [4] | AI-assisted copy editing <!-- VERIFY: Springer Nature's guidance pages disagree on whether copy-editing use must be declared; confirm on the journal's own policy page --> |
| ICMJE journals | Cover letter and the manuscript [5] | Not specified |
| APA journals | Most relevant section, often the introduction or method [6] | Check the journal |

### Sample AI disclosure statement

Elsevier publishes a template [1]. Adapted for a typical case:

> During the preparation of this work, the authors used [tool name] to search the literature, suggest an outline and draft early versions of the introduction from the authors' notes. After using this tool, the authors reviewed and edited the content as needed and take full responsibility for the content of the published article.

Name the tool and version or date, say what it did, and say which sections it touched. If you used AI in analysis or to write code, describe that in the methods, as APA and Springer Nature expect [4][6].

## FAQ

### Is it allowed to use AI to write a research paper?

Most major publishers allow AI assistance if a human author stays responsible for the content and the use is disclosed. Elsevier, Wiley, Springer Nature and the ICMJE all say AI cannot be listed as an author. What is not allowed is presenting AI output as your own original work without disclosure, or submitting fabricated data or references. Your university or course may set stricter rules, so check them first.

### How do I disclose AI use in a paper?

Follow your target journal's instructions. Elsevier asks for a separate AI declaration before the references; IEEE asks for it in the acknowledgments; Springer Nature asks for documentation in the methods or a similar section. Name the tool, say what you used it for, and state that you reviewed the output and take responsibility for the content. Basic grammar and spelling help often needs no statement.

### Can AI write my whole research paper?

It can produce a draft, but submitting it as your work would break most journal and university rules, and the draft cannot contain results you did not obtain. AI can't run your study, decide what your data mean or vouch for its own citations. Use it to draft from your own notes and results, then rewrite, check every reference, and disclose the use.

### Do AI writing tools make up citations?

General chatbots can produce references that look real but do not exist, or attach a real title to the wrong authors and year. Tools that search a scholarly index before writing, and link each citation to a real record, reduce this risk but don't remove the need to check. Open every cited paper and confirm it says what your sentence claims.

### Which parts of a paper is AI best at helping with?

AI helps most with searching and screening literature, turning an outline into a first draft, tightening long sentences, drafting an abstract from a finished paper, and checking references. It helps least, and needs most checking, with the discussion, where you interpret your results and compare them with other studies. Keep the methods and results tied exactly to what you did and found.
