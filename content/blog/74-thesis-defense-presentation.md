# How to build a thesis defense presentation, slide by slide

*Last updated: 12 November 2026*

A thesis defense presentation is a 15 to 30 minute talk that tells examiners what you asked, how you studied it, what you found and why it matters. For a 20-minute talk, use about 12 main slides: one idea each, figures over text, and backup slides after the end for hard questions. Rehearse it aloud, timed, at least three times.

This guide is for master's and PhD candidates building their defense slides. For what happens on the day, read the pillar: [thesis defense: what happens and how to prepare](/blog/thesis-defense-guide).

## What is a thesis defense presentation for?

**A thesis defense presentation is the opening talk at a defense, in which the candidate summarizes the research so that examiners and the audience share the same picture before questions begin.** It is not a second copy of the thesis. Your examiners have read the thesis. The talk shows that you can explain it clearly and sets the agenda for the questions that follow.

That changes what goes on the slides. Every slide you show is an invitation to ask about it. Put the strongest parts of your work on screen and keep the rest in the thesis and in backup slides.

## How long is a thesis defense presentation?

Most are 15 to 30 minutes; 20 minutes is the most common request. Check the rule for your program: some fix it exactly. At the University of Helsinki, the opening lecture (the lectio praecursoria) lasts at most 20 minutes [1]. Leiden University asks candidates to prepare a short lay talk for a general audience before the defense [2]. <!-- VERIFY: Leiden lay talk length (10 minutes per 2024 guidance) --> A UK viva often has no presentation at all, though examiners may ask for a five-minute summary.

## How many slides for a thesis defense?

Plan for about one main slide per 90 seconds. That gives 12 to 15 slides for 20 minutes and 18 to 20 for 30 minutes.

| Talk length | Main slides | Backup slides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 minutes (often a master's) | 7 to 8 | 3 to 5 |
| 15 minutes | 10 | 5 |
| 20 minutes | 12 to 15 | 5 to 8 |
| 30 minutes | 18 to 20 | 8 to 10 |

Slide count is a guide, not a rule. A results slide with one chart can take three minutes; a roadmap slide takes 15 seconds.

## The 12-slide thesis defense presentation outline

This outline fits a 20-minute talk. The timings add up to 20 minutes.

| # | Slide | What goes on it | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Title | Thesis title, your name, degree, date, supervisor | 0:30 |
| 2 | The problem | One fact, number or case that shows why the question matters | 1:30 |
| 3 | What we know and the gap | Three to five key studies as a simple diagram or timeline; the gap in one sentence | 2:00 |
| 4 | Research question | The question (and hypotheses, if any), alone on the slide | 1:00 |
| 5 | Design | Design, setting, sample, in a diagram or flowchart | 1:30 |
| 6 | Methods and analysis | Measures or data sources; the analysis in plain words | 1:30 |
| 7 | Result 1 | One chart or table, one-line takeaway as the slide title | 2:30 |
| 8 | Result 2 | Same format | 2:30 |
| 9 | Result 3 or synthesis | Same format, or a summary figure linking results | 2:00 |
| 10 | What it means | Your interpretation, tied back to the gap on slide 3 | 2:00 |
| 11 | Limits and future work | Two or three limits, each with the next study that addresses it | 1:30 |
| 12 | Contribution | One to three contributions, one line each; thank you | 1:30 |
| | **Total** | | **20:00** |

Then add backup slides, numbered B1, B2 and so on, after slide 12.

### Worked example (invented)

A PhD candidate in public health is defending a thesis on heat waves and emergency department visits in three cities, 2015 to 2024. Her slide 2 shows one number: visits for heat-related illness rose by 38% on days above 35°C in the largest city (invented figure). Slide 4 holds the question alone. Slides 7 to 9 each carry one chart: one per city's exposure-response curve, with the slide title stating the finding ("Risk rises sharply above 33°C in all three cities"). Her backup slides hold the model specification, the full coefficient table and a sensitivity analysis excluding 2020, the year an examiner is most likely to ask about.

## What to include in a thesis defense presentation, and what to leave out

Include the parts your examiners need to judge the contribution. Leave out the parts they can read.

| Include | Leave out |
| --- | --- |
| The gap, in one sentence | A full literature review |
| Your question and design | Every variable and every survey item |
| Two or three key results, as figures | All results in tables |
| The limits you would raise yourself | A long acknowledgments slide |
| Your contribution, stated plainly | Paragraphs of text to read aloud |

## How to design thesis defense slides

Design every slide around one idea. Bourne's ten rules for scientific talks make the same points: less is more, make the take-home message persistent, and use visuals sparingly but effectively [3].

1. **Write the takeaway as the slide title.** "Peer tutoring lowered anxiety by 0.6 points" is a title. "Results" is a label.
2. **Use figures, not paragraphs.** One chart per slide. Remove gridlines and legends you don't need; label lines directly.
3. **Keep text short.** No more than about 30 words on a slide, in phrases, not sentences.
4. **Make it readable from the back.** Body text at 24 points or larger, axis labels at 18 points or larger. Keep text contrast at least 4.5:1, the WCAG minimum for normal text [4].
5. **Number your slides.** Examiners can then say "go back to slide 8".
6. **Cite on the slide.** Put a short citation (Author, year) under any figure or claim from another study, and a full references slide at the end.

## How to start a thesis defense presentation

Open with the problem, not with yourself or an agenda. A strong first minute has three moves:

1. Thank the chair and committee in one sentence.
2. Show the problem with one concrete fact, number or case (slide 2).
3. State the question (slide 4) and say how the talk is organized in one sentence.

Learn these first two minutes word for word. Nerves peak at the start, and a fluent opening steadies the rest. End the same way: restate the contribution, say thank you and stop. Don't end on "Any questions?" while the limits slide is on screen; leave the contribution slide up.

## Backup slides: preparing for the hard questions

**Backup slides are extra slides placed after the closing slide and shown only when a question calls for them.** They let you answer with evidence instead of memory. Build one for each of the likely hard questions from your practice list (see [thesis defense questions](/blog/thesis-defense-questions)):

- full model output or regression table;
- sample characteristics by group;
- a sensitivity or robustness analysis;
- a coding example from quote to theme (qualitative work);
- an alternative design you considered and why you rejected it.

In PowerPoint, add a hidden index slide with links to each backup slide, or memorize their numbers so you can type the number and press Enter to jump.

## How to rehearse a thesis defense presentation

1. **Run it alone, aloud, timed.** Cut until it fits with one minute spare.
2. **Record it once.** Watch for filler words and slides you rush.
3. **Give it to peers.** Ask them to note any slide they didn't understand.
4. **Hold a mock defense.** Give the talk, then take 30 minutes of questions from your supervisor and peers.
5. **Rehearse in the room** if you can, with the room's computer, clicker and projector. If the defense is online, rehearse with the same video tool and test presenter view.

Bring the deck as PowerPoint and as PDF on a USB stick, and email a copy to yourself.

## FAQ

### How many slides should a 20-minute thesis defense have?

About 12 to 15 main slides, which works out at roughly 90 seconds per slide. Results slides usually need more time and the title slide less. Put extra detail on backup slides after the closing slide, so you can show them only if an examiner asks. Going over time is a worse mistake than having too few slides.

### What should I include in a thesis defense presentation?

Include the problem and why it matters, the gap, your research question, your design and method, the main results, what they mean, the limits, your contribution and future work. Add a title slide, a short roadmap and a closing slide that restates the contribution. Leave out the full literature review: name only the studies that define the gap.

### How do I start a thesis defense presentation?

Thank the chair in one sentence, then open with the problem, not with yourself or an outline. A concrete fact or case works well: one number or one example that shows why the question matters. Then state your research question on its own slide. Learn the first two minutes word for word, because that is when nerves are worst.

### Should I use PowerPoint or another tool for my thesis defense?

Use whatever your department's room and your examiners can open reliably. PowerPoint is the safest choice for a shared room computer. Bring a PDF copy as a backup, since PDFs keep fonts and layout on any machine. If you present online, test screen sharing and presenter view in the same software the committee will use.

### What are backup slides in a thesis defense?

Backup slides are extra slides placed after your closing slide, shown only if an examiner asks a question they answer. Typical ones are full regression tables, sample characteristics, extra analyses, a sensitivity check or a coding example. They let you give a precise answer with evidence instead of describing a table from memory.
