# AI screenplay writer: what AI can (and shouldn't) do for screenwriters

*Last updated: 6 November 2026*

An AI screenplay writer is most useful as a research assistant, brainstorming partner and notes reader, not as the author. The WGA's agreement says AI output is not literary material, and US copyright protects only human-authored expression. Use AI to research, outline and diagnose your draft, then write the pages yourself.

This article is part of our guide to [screenwriting software](/blog/screenwriting-software). It covers what the contracts and the Copyright Office say, which uses help, which backfire, and a full workflow from idea to draft.

**An AI screenplay writer is any tool that uses a large language model to generate or revise material for a script: loglines, outlines, scenes, dialogue or notes.** The term covers general chatbots, screenwriting apps with AI features and dedicated coverage services.

> This article explains public sources. It is not legal advice. For a specific contract or sale, ask the WGA or an entertainment lawyer.

## What the WGA agreement says about AI

The Writers Guild of America won its first AI terms in the 2023 Minimum Basic Agreement (MBA), after a 148-day strike. The 2026 MBA, ratified by members in April 2026 and running from 2 May 2026 to 1 May 2030, keeps those terms and adds one more. Here is what the WGA's own summary says, in plain terms.

| Rule | What the WGA summary says | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI is not a writer | Neither traditional AI nor generative AI is a writer, so "no written material produced by traditional AI or GAI can be considered literary material." | AI output can't earn writing credit or count as a writer's work under the MBA |
| AI material isn't source material | Unpublished AI material is not "assigned material" or "source material" | A company can't hand you an AI draft and pay you a rewrite fee instead of a writing fee |
| Disclosure | The company must tell the writer if any material it provides was generated by AI | You can ask, and you should be told |
| No forced use | A company "can't require the writer to use AI software (e.g., ChatGPT)" | Using AI is your choice |
| Writer's use needs consent | A writer may use AI if the company consents and the writer follows company policies | Ask before you use AI on a paid job; policies differ by studio |
| Training | The WGA reserves the right to argue that training AI on writers' material is prohibited | This question is still open |
| 2026 addition | When companies license writers' scripts to AI developers to train a commercial AI system, they must give the Guild written notice, including any pay for writers | New notice rule for training licenses |

### What this means if you write on spec

The MBA governs signatory companies and the writers they employ or buy from. A spec writer working alone isn't under a contract yet, so the rules don't stop you using a chatbot at home. They matter the moment you sell or get hired:

- **The buyer will want to know what you wrote.** Literary material and credit are defined around human writers. Text that a model generated is not literary material under the MBA.
- **Company policies apply on assignment.** Once you are hired, you need consent to use AI, and you follow that company's policy.
- **Chain of title matters.** Buyers and their insurers want a clean record of who wrote what. Dated drafts in your own words are the simplest evidence.

The safest working rule for a spec writer: use AI for everything around the pages, and write the pages.

## What the US Copyright Office says about AI authorship

The Copyright Office has published guidance in three steps:

1. **March 2023 registration guidance.** Copyright protects only material a human authored. When you register a work that contains more than a minimal amount of AI-generated material, you must disclose it and describe the human contribution.
2. **January 2025 report, Part 2: Copyrightability.** Using AI to assist creation does not bar protection, and neither does including AI material in a larger human work. Protection extends to human expression, including "creative arrangements or modifications of the output," but not to "the mere provision of prompts."
3. **The courts agree on the basic rule.** In *Thaler v. Perlmutter* (D.C. Circuit, 18 March 2025), the court held that the Copyright Act requires a human author and upheld the refusal to register an image credited solely to an AI system. <!-- VERIFY: reports say the Supreme Court denied review on 2 March 2026; confirm on supremecourt.gov before adding -->

For a screenwriter, the practical reading is simple. Your outline, your scenes and your dialogue are protected because you wrote them. Lines a model wrote and you pasted in unchanged are on weaker ground. A long prompt describing a scene does not make you the author of the scene the model returns.

## Uses that work

These uses keep you as the author and play to what models do well: reading fast, summarizing, listing options and spotting patterns.

| Use | Example prompt | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research | "Summarize how a 1970s newspaper newsroom produced a front page, with sources I can check." | Speeds up background reading; you verify the sources |
| Brainstorming options | "Give me 20 ways a heist could go wrong in act two. One line each." | You pick and rewrite; the list just widens the options |
| Logline testing | "Here is my logline. What genre, protagonist and stakes does a reader assume?" | Tells you what your pitch actually communicates |
| Beat sheet check | "Here is my outline. Where is the midpoint, and what changes there?" | Diagnoses structure without writing scenes |
| Notes on a draft | Coverage prompts with page-cited notes | A fast first read before a human one |
| Continuity | "List every contradiction in names, dates and ages, with pages." | Mechanical checking across 110 pages |
| Dialogue diagnosis | "Which characters sound alike? Quote the lines." | Flags a problem; you fix it in your voice |

For a full set of coverage prompts and a scoring rubric, see [AI script coverage](/blog/ai-script-coverage).

## Uses that backfire

| Use | What goes wrong |
| --- | --- |
| Generating scenes or full drafts | Output converges on familiar beats and flat, explanatory dialogue. Readers notice. |
| "Polishing" your dialogue | The model smooths away the rhythm and oddness that make a character sound like a person. |
| Asking AI for facts without sources | Models invent dates, cases and quotes. A wrong fact in a based-on-a-true-story script is a legal problem, not just an embarrassment. |
| Pasting AI text into your draft | Weakens your authorship claim, and you must be ready to tell a buyer what you wrote. |
| Uploading unproduced scripts without reading the terms | Consumer chatbot plans may train on your chats unless you turn that off. |
| Using AI to imitate a living writer's style | Raises ethical and possibly legal problems, and makes the script sound secondhand. |

## A workflow from idea to draft, with AI at each step

| Stage | You do | AI does | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Idea | Pick the story you want to tell | Lists comparable films so you can check the idea isn't already made | A one-paragraph premise |
| 2. Research | Read the primary sources | Finds and summarizes sources; answers questions about PDFs with page numbers | A research bible ([how to research a screenplay](/blog/how-to-research-a-screenplay)) |
| 3. Logline | Write five versions | Tells you what each one communicates | One logline |
| 4. Treatment | Write the treatment | Flags gaps in cause and effect | A 3 to 10 page treatment |
| 5. Outline | Build the beat sheet | Locates act breaks and flat stretches | A scene-by-scene outline |
| 6. Draft | Write every page in your formatter | Nothing | Draft one |
| 7. Notes | Decide which notes to take | Coverage-style notes with page references | A revision list |
| 8. Rewrite | Rewrite by hand | Continuity check on the new draft | Draft two |

Step 6 is empty on purpose.

### A treatment template you can copy

AI for Research has no built-in treatment template, so here is one in Markdown. Paste it into any editor.

```markdown
# [TITLE]: Treatment

**Writer:** [Name]   **Draft:** [Number, date]
**Format:** Feature / Pilot   **Genre:** [Genre]   **Length:** [Pages]

## Logline
[One sentence: protagonist, goal, obstacle, stakes.]

## Why this story, why now
[Two or three sentences. What draws you to it; any true events it draws on.]

## Main characters
- **[Name], [age]:** [want] / [need] / [flaw]
- **[Name], [age]:** [relationship to protagonist, what they want]

## Story
### Act one
[Present tense, one paragraph per major sequence. End on the act break.]
### Act two
[Midpoint in bold. End on the low point.]
### Act three
[Climax and resolution.]

## Tone and comparable titles
[Two or three comparisons and what you share with each.]

## Research notes
[Key sources, with links. See the research bible.]
```

## FAQ

### Can AI write a screenplay?

AI can generate text in screenplay format, but the result is usually generic, structurally safe and thin on voice. Under the WGA agreement, AI-generated text is not literary material, and the US Copyright Office does not protect material whose expressive elements a machine determined. AI is more useful for research, outlining and notes than for writing the pages.

### Can I sell a script written with AI?

Nothing in the WGA agreement stops a writer using AI with a company's consent, but AI output is not treated as literary material, and buyers will ask what you wrote. US copyright protects only the human-authored parts. If a script contains AI-generated text, talk to an entertainment lawyer or the WGA before selling it. This article is not legal advice.

### What are the WGA rules on AI?

The 2023 WGA MBA says AI is not a writer and AI output is not literary material. A company must disclose AI-generated material it gives a writer, cannot require a writer to use AI, and cannot use AI material to cut a writer's credit or pay. The 2026 MBA keeps these terms and adds notice rules for licensing scripts to AI developers.

### What is the best AI for screenwriting?

It depends on the job. General chatbots such as ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are flexible for brainstorming and notes. Dedicated coverage tools give a coverage report without prompting. A research editor with real citations, such as AI for Research, suits fact-based projects. Keep the script itself in a dedicated formatter.

### Do I have to disclose that I used AI?

Under the WGA MBA, the disclosure duty runs from the company to the writer when the company hands over AI material. A writer using AI needs the company's consent and must follow its policies. For copyright registration, the Copyright Office asks applicants to disclose more than minimal AI-generated content. Check current rules with the WGA or a lawyer.
