# Final Draft alternatives: 9 free and paid options compared

*Last updated: 6 November 2026*

The best Final Draft alternative for most writers is Fade In: about $80 once, free updates, and it opens and saves `.fdx` files. Beat (Mac) and Trelby (Windows, Linux) are the best free ones. WriterDuet and Arc Studio are best for co-writing. All follow standard screenplay format, so your pages look the same.

This is a supporting guide to our [screenwriting software comparison for 2026](/blog/screenwriting-software). Screenwriting software is a program that formats a script into standard screenplay layout as you type: scene headings, action, character cues, dialogue and transitions, each at the right indent, with page breaks handled for you. Every option below does that. They differ on price, file compatibility and how you work with other people.

Prices were checked on each vendor's site in November 2026 and change often. Confirm before you buy.

## Why writers leave Final Draft

Final Draft is good software. It is the format many studios and showrunners expect, version 13 added real-time collaboration, and it includes tracked revisions, comments and the Beat Board. Writers leave for three practical reasons:

1. **Price.** A list price near $250 is a lot for a tool whose core job, standard formatting, is also done by free software. Major version upgrades are paid.
2. **Collaboration limits.** Final Draft 13's real-time collaboration only works with other Final Draft 13 users. A co-writer on an older version, or without a license, cannot join.
3. **Platforms.** The desktop app runs on macOS 12 or later and Windows 11. There is no Linux or Chromebook version, and the iOS app (Final Draft Go) is a separate product.

If none of these bother you, or a job requires Final Draft, stay. If they do, here are the options.

## The 9 alternatives, with pros, cons and `.fdx` support

### 1. Fade In: the best paid alternative

Fade In is the closest thing to Final Draft without its price. You buy it once, and "updates to Fade In are free" for the current version, per its developer. One individual license covers installation on your own Windows, Mac and Linux computers.

- **Pros:** about $79.95 one-time; opens, edits and saves `.fdx`; revisions, locking and production reports; outlining and navigation tools; runs on Linux.
- **Cons:** collaboration, cloud sync and phone access need Fade In Access, a separate subscription; the interface is plainer than newer browser apps.
- **`.fdx`:** full round trip. You can receive an `.fdx` from a Final Draft writer, edit it, and send `.fdx` back.

### 2. WriterDuet: the best for co-writing

WriterDuet was built for two writers in the same document at once, in the browser or its apps.

- **Pros:** real-time co-writing; infinite revision history; works on Chromebooks; free plan with three projects.
- **Cons:** subscription for unlimited projects and full features; the free plan is a trial for anyone with many scripts.
- **`.fdx`:** imports Final Draft files; `.fdx` export is described as a paid feature.

### 3. WriterSolo: free WriterDuet without the cloud

WriterSolo is WriterDuet's free offline app, without the collaboration features. It suits writers who want a modern editor, no subscription and no cloud.

- **Pros:** free; offline; handles `.fdx` and Fountain files.
- **Cons:** no co-writing; fewer extras than the paid WriterDuet plans.

### 4. Highland Pro: the best for Mac, iPad and iPhone

Highland Pro, from Quote-Unquote Apps, writes in Fountain, a plain-text screenplay markup, and syncs across Apple devices through iCloud. It replaced Highland 2.

- **Pros:** native on Mac, iPad and iPhone; plain-text files you can open anywhere; fast, distraction-free writing.
- **Cons:** subscription only; Apple only; no real-time co-writing.
- **`.fdx`:** imports and exports Final Draft files. Features that Fountain does not describe (one older review mentions starred revisions) may not carry over.

### 5. Arc Studio: the best modern all-rounder

Arc Studio combines a clean editor with outlining, a "Stash" for cut material and season outlines on its Pro plan.

- **Pros:** real-time co-writing and comments on all plans; browser plus desktop and Apple mobile apps; works offline.
- **Cons:** the free plan allows two scripts and watermarks PDFs; inviting collaborators needs a paid plan; no native Android app.
- **`.fdx`:** Final Draft import and export on all plans, including free.

### 6. Beat: the best free alternative on a Mac

Beat is a free, open-source (GPL v3) macOS app. Its developer says the desktop app "will remain free forever."

- **Pros:** free; files stay on your device and Beat collects no user data; Fountain files; plugins.
- **Cons:** macOS and iOS only, with no Windows or Linux version planned; no built-in collaboration.
- **`.fdx`:** imports and exports Final Draft files, including notes and revisions on import.

### 7. Trelby: the best free alternative on Windows and Linux

Trelby is free, open-source (GPL) software for Windows and Linux.

- **Pros:** free; offline; correct formatting; exports PDF, Fountain and `.fdx`.
- **Cons:** dated interface; slow development; no Mac version and no collaboration.

### 8. Celtx: for students and small crews

Celtx moved from a free desktop app to a browser studio that ties the script to story development and production planning.

- **Pros:** script plus breakdowns and scheduling; team and education plans.
- **Cons:** the free plan allows one project; extra collaborator seats cost extra on Writer plans.
- **`.fdx`:** imports Final Draft (`.fdx`) files, along with PDF and Fountain. Its help center notes that a text-based PDF is the best import option.

### 9. StudioBinder: if you also produce

StudioBinder is production management software (call sheets, shot lists, schedules) with a free screenwriting tool.

- **Pros:** free screenplay editor; your script flows into breakdowns and schedules; imports `.fdx`, PDF, Word and Fountain.
- **Cons:** the paid plans are priced for production teams, not solo writers; the editor is not its main product.

**Also worth knowing:** KIT Scenarist, once a popular free option, was discontinued in 2023. Its developers now make Story Architect (STARC). <!-- VERIFY: Story Architect pricing and .fdx support at starc.app. -->

## One-time purchase vs. subscription

If you searched for screenwriting software with no subscription, these are your choices:

| No subscription | Subscription |
| --- | --- |
| Fade In (about $79.95, free updates to the current version) | Highland Pro (about $59.99/year) |
| Final Draft 13 perpetual license (about $249.99 list) | Arc Studio (about $69 to $99/year) |
| Beat (free) | WriterDuet paid plans (from about $9.99/month) |
| Trelby (free) | Celtx paid plans |
| WriterSolo (free) | Final Draft Suite (about $99.99/year list) |

The arithmetic: Highland Pro at about $60 a year costs more than Fade In after about 16 months. Final Draft's perpetual license costs roughly the same as two and a half years of the Final Draft Suite at list price. Subscriptions make sense when you get something a one-time app does not give you: real-time co-writing, cloud sync, or a new version every year.

## Moving your scripts: export and import steps

Switching takes an afternoon if you do it in this order:

1. **Export everything from Final Draft before your license or subscription lapses.** For each script, keep the `.fdx` file and save a PDF as your reference copy.
2. **Note your page counts.** Write down the page count of each PDF. You will compare later.
3. **Import one script first.** In Fade In, open the `.fdx` directly. In WriterDuet, Arc Studio, Celtx or StudioBinder, use their import option. In Beat or Highland Pro, open or import the `.fdx`.
4. **Check the parts most likely to break:** the title page, scene numbers, revision colors and asterisks, dual dialogue, script notes and comments, and custom element styles.
5. **Compare against the PDF.** Scroll both side by side for the first 10 pages and spot-check the rest. If the page count moved by more than a page or two, look at how the new tool handles (MORE) and (CONT'D) and scene heading spacing.
6. **Then move the rest.** Keep the original `.fdx` files in an archive folder. A plain `.fdx` is your insurance if you switch tools again.

**Tip:** If an import goes badly, try Fountain or a text-based PDF as the bridge. Fountain is plain text and is read by Highland Pro, Beat, Trelby, WriterSolo, Celtx and others.

## Where to keep everything else: research, notes and the series bible

A formatter holds the script. It is not a good home for the treatment, the series bible, the research behind a fact-based story, or the coverage and notes a draft collects. Most writers end up with those spread across Google Docs, email and a downloads folder.

AI for Research is a web editor for that development work. It **does not format screenplays and does not open `.fdx` files**, so keep your script in whichever alternative you chose. Use AI for Research beside it for:

- **Treatments and bibles** with real-time co-editing, comments, suggestions (tracked changes) and version history. It opens `.docx` files and keeps their styles, and exports Word and PDF.
- **Research with sources:** search 250M+ scholarly works and type `@` to cite one.
- **Your PDFs in the Library:** upload source documents, or your exported script PDF, and use Ask on one PDF at a time. Answers carry page numbers you can check.

## FAQ

### What is the best free alternative to Final Draft?

On a Mac, Beat: it is free, open source, and imports and exports .fdx files including notes and revisions. On Windows or Linux, Trelby is free and exports .fdx, and WriterSolo is a free offline app from the makers of WriterDuet. If you need to co-write, WriterDuet's free plan (three projects) and Arc Studio's free plan (two scripts, watermarked PDFs) work in a browser.

### Which Final Draft alternative is a one-time purchase?

Fade In is the main paid option you buy once: about $79.95 as of November 2026, with free updates to the current version and one license covering your own Windows, Mac and Linux computers. Trelby, Beat and WriterSolo are free with no subscription. Highland Pro, Arc Studio, WriterDuet's paid plans and Celtx are subscriptions.

### Can other screenwriting programs open Final Draft files?

Yes. Fade In opens, edits and saves .fdx files. Beat imports and exports them, Highland Pro imports and exports them, WriterDuet and Arc Studio import them, and Celtx and StudioBinder import them. Trelby exports .fdx. After any import, check the title page, scene numbers, revision marks and dual dialogue, because features outside the basic screenplay elements do not always survive.

### Is Final Draft worth it in 2026?

It is worth it if a studio, showrunner or production you work with requires Final Draft, or if you co-write live with other Final Draft 13 users. Otherwise most writers can get the same standard formatting and .fdx output from Fade In for about a third of Final Draft's list price, or from a free tool.

### Will switching from Final Draft change my page count?

Usually by little or nothing, because all these programs follow the same standard layout: 12-point Courier, a 1.5-inch left margin and fixed indents for each element. Small differences in how each program breaks pages, handles (MORE) and (CONT'D), or spaces scene headings can move a page break. Compare the page count of your PDF before and after the move.
