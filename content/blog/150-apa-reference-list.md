# References in APA: how to format an APA 7 reference list

*Last updated: 14 November 2026*

To format references in APA 7, start a new page titled **References** (bold, centered), list every source you cite in the text, and order the entries alphabetically by the first author's surname. Double-space the list, give each entry a 0.5-inch hanging indent, and end each entry with its DOI written as an https://doi.org/ link. Each entry has four parts: author, date, title and source [1].

This is the pillar of our citation styles series. It covers APA in depth. If you need to compare APA with Vancouver, IEEE and Harvard, read [how to format a reference list](/blog/how-to-format-a-reference-list) first. For in-text citations by number of authors, see [how to cite two or three authors in APA](/blog/how-to-cite-two-or-three-authors-apa).

## What does an APA reference entry contain?

**Every APA reference answers four questions in a fixed order: who (author), when (date), what (title) and where (source).** Each part ends with a period [1][2].

Here is the house example, the 1953 paper that described the structure of DNA:

> Watson, J. D., & Crick, F. H. C. (1953). Molecular structure of nucleic acids: A structure for deoxyribose nucleic acid. *Nature*, *171*(4356), 737–738. https://doi.org/10.1038/171737a0

| Part | In the example | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Author | Watson, J. D., & Crick, F. H. C. | Surname, then initials with periods and a space; ampersand before the last author |
| Date | (1953). | Year in parentheses, then a period |
| Title | Molecular structure of nucleic acids: A structure for deoxyribose nucleic acid. | Sentence case: capitalize the first word, the first word after a colon, and proper nouns |
| Source | *Nature*, *171*(4356), 737–738. https://doi.org/10.1038/171737a0 | Journal name in title case and italics; volume italic; issue in parentheses, not italic; page range with an en dash; DOI as a link |

## How do you set up the APA reference page?

**Put the references on a new page after the text, title it References in bold and centered, double-space everything, and use a 0.5-inch hanging indent** [1][3].

- **Title:** "References", not "Bibliography" or "Works Cited". Bold, centered, at the top of a new page.
- **Spacing:** double-spaced within and between entries. No extra blank lines between entries.
- **Hanging indent:** the first line of each entry sits at the left margin; every following line is indented 0.5 inch (1.27 cm).
- **Font:** the same font as the rest of the paper.
- **What goes in:** only works you cited in the text. Personal communications (emails, interviews you did not record, private chats) are cited in the text only and do not appear in the list [1].

To set a hanging indent in Word, select the list, open **Paragraph**, choose **Special: Hanging** and set **By: 0.5"**. In Google Docs, use **Format > Align & indent > Indentation options > Special indent: Hanging**. Never fake the indent with tabs or line breaks.

## How do you order an APA reference list?

**Alphabetize by the first author's surname, letter by letter, and remember that nothing comes before something: "Brown, J." precedes "Browning, A."** [1][4]. The table shows each tie-breaking rule with real works.

| Situation | Rule | Correct order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Same first author, one alone and one with coauthors | The single-author work comes first | Kahneman, D. (2011) before Kahneman, D., & Tversky, A. (1979) |
| Same authors, same order, different years | Earliest year first | Tversky, A., & Kahneman, D. (1974) before Tversky, A., & Kahneman, D. (1981) |
| Same first author, different second authors | Alphabetize by the second author's surname | Kahneman, D., & Tversky, A. comes after any "Kahneman, D., & Frederick, S." entry |
| Same authors, same year | Order by title, add a, b, c to the year | (2020a), (2020b) |
| No date and dated works by the same author | n.d. first, then dated works oldest to newest, then "in press" | (n.d.), (2015), (2019), (in press) |
| Group author | Alphabetize by the first significant word of the full name, never the abbreviation | "American Psychological Association", not "APA" |
| No author | Alphabetize by the first significant word of the title, ignoring A, An, The | *The Elements of Style* files under "E" |

The two Kahneman and Tversky papers are a common trap. They have the same two authors in a different order, so they are different author groups: "Kahneman, D., & Tversky, A. (1979)" files under K, and "Tversky, A., & Kahneman, D. (1974)" files under T.

If your list is long, the [reference list sorter](/tools/reference-list-sorter) applies these rules and flags duplicates.

## How many authors do you list in an APA reference?

**List up to 20 authors; for 21 or more, list the first 19, an ellipsis, then the final author** [1][2].

| Number of authors | Reference list format |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Cohen, J. (1992). |
| 2 | Braun, V., & Clarke, V. (2006). |
| 3 to 20 | Simmons, J. P., Nelson, L. D., & Simonsohn, U. (2011). |
| 21 or more | First 19 names, . . . , last author (no ampersand) |

The 2016 paper on the FAIR data principles has 53 authors, so the entry shows the first 19 and the last:

> Wilkinson, M. D., Dumontier, M., Aalbersberg, I. J., Appleton, G., Axton, M., Baak, A., Blomberg, N., Boiten, J.-W., da Silva Santos, L. B., Bourne, P. E., Bouwman, J., Brookes, A. J., Clark, T., Crosas, M., Dillo, I., Dumon, O., Edmunds, S., Evelo, C. T., Finkers, R., . . . Mons, B. (2016). The FAIR Guiding Principles for scientific data management and stewardship. *Scientific Data*, *3*, Article 160018. https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2016.18 <!-- VERIFY: Crossref records issue 1 for this volume; the journal page shows volume 3 and article 160018 only. Decide whether to print *3*(1) -->

Never write "et al." in an APA reference list. It belongs in the text only. Our [et al. guide](/blog/et-al-in-citations) compares how other styles shorten long author lists.

## APA reference examples by source type

Each example below is a real, checkable work. The general pattern is shown first.

### Journal article

Pattern: Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of article. *Journal Name*, *volume*(issue), pages. https://doi.org/xxxx

> Kahneman, D., & Tversky, A. (1979). Prospect theory: An analysis of decision under risk. *Econometrica*, *47*(2), 263–291. https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185

For an online journal that uses article numbers instead of pages, write "Article" and the number:

> Ioannidis, J. P. A. (2005). Why most published research findings are false. *PLoS Medicine*, *2*(8), Article e124. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.0020124

| Incorrect | Correct | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision Under Risk. | Prospect theory: An analysis of decision under risk. | Article titles use sentence case |
| *Econometrica*, 47(2) | *Econometrica*, *47*(2) | The volume is italic too |
| doi:10.2307/1914185 | https://doi.org/10.2307/1914185 | APA 7 writes DOIs as links |
| Retrieved from https://doi.org/... | https://doi.org/... | No "Retrieved from" |

### Book

Pattern: Author, A. A. (Year). *Title of book* (ed.). Publisher. DOI if it has one

> Kahneman, D. (2011). *Thinking, fast and slow*. Farrar, Straus and Giroux.

> Creswell, J. W., & Creswell, J. D. (2018). *Research design: Qualitative, quantitative, and mixed methods approaches* (5th ed.). SAGE.

APA 7 dropped the publisher's city. Give the edition in parentheses after the title, not italic. When the author and the publisher are the same organization, leave the publisher out:

> American Psychological Association. (2020). *Publication manual of the American Psychological Association* (7th ed.). https://doi.org/10.1037/0000165-000

### Chapter in an edited book

Pattern: Author, A. A. (Year). Title of chapter. In E. E. Editor (Ed.), *Title of book* (pp. xx–xx). Publisher.

> Tajfel, H., & Turner, J. C. (1979). An integrative theory of intergroup conflict. In W. G. Austin & S. Worchel (Eds.), *The social psychology of intergroup relations* (pp. 33–47). Brooks/Cole. <!-- VERIFY: page range 33–47 and publisher name as printed (Brooks/Cole) -->

Editors' initials come before their surnames, the chapter title is not italic, and the book title is. Use "(Ed.)" for one editor and "(Eds.)" for two or more.

### Report from an organization

Pattern: Organization. (Year). *Title of report* (Report No. if any). Publisher if different. URL or DOI

> National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine. (2019). *Reproducibility and replicability in science*. The National Academies Press. https://doi.org/10.17226/25303

The publisher appears here because it differs from the author's name. 
### Web page and blog post

Pattern for a web page: Author or Organization. (Year, Month Day). *Title of page*. Site Name. URL

Pattern for a blog post: Author, A. A. (Year, Month Day). Title of post. *Blog Name*. URL

APA treats a blog post like an article, so the post title is plain and the blog name is italic:

> McAdoo, T., Denneny, S., & Lee, C. (2025, September 9). Citing generative AI in APA Style: Part 1—Reference formats. *APA Style*. https://apastyle.apa.org/blog/cite-generative-ai-references <!-- VERIFY: exact post title punctuation and date on apastyle.apa.org -->

For a web page, the page title is italic and the site name is plain. If the author and the site name are the same, drop the site name. Add a retrieval date only when the page is designed to change and is not archived, such as a dictionary entry: "Retrieved November 14, 2026, from https://..." [1].

### YouTube video

Pattern: Uploader Name [Username]. (Year, Month Day). *Title of video* [Video]. YouTube. URL

> Harvard University. (2019, August 28). *Soft robotic gripper for jellyfish* [Video]. YouTube. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=guRoWTYfxMs <!-- VERIFY: this is APA's own sample; confirm the date and video ID on apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/references/examples/youtube-references -->

The person or channel that uploaded the video is the author. Add the username in square brackets only when it differs from the real name. For a quote, give a timestamp in the text instead of a page: (Harvard University, 2019, 0:45).

### ChatGPT and other generative AI

**In September 2025 APA replaced its 2023 format: the company is the author, and the reference points to a specific chat with a shareable link** [5].

Pattern: Company. (Year, Month Day). *Title of chat* [Generative AI chat]. Tool Name. URL

> OpenAI. (2025, August 21). *High school grammar concepts* [Generative AI chat]. ChatGPT. https://chatgpt.com/share/68a77b60-0ee4-800c-9acc-cd3fd573c311 <!-- VERIFY: this example is quoted from secondary guides summarizing McAdoo et al. (2025); check it against the APA post before publishing -->

In the text: (OpenAI, 2025). Older guides still show the 2023 form "OpenAI. (2023). *ChatGPT* (Mar 14 version) [Large language model]." That format is now retired, so check which one your instructor expects. If you cannot share the chat, APA's guidance on unrecoverable sources applies; your course may ask you to paste the prompt and output in an appendix instead.

## What if there is no author or no date?

**Move the title into the author position when there is no author, and write (n.d.) when there is no date** [1][6].

| Missing | Reference format | In-text citation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Date | Author. (n.d.). *Title*. Source. URL | (Author, n.d.) |
| Author | *Title of work*. (Year). Source. URL | (*Title of Work*, Year) |
| Author and date | *Title of work*. (n.d.). Source. URL | (*Title of Work*, n.d.) |
| Title | [Description of the work]. (Year). Source. URL | ([Description], Year) |

Three checks before you use these rules:

1. **Look for a group author.** A page on a government or university site with no person's name is usually authored by that organization. Use the organization as author.
2. **Look for a date.** A "last updated" date counts if it applies to the content you used. A copyright date in the site footer does not.
3. **Use "Anonymous" only if the work is signed "Anonymous".**

In the text, a title used in place of an author takes title case; it is italic if the title is italic in the reference, or in quotation marks if it is not.

## How do you format DOIs and URLs in APA 7?

**Write every DOI as https://doi.org/ followed by the DOI, with no period after it and no label in front** [1].

| Old or wrong form | APA 7 form |
| --- | --- |
| doi:10.1037/0033-2909.112.1.155 | https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.112.1.155 |
| DOI: 10.1037/0033-2909.112.1.155. | https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.112.1.155 |
| http://dx.doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.112.1.155 | https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.112.1.155 |
| Retrieved from https://www.example.org/page | https://www.example.org/page |

Include a DOI whenever the work has one, even for print copies. If there is no DOI and you read the work online, add the URL, except for academic databases where a URL would not work for other readers. Live links or plain text are both acceptable; keep them consistent. To find a missing DOI, search the title on Crossref or paste it into the [APA citation generator](/tools/citation-generator/apa).

## Common mistakes in APA references

- **Title case article titles.** Only the journal name takes title case. Article, book and chapter titles use sentence case.
- **Missing italics on the volume number.** *Nature*, *171*(4356): volume italic, issue plain.
- **"et al." in the list.** List up to 20 authors.
- **Publisher locations.** "New York, NY: Penguin" was APA 6; APA 7 writes "Penguin".
- **Entries for uncited works.** The list must match the text. Run the [citation coverage checker](/tools/citation-coverage-checker) before you submit.
- **Mixed DOI formats.** Copying from databases gives you a mix of doi:, DOI: and http links.
- **Reordered authors.** The author order on the paper is fixed.
- **Fake references.** References written by a chatbot without a search can look perfect and not exist. Check that each DOI resolves to the paper you cite.

## FAQ

### How do you format references in APA 7?

Start the list on a new page titled References, bold and centered. Double-space every entry and use a 0.5-inch hanging indent. Order entries alphabetically by the first author's surname. Each entry gives the author, the date in parentheses, the title and the source, such as the journal with its volume, issue and pages, followed by the DOI as an https://doi.org/ link when the work has one.

### How many authors do you list in an APA reference?

APA 7 lists up to 20 authors in a reference entry. Use an ampersand before the last name. For 21 or more authors, list the first 19, then an ellipsis, then the final author's name, with no ampersand. This changed from APA 6, which listed up to seven. In the text, any work with three or more authors is cited with the first author and et al.

### What do you do when an APA source has no author or no date?

With no author, move the title into the author position and alphabetize by its first significant word, ignoring A, An and The. With no date, write (n.d.) in place of the year. Before deciding a work has no author, check for a group author: most web pages without a person's name are written by the organization that publishes them, which then becomes the author.

### Does APA 7 still use 'Retrieved from' before URLs?

No. In APA 7 the DOI or URL goes at the end of the entry with no label, and DOIs are written as https://doi.org/ links. "Retrieved from" is gone, except for pages that are designed to change, such as a dictionary entry or a live data page. For those, write "Retrieved Month Day, Year, from" followed by the URL.

### How do you cite ChatGPT or another AI tool in an APA reference list?

APA updated its guidance in September 2025. The company that made the tool is the author, the date of the chat is the date, the chat's title is italicized with the label [Generative AI chat], then the tool name and a shareable link to the chat. Many instructors also want the prompt described in your text. Check your course rules, because some ban or limit AI use.
