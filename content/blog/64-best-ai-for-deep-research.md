# The best AI for deep research on scholarly questions

*Last updated: 12 November 2026*

The best AI for deep research depends on what your question needs. For questions answered by published studies, use a tool that searches a scholarly index and filters by study type, such as Consensus or AI for Research. For questions that also need policy, statistics or news, ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity cover the web better. Check every citation either way.

This guide is for students, researchers and analysts choosing an AI tool for research. It belongs to our series on [what deep research AI is and when to trust it](/blog/what-is-deep-research-ai), and it is the hub for our tool comparisons: [Consensus](/blog/consensus-ai-review), [SciSpace](/blog/scispace-review) and the citation-map tools [Connected Papers, ResearchRabbit and Litmaps](/blog/connected-papers-vs-researchrabbit-vs-litmaps). For a wider list of AI tools for researchers beyond deep research, see [AI literature review tools](/blog/ai-literature-review-tools).

## What makes an AI good for deep research?

**A good deep research tool reads the right sources, cites them so you can check each claim, lets you narrow the search, and exports what you need to keep.** Judge tools on these five criteria before you look at any brand.

| Criterion | What to ask | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Sources | Does it search a scholarly index, the web, or both? Can you add your own PDFs? | Every claim is only as good as what was searched |
| 2. Citations you can open | Does each claim link to a specific source? Is it the paper, or a page about it? | You must confirm the source says what the sentence says |
| 3. Full text | Does it read full texts or only abstracts? Does it tell you which? | Methods, numbers and limits are in the full text |
| 4. Filters | Can you limit by year, study type, peer review, field? | Lets you ask for trials or reviews, not opinion pieces |
| 5. Export | Can you get the report (Word, PDF, Docs) and the references (RIS, BibTeX)? | You need the papers in your reference manager, not just a report |

Add two practical criteria: **price and limits** (how many runs a month you get) and **what happens next** (can you move from the report to writing, reviewing or a reference library without copying links by hand).

**A scholarly index is a database of records of academic works (title, authors, journal, year, DOI, abstract) that you search directly, as opposed to web pages ranked by a search engine.** Tools built on one return papers; web agents return pages.

### ChatGPT deep research

**Good at:** long, well-structured reports that mix papers with policy documents and data pages, and control over which websites it uses. You can restrict a run to listed domains or prioritize them, connect apps with read-only access, and download the report as Markdown, Word or PDF [2]. OpenAI says runs take 5 to 30 minutes [1].

**Limits for scholarly work:** it searches the web, so paywalled papers are usually read as abstracts, and there are no study-type filters. OpenAI's pricing page lists deep research on every plan but gives no exact counts there [3]. Full guide: [ChatGPT deep research for academic work](/blog/chatgpt-deep-research-for-academic-work).

### Gemini deep research

**Good at:** speed, breadth and planning. It shows a research plan you can edit before it runs, browses hundreds of sites, and exports to Google Docs [4]. It can also read your uploads and Drive files, so PDFs you got through your library can be part of the run. It is free for signed-in adults, with higher limits on paid plans [4][5].

**Limits for scholarly work:** the same web-search limits as ChatGPT, and no reference export. Full guide: [Gemini deep research for a literature search](/blog/gemini-deep-research-for-literature-search).

### Perplexity deep research

**Good at:** fast answers with sources shown up front. Perplexity says its Research mode runs dozens of searches, reads hundreds of sources and finishes most tasks in under 3 minutes, and reports export to PDF, a document or a shareable Page [6]. Its developer documentation describes an academic filter that prioritizes scholarly databases and journals [9].

**Limits for scholarly work:** the free plan includes one Research query a month and Pro limits are described as "monthly limits (average use)", not numbers [7]. Results still come from web search ranking.

### Claude Research

**Good at:** research that needs your own working documents. Claude runs several searches that build on each other and can use Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Docs and other connectors you enable [8]. Useful if your notes, drafts and supervisor emails live in Google Workspace.

**Limits for scholarly work:** paid plans only (Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise) [8][11]. It searches the web, not a scholarly index, and Anthropic's help page does not describe export or run time.

### Consensus

**Good at:** answering yes-or-no research questions from papers only. Consensus searches academic papers (it says 200M+ in one place and 400M+ in another) drawn from Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex and its own crawl [10][12]. Its Consensus Meter labels the top 20 relevant results "yes", "no" or "possibly" for a yes-or-no question [10]. Deep Search is its deep research mode: 3 a month free, 15 on Pro, 200 on Deep [12].

**Limits:** it does not include web or policy sources, and the meter counts studies without weighing design. Full review: [Consensus AI](/blog/consensus-ai-review).

## Other AI tools for researchers worth knowing

- **Elicit** searches 138M+ papers and is strongest for structured screening and extraction tables; Pro is $49 a month per user, or $588 a year [13].
- **SciSpace** combines search with reading and chat with PDF; see our [SciSpace review](/blog/scispace-review).
- **Citation-map tools** build a graph from a seed paper instead of answering a question; see [Connected Papers vs ResearchRabbit vs Litmaps](/blog/connected-papers-vs-researchrabbit-vs-litmaps).

## Which AI is best for academic research?

**For academic research, choose a tool that searches a scholarly index, filters by study type and exports references; use a web agent only to orient.** Match the tool to the job:

| If you need to... | Start with | Then |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Get oriented in an unfamiliar topic in 10 minutes | Gemini or Perplexity | Note key terms and reviews |
| Write a background section mixing papers, policy and data | ChatGPT deep research, restricted to chosen sites | Check every claim; cite the original papers |
| Answer "does X affect Y?" from studies | Consensus or AI for Research | Read the strongest studies yourself |
| Build a screening and extraction table | Elicit | Check a sample of extractions |
| Work from your own emails, Docs and notes | Claude Research | Trace claims back to papers |
| Draft, review and cite in one place | AI for Research | Run a citation check before you submit |
| Run a systematic review search | None of them alone | Search databases through your library; follow PRISMA 2020 [14] |

## Is Perplexity better than ChatGPT for research?

**Perplexity is faster; ChatGPT gives more control over sources and export.** Perplexity says most Research tasks finish in under 3 minutes [6]; OpenAI says ChatGPT takes 5 to 30 [1]. ChatGPT lets you restrict a run to listed sites and download Word or PDF [2]. For scholarly questions both share the same weakness, web ranking rather than a paper index, so the choice matters less than your checks.

## How to choose in four steps

1. **Write your question and the three papers you already know matter.** If you know none, start with a web agent to find them.
2. **Run the same question in two tools** on the same day: one web agent, one scholarly tool.
3. **Score each report** on the five criteria: known papers found, share of peer-reviewed sources, ten claims checked against their sources, full text versus abstract, and how easily the references export.
4. **Pick the tool that scored best on your field's questions,** not the one with the longest report. Recheck every few months; these tools change quickly.

## FAQ

### Which AI is best for research?

For questions answered by published studies, pick a tool that searches a scholarly index, such as Consensus, Elicit or AI for Research, because every source is a paper you can cite. For questions that need policy documents, statistics or news alongside papers, ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity deep research cover the open web better. Many researchers use one of each.

### Which AI is best for academic research?

The best AI for academic research searches a scholarly index, lets you filter by year and study type, links each claim to a paper you can open, and exports references to your reference manager. General assistants are good for orientation but read the open web and often only abstracts of paywalled papers. Use a scholarly tool for anything you will cite.

### Is Perplexity better than ChatGPT for research?

Perplexity is faster: it says most deep research tasks finish in under 3 minutes, against OpenAI's 5 to 30 minutes for ChatGPT, and its free plan includes one Research query a month. ChatGPT lets you restrict a run to chosen websites and download the report as Word or PDF. For academic sources, neither replaces a scholarly index, so check every citation.

### Is Claude good for deep research?

Claude's Research mode runs several searches that build on each other and can use connected Google Workspace and other apps, which is useful if your notes and drafts live there. It is only on paid plans (Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise) as of November 2026. Like the other general assistants, it searches the web, not a scholarly database.

### Can I use free AI tools for deep research?

Yes, with low limits. Gemini deep research is free for signed-in adults, ChatGPT lists limited deep research on its free plan, Perplexity's free plan includes one Research query a month, and Consensus's free plan includes three Deep Searches a month. AI for Research's free plan has 45 credits a month, enough for three Deep reports or dozens of Summary answers.
