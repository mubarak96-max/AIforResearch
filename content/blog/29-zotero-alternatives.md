# Best Zotero alternatives: reference managers that help you write

*Last updated: 11 November 2026*

The best Zotero alternatives in 2026 are Mendeley for free cloud storage, Paperpile for Google Docs, ReadCube Papers for reading with AI, EndNote for institutions, JabRef for open-source LaTeX work and Citavi for knowledge organization. If your real problem is the gap between your library and your draft, keep Zotero and import it into a writing tool.

**A reference manager is software that stores the sources you collect, keeps their PDFs and notes, and inserts formatted citations and bibliographies into your writing.** Zotero is the default free choice for good reasons. This guide is for when it isn't working for you. It is part of our series on [citations that point to real sources](/blog/ai-citation-generator).

## Why people look for a Zotero alternative

The complaints that show up in forum threads, including the Reddit ones behind "reddit zotero alternative" searches, fall into four groups:

1. **Word plugin friction.** Citation fields break when documents pass between co-authors, and long documents get slow to refresh. Zotero 8's new unified citation dialog addressed part of this. [8]
2. **Storage limits.** The free 300 MB of file storage fills with a few hundred PDFs; paid plans run from $20 to $120 a year. [9]
3. **No built-in AI.** Zotero doesn't summarize papers, answer questions about PDFs or suggest sources. Plugins fill some of that gap.
4. **Collaboration outside academia.** Zotero groups work well among Zotero users. Editors, clients and colleagues who don't use Zotero can't easily work with the citations in your draft.

Match the alternative to the problem. A different reference manager fixes storage or plugin issues. It won't fix the gap between your sources and the people you write with.

## The alternatives

### Mendeley

Free, with 2 GB of cloud storage, a PDF reader, private groups and the Mendeley Cite add-in for Word. [1] Elsevier has owned it since 2013, and the original Mendeley Desktop stopped being available to download on 1 September 2022; new users get Mendeley Reference Manager. [10] Pick it for the storage and simplicity. Skip it if you need Google Docs or LibreOffice support, or prefer an open tool. Full comparison: [Zotero vs. Mendeley](/blog/zotero-vs-mendeley).

### EndNote

Clarivate's EndNote is the long-standing institutional choice. EndNote 2025 is sold as a one-time license (about $275 full, $150 student, $125 upgrade, US list prices). [2] It handles very large libraries and complex reference types well, and Cite While You Write in Word is mature. It is expensive if you pay yourself, so check whether your employer or university provides it.

### Paperpile

Paperpile is a browser-based manager built around Google Docs, with a Word plugin too. It is subscription-only, billed annually: Regular about $8.30/month and Expert about $11.50/month, with 50% off for academics and nonprofits, and a 30-day trial. [3] Both plans include unlimited PDF storage. Expert adds full-text search, annotations and shared libraries. Pick it if you live in Google Docs.

### ReadCube Papers

ReadCube Papers centers on reading: a good PDF viewer, annotation, and AI chat with PDFs (with daily limits on Essentials and more on Plus, which can chat with up to 20 articles at once). SmartCite inserts citations in Word and Google Docs in 10,000+ styles. [4] Essentials costs about $10/month or $95/year and Plus about $20/month or $185/year, with 40% off for academics and a 30-day Plus trial.

### JabRef

JabRef is a free, open-source reference manager that stores your library as a BibTeX or BibLaTeX file. [5] That makes it the natural pick for LaTeX writers and anyone who wants a plain-text library under version control. It also integrates with Word and LibreOffice. JabRef 6.0 pre-releases added opt-in AI chat and summaries for entries with linked PDFs, with links back to page locations. ### Citavi

Citavi, now from Lumivero, combines reference management with knowledge organization: quotations, categories, outlines and a task planner around each project. It comes as Citavi Web and Citavi Desktop, with a Word add-in, and has added AI key insights, passage summaries and AI paper search. [7] Prices aren't listed publicly, and many universities in Germany, Austria and Switzerland provide site licenses. <!-- VERIFY: Citavi Desktop platform support (historically Windows only) and whether the DACH site-license claim holds --> Pick it if you organize ideas and quotes as much as references.

### AI for Research

AI for Research is not a standalone reference manager. It is a web editor with a Library built in, so it closes the gap between sources and draft:

- **Import instead of rebuilding.** Paste a Zotero API key, or sign in with Mendeley, and your library comes into the AI for Research Library. Import is on the **Plus plan and up**.
- **Cite with `@`.** Type `@` and the menu searches your Library and 250M+ scholarly works together.
- **Ask your PDFs.** Use Ask on one PDF at a time; answers carry page numbers. AI Chat beside the document can use your whole Library as a source and cites the papers it draws on.
- **Write with cited autocomplete,** with sources you can limit to a Library folder, a year range or a minimum citation count.
- **Review and share.** Claim checks and source-quality flags (retracted papers, preprints, wrong DOIs) appear as comments. Co-editors don't need a reference manager.
- **Styles and export.** 10,000+ CSL styles; Word, PDF and LaTeX export (LaTeX on Plus and up).

## Self-hosted and open-source options

If the reason you're leaving is data control, your options are:

| Option | What you control | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Zotero with WebDAV** | Attachments sync to your own WebDAV server; the library database is local | Metadata still syncs through zotero.org if you use sync |
| **JabRef with a BibTeX file** | Everything: the library is a text file you store wherever you like, such as a private Git repository | No built-in cloud sync or web library |

**An open-source reference manager is one whose code is published under a license that lets anyone read, modify and redistribute it.** Zotero (AGPL) and JabRef qualify. Mendeley, EndNote, Paperpile, ReadCube, Citavi and AI for Research do not.

## Keep Zotero and add a writing layer

Most people who search for a Zotero alternative don't dislike Zotero. They dislike the step between Zotero and the draft. If that's you, don't migrate. Keep Zotero as the place you collect and organize, and add a writing tool that reads from it.

How that works in AI for Research:

1. **Create a Zotero API key** in your Zotero account settings with read access to your library.
2. **In AI for Research, open the Library, add from Zotero and paste the key.** (Plus plan and up.)
3. **Pick what to import:** the whole Zotero library or one collection. A collection arrives as a Library collection with the same name, and you can ask AI for Research to look for the PDFs.
4. **Write.** Type `@` in any paragraph and pick from your imported items or from 250M+ scholarly works.
5. **Switch styles** for the whole document when you're done, and export to Word or PDF.
6. **Keep collecting in Zotero** and import again when you add sources. Items already in your Library are skipped, so a re-import doesn't create duplicates.

## FAQ

### What is the best free alternative to Zotero?

For a free, open-source desktop tool, JabRef is the strongest alternative, especially if you write in LaTeX, because it stores your library as a plain BibTeX file. Mendeley is free with 2 GB of cloud storage but is owned by Elsevier and is not open source. ZoteroBib is free for one-off bibliographies without an account.

### Is there a self-hosted Zotero alternative?

JabRef keeps your library as a local BibTeX file you can store anywhere, including a private Git repository or shared drive. Zotero itself can sync attachments to your own WebDAV server.

### What is a good Mendeley alternative?

Zotero is the most common Mendeley alternative: free, open source, and it can import a Mendeley library directly. Paperpile suits people who write in Google Docs, ReadCube Papers suits people who read and annotate heavily, and EndNote suits institutions that license it. Export a backup from Mendeley in RIS or BibTeX before you switch.

### Which reference manager has AI features?

Several now do. ReadCube Papers includes AI chat with PDFs, Citavi offers AI summaries and paper search, and JabRef has added opt-in AI chat for entries with PDFs in its 6.0 releases. AI for Research adds AI to the writing side: autocomplete that cites papers, PDF questions answered with page numbers, and claim checks on your draft.

### Do I have to stop using Zotero to use AI for Research?

No. On the Plus plan and up, you paste a Zotero API key and AI for Research imports your library into its Library. You keep collecting sources in Zotero and cite them while you write in AI for Research by typing @. The free plan doesn't include Zotero or Mendeley import.
