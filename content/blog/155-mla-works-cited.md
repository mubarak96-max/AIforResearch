# MLA works cited and in-text citations: the MLA 9 guide with examples

*Last updated: 14 November 2026*

MLA citations come in two parts: a short in-text citation with the author's last name and a page number, such as (Watson and Crick 737), and a full entry on the Works Cited page at the end of the paper. Each Works Cited entry is built from MLA 9's nine core elements: author, title of source, title of container, other contributors, version, number, publisher, publication date and location [1]. The list is alphabetical and uses a half-inch hanging indent.

This guide covers the citations. For the page layout (heading, header, margins, the Works Cited page title), see [MLA format](/blog/mla-format). For how list layouts compare across styles, see [how to format a reference list](/blog/how-to-format-a-reference-list); this guide does not repeat it.

## What are the MLA core elements?

**The core elements are nine pieces of information, always in the same order, that make up every MLA 9 entry.** You use only the ones that apply to your source and skip the rest [1], [2].

| # | Element | Punctuation after it | Example (Watson and Crick) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Author | Period | Watson, J. D., and F. H. C. Crick. |
| 2 | Title of source | Period | "Molecular Structure of Nucleic Acids: A Structure for Deoxyribose Nucleic Acid." |
| 3 | Title of container | Comma | *Nature*, |
| 4 | Other contributors | Comma | (none) |
| 5 | Version | Comma | (none) |
| 6 | Number | Comma | vol. 171, no. 4356, |
| 7 | Publisher | Comma | (omitted: not needed for journals) |
| 8 | Publication date | Comma | 1953, |
| 9 | Location | Period | pp. 737–38, https://doi.org/10.1038/171737a0. |

Put together, that gives the full entry [3]:

> Watson, J. D., and F. H. C. Crick. "Molecular Structure of Nucleic Acids: A Structure for Deoxyribose Nucleic Acid." *Nature*, vol. 171, no. 4356, 1953, pp. 737–38, https://doi.org/10.1038/171737a0.

### Rules for each element

- **Author.** One author: Last, First. Two: Last, First, and First Last. Three or more: Last, First, et al. Reverse only the first name. Use the name as the source gives it (initials if the source uses initials).
- **Title of source.** Title case. Quotation marks for a part of a larger work (article, chapter, web page, episode, song); italics for a self-contained work (book, film, website, journal).
- **Title of container.** The larger whole that holds the source: a journal, a book anthology, a website, a streaming service. Italics.
- **Other contributors.** People other than the author, introduced by their role: edited by, translated by, directed by, performance by.
- **Version.** An edition (2nd ed., expanded ed.) or a version (director's cut).
- **Number.** Volume and issue (vol. 171, no. 4356), or season and episode.
- **Publisher.** The organization chiefly responsible for producing the work. Omit it for journals and magazines, for a website whose name is essentially the publisher's name, and for sites that host but do not produce the work, such as YouTube or a database [1]. Write "UP" for University Press (Oxford UP) and drop business words such as Inc. and Company.
- **Publication date.** As fully as the source gives it, day month year: 23 Apr. 2005. Abbreviate months longer than four letters (Jan., Feb., Mar., Apr., Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov., Dec.); write May, June and July in full.
- **Location.** Page numbers (p. 12, pp. 737–38), a DOI (preferred, as https://doi.org/...), or a URL. MLA recommends dropping http:// or https:// from URLs but keeping the full DOI link [1]. <!-- VERIFY: MLA 9 wording on omitting http:// and https:// from URLs -->

## What is a container in MLA?

**A container is the larger work that holds your source, such as the journal an article appears in or the website a page sits on.** Some sources have two containers: an article (source) in a journal (container 1) that you read in a database (container 2) [1], [2].

Elements 3 to 9 repeat for each container. A second container follows the first, after a period:

```text
Author. "Title of Source." Container 1, Other contributors, Version,
     Number, Publisher, Date, Location. Container 2, Other
     contributors, Version, Number, Publisher, Date, Location.
```

| Source | Container 1 | Container 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Journal article read on the publisher's site | The journal | (none) |
| Journal article read in JSTOR or ProQuest | The journal | The database |
| Chapter in an edited book | The book | (none) |
| TV episode on a streaming service | The series | The service |
| Web page | The website | (none) |

## How do in-text citations work in MLA?

**An MLA in-text citation gives the author's last name and the page number in parentheses, with no comma: (Watson and Crick 737).** It points to the Works Cited entry that starts with the same name [1], [4].

| Case | Parenthetical | Narrative (name in your sentence) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One author | (Watson 54) | Watson describes ... (54). |
| Two authors | (Watson and Crick 737) | Watson and Crick suggest ... (737). |
| Three or more | (Kolb et al. 2005) | Kolb et al. call ... (2005). |
| No author | ("Formatting") | The MLA Style Center's "Formatting a Research Paper" advises ... |
| Two works by one author | (Watson, *Double Helix* 54) | In *The Double Helix*, Watson ... (54). |
| No page numbers | (Watson and Crick) | Watson and Crick suggest ... |
| Video or audio | ("Me at the Zoo" 00:00:05–00:00:10) | In "Me at the Zoo," ... (00:00:05–00:00:10). |
| Two sources at once | (Watson 54; Skloot 2) | |

The sources are the real works listed in the examples below; the page numbers for the two books are illustrative. Kolb et al. is a chemistry paper also used in our [ACS citation format](/blog/acs-citation-format) guide, and 2005 is a page inside its range.

### Correct and incorrect in-text citations

| Correct | Incorrect | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (Watson and Crick 737) | (Watson and Crick, 737) | No comma between author and page |
| (Watson and Crick 737) | (Watson & Crick, 1953, p. 737) | MLA uses "and", no year, no "p." |
| "... (D.N.A.)" (Watson and Crick 737). | "... (D.N.A.)." (Watson and Crick 737) | The period goes after the parentheses in a run-in quote |
| (Kolb et al. 2005) | (Kolb, Finn, and Sharpless 2005) | Three or more authors: first name and et al. |

For a whole list of rules on "et al." across styles, see [et al. in citations](/blog/et-al-in-citations).

## MLA works cited examples for common sources

Every example below is a real, checkable source. In your paper, the parts shown in *italics* here are italic, and every entry has a half-inch hanging indent.

### Journal article

> Watson, J. D., and F. H. C. Crick. "Molecular Structure of Nucleic Acids: A Structure for Deoxyribose Nucleic Acid." *Nature*, vol. 171, no. 4356, 1953, pp. 737–38, https://doi.org/10.1038/171737a0.

Three or more authors:

> Kolb, Hartmuth C., et al. "Click Chemistry: Diverse Chemical Function from a Few Good Reactions." *Angewandte Chemie International Edition*, vol. 40, no. 11, 2001, pp. 2004–21, https://doi.org/10.1002/1521-3773(20010601)40:11<2004::AID-ANIE2004>3.0.CO;2-5.

MLA shortens a page range to the last two digits of the second number when the first digits are the same (737–38, 2004–21).

### Book

> Watson, James D. *The Double Helix: A Personal Account of the Discovery of the Structure of DNA*. Atheneum, 1968.

> Skloot, Rebecca. *The Immortal Life of Henrietta Lacks*. Crown, 2010.

With a translator (other contributor):

> Homer. *The Odyssey*. Translated by Emily Wilson, W. W. Norton, 2017.

### Chapter in an edited book

> Denzin, Norman K., and Yvonna S. Lincoln. "Introduction: The Discipline and Practice of Qualitative Research." *The SAGE Handbook of Qualitative Research*, edited by Norman K. Denzin and Yvonna S. Lincoln, 5th ed., SAGE, 2018, pp. 1–26. <!-- VERIFY: page range of the introduction in the 5th edition (2018) -->

The chapter is the source (in quotation marks); the book is the container (in italics), followed by the editors, edition, publisher, date and pages.

### Web page

With no author and no date (the title moves to the front, and an access date is added):

> "Formatting a Research Paper." *MLA Style Center*, Modern Language Association of America, style.mla.org/formatting-papers/. Accessed 14 Nov. 2026.

The publisher is kept here because it differs from the website's name. When the site name and publisher are essentially the same, omit the publisher.

### Film

> *Casablanca*. Directed by Michael Curtiz, Warner Bros., 1942.

If your paper focuses on the director, start with the director instead: Curtiz, Michael, director. *Casablanca*. Warner Bros., 1942.

### YouTube video

> "Me at the Zoo." *YouTube*, uploaded by jawed, 23 Apr. 2005, www.youtube.com/watch?v=jNQXAC9IVRw.

YouTube is the container and is not a publisher, so no publisher appears. Use "uploaded by" with the account name when the uploader may not be the creator; when you know the creator, you may start with that person as author.

### Social media post

For a short post with no title, use the full text of the post as the title, in quotation marks, exactly as written:

> Dorsey, Jack [@jack]. "just setting up my twttr." *Twitter*, 21 Mar. 2006, twitter.com/jack/status/20.

<!-- VERIFY: MLA 9 name-and-handle format, and whether MLA now advises "X" rather than "Twitter" as the container for posts accessed after the 2023 rename -->

### AI-generated text

MLA does not treat an AI tool as an author. Describe the prompt as the title, name the tool as the container, then the version, the company, the date the text was generated and the URL [5]:

> "Explain why Watson and Crick's 1953 letter proposed a double helix" prompt. *ChatGPT*, [version used], OpenAI, 14 Nov. 2026, chatgpt.com.

*Example (illustrative): the prompt is ours, and you replace "[version used]" with the version label the tool shows.* In the text, cite a shortened prompt: ("Explain why"). MLA also asks you to say in your paper how you used the tool, and to check and cite the real sources behind any claim it makes [5]. Your course policy on AI use comes first.

### Correct and incorrect entries

| Correct | Incorrect | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Watson, J. D., and F. H. C. Crick. | Watson, J. D., and Crick, F. H. C. | Reverse only the first author |
| *Nature*, vol. 171, no. 4356, 1953, | *Nature* 171.4356 (1953): | MLA 9 uses labels, not the MLA 7 pattern |
| Oxford UP | Oxford University Press, Inc. | Use UP; drop business words |
| 23 Apr. 2005 | April 23, 2005 | Day month year, abbreviated month |
| https://doi.org/10.1038/171737a0 | doi:10.1038/171737a0 | Give the DOI as a link |
| "Me at the Zoo." *YouTube*, uploaded by jawed, | jawed. "Me at the Zoo." *YouTube*, YouTube, | YouTube is not the publisher |

## How to cite a source with no author or no date

**No author: start the entry with the title. No date: leave the date out and add an access date at the end.** Do not write "Anonymous", "Unknown" or "n.d." [1].

| Missing | Works Cited | In text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Author | Start with the title: "Formatting a Research Paper." *MLA Style Center*, ... | ("Formatting") |
| Date | Omit it; end with Accessed 14 Nov. 2026. | No change |
| Page numbers | Omit the location's page part; give the DOI or URL | (Watson and Crick) |
| Author and date | Title first, access date last | Short title only |

When the author is an organization that is also the publisher, MLA says to skip the author and list the organization as publisher, so the entry starts with the title [1]. <!-- VERIFY: MLA 9 rule on organization as both author and publisher -->

## How to alphabetize a works cited list

**Alphabetize letter by letter by the first word of each entry, usually the first author's last name, and ignore A, An and The at the start of a title** [1], [4].

1. **By last name.** Crick comes before Watson.
2. **Titles that start the entry** sort by the first important word: "The Odyssey" files under O.
3. **Same author, several works.** Give the name in the first entry only. In the rest, write three hyphens and a period (---.), and order the works by title.
4. **Single author before co-authored.** "Watson, James D." comes before "Watson, J. D., and F. H. C. Crick."

Ordered example (titles in italics in your paper):

```text
"Formatting a Research Paper." MLA Style Center, ...
Homer. The Odyssey. Translated by Emily Wilson, ...
Skloot, Rebecca. The Immortal Life of Henrietta Lacks. Crown, 2010.
Watson, James D. The Double Helix: A Personal Account ...
Watson, J. D., and F. H. C. Crick. "Molecular Structure of ...
```

## FAQ

### What are the nine core elements in MLA?

The MLA 9 core elements are author, title of source, title of container, other contributors, version, number, publisher, publication date and location, in that order. You include only the elements that apply to your source. Put a period after the author and the title of the source, commas between the container elements, and a period at the end of each container.

### How do you do an in-text citation in MLA?

Put the author's last name and the page number in parentheses, with no comma between them: (Watson and Crick 737). If you name the author in your sentence, give only the page: Watson and Crick write that ... (737). For three or more authors, use the first name and et al. If there is no page number, give only the author.

### How do you cite a source with no author in MLA?

Start the works cited entry with the title of the source and alphabetize it by the first important word, ignoring A, An and The. In the text, use a shortened form of the title in place of the author, in quotation marks or italics to match the entry, for example ("Formatting"). Do not write Anonymous or Unknown unless the source itself says so.

### Do you need an access date in MLA 9?

An access date is optional in MLA 9. Add one when a web source has no publication date or is likely to change, and put it at the end of the entry: Accessed 14 Nov. 2026. You do not need it for journal articles with a DOI or for print books, because those versions are fixed and easy to find again.

### How do you alphabetize a works cited list?

Sort entries letter by letter by the first word of each entry, which is usually the first author's last name. Ignore A, An and The at the start of a title. For several works by the same author, give the name in the first entry only, use three hyphens and a period in later ones, and order those works by title.

### How do you cite ChatGPT in MLA?

MLA does not treat an AI tool as an author. Start with a description of your prompt in quotation marks, then the tool's name in italics as the container, the version, the company, the date the text was generated and the URL. In the text, use a shortened form of the prompt description. Also say in your paper how you used the tool.
