# QuillBot alternatives for academic writing: 5 options that keep your meaning and citations

*Last updated: 12 November 2026*

The best QuillBot alternative for academic writing depends on the job. Wordtune gives natural rewrites of one sentence, Grammarly fixes grammar and tone everywhere you type, Paperpal edits academic English in Word and Overleaf, and AI for Research rewrites cited passages without detaching the citations. Whichever you use, check every paraphrase against its source.

This guide is for students and researchers who use QuillBot AI, or are deciding whether to, and want a paraphrasing tool for academic writing. It is part of our series on [the best AI for deep research](/blog/best-ai-for-deep-research) and writing tools; for drafting tools beyond paraphrasing, see [the best AI for writing papers](/blog/best-ai-for-writing-papers).

## What does QuillBot do well?

**QuillBot is an AI writing tool best known for its Paraphraser, which rewrites a sentence or paragraph in a chosen mode while keeping its meaning.** It is quick, simple and widely used by students.

What it does well:

- **Fast rewording.** Paste a sentence, pick a mode, get a new version in seconds.
- **Named modes.** The free plan includes Standard and Fluency; Premium adds modes such as Formal, Academic, Simple, Expand, Shorten and Custom [1]. <!-- VERIFY: current mode list on QuillBot's paraphrasing tool page -->
- **Help for second-language writers.** If you know what you want to say, Fluency can smooth the grammar of a sentence you wrote.
- **A free plan.** Free users can paraphrase a limited number of words at a time; QuillBot's pages give 125 in one place and 100 in another [1], [2]. <!-- VERIFY: free word limit on quillbot.com; its pages blocked our automated checker -->

QuillBot also offers a grammar checker, summarizer and citation generator as separate tools [1]. Premium pricing varies by region and billing period [2]. <!-- VERIFY: QuillBot Premium price on quillbot.com/premium -->

## Is QuillBot good for academic writing?

**QuillBot is good for rewording your own sentences and weaker for rewording sources, because it doesn't know what your citations say.** That gap causes two problems in academic work.

### Problem 1: no sources

QuillBot rewrites text; it doesn't find, add or check references. If the sentence you paste is wrong, the paraphrase is wrong too, only smoother.

### Problem 2: citation drift

**Citation drift is when a rewritten sentence no longer says what its cited source says, even though the citation is still attached.** It happens when a paraphrase strengthens a hedge, swaps a technical term for a looser synonym, or merges two claims under one citation.

*Example (invented, for illustration).* The source says:

> In a cohort of 1,240 first-year students, those sleeping under 6 hours had lower exam scores (adjusted mean difference −3.1 points, 95% CI −4.9 to −1.3) (Lee, 2023).

| Version | Text | Problem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drifted | Lack of sleep causes students to fail exams (Lee, 2023). | "Causes" from an observational cohort; "fail" is not what was measured |
| Drifted | Students who sleep less score significantly lower in every subject (Lee, 2023). | "Every subject" is invented; "significantly" adds a claim |
| Faithful | First-year students who slept less than 6 hours scored about 3 points lower on exams than those who slept more (Lee, 2023). | Same population, measure, size and hedge |

Synonym-based rewording makes drift more likely: "associated with" becomes "linked to", then "leads to". Each step reads naturally, and the last one is wrong.

### Wordtune

**Good at:** offering several natural-sounding rewrites of one sentence, so you choose rather than accept. The free Basic plan includes 10 rewrites and AI suggestions a day and unlimited spelling and grammar checks; Advanced gives 30 rewrites a day for $6.99 a month or $4.89 billed annually; Unlimited is $9.99 a month or $6.99 billed annually [3]. It runs as a Chrome and Edge extension and an iOS app [3].

**For academic work:** good for a clumsy sentence in your own argument. It doesn't handle sources, so keep it away from sentences that report a study's findings, or check them closely.

### Grammarly

**Good at:** catching errors across every app you write in. Free covers spelling, grammar and 100 AI prompts a month; Pro, at $12 a month, adds full-sentence rewrites, tone adjustment, citation consistency checks and plagiarism checking [4]. Its Citation Finder, inside Grammarly's own docs editor, flags claims that may need a source and inserts citations in APA, MLA or Chicago [5].

**For academic work:** the best general proofreader on this list. Its rewrites are sentence-level, which limits drift.

### Paperpal

**Good at:** academic English specifically. Its tools include a paraphraser, sentence rewriter, AI proofreader, reference checker and submission readiness checker, and it works in Microsoft Word through an add-in and in Google Docs and Overleaf through its browser extension [7]. Free gives 200 language suggestions a month and 5 generative AI uses a day; Prime removes most caps [6].

**For academic work:** the closest like-for-like academic QuillBot alternative, especially if you write in Word or LaTeX. Confirm prices on Paperpal's site; the figures we found are from its publisher's blog, updated January 2025 [6].

### AI for Research

**Good at:** rewriting passages that contain citations. The Paraphrase tool in Writing Studio rewrites a passage in your own natural style, keeping its meaning and citations, at three strengths: Light (keeps the structure), Standard and Strong (new sentence structure). The Text Humanizer rewrites stiff or generic text with varied sentences and plain words, keeping your facts and citations. Details and costs are below.

**For academic work:** built for the citation drift problem, though no tool removes the need to read the result against the source.

## Is paraphrasing with AI plagiarism? The rules

**Paraphrasing a source without citing it is plagiarism; paraphrasing with a citation is normal practice, whether you reword it by hand or with AI.** APA Style treats a paraphrase as restating another author's idea in your own words and requires a citation; page numbers are encouraged but not required [8].

AI adds two duties:

- **Accuracy.** You are responsible for what the paraphrase says. The ICMJE recommendations say authors must review AI output, which may be incorrect or incomplete, and must make sure there is no plagiarism in AI-assisted text [9].
- **Disclosure.** Many courses and journals require you to say you used AI. Follow the written policy; see [AI in academic research policies](/blog/ai-in-academic-research-policies).

Rewording your own draft is editing, not plagiarism. Rewording another author's sentence until it no longer matches, without citing them, is still plagiarism, even if every word changed.

### How to paraphrase a source correctly, with or without AI

1. **Understand it first.** Read the passage and the surrounding paragraph until you can explain it aloud.
2. **Write your version** from your notes, not by editing the original sentence.
3. **If you use a tool, paste your version**, not the source's, and ask it to improve clarity.
4. **Compare with the source:** population, measure, numbers, direction and hedging must all match.
5. **Quote what you can't reword.** Distinctive phrases or defined terms go in quotation marks.
6. **Cite**, with a page number where it helps.
7. **Log the AI use** if your course or journal asks for disclosure.

## FAQ

### Is QuillBot good for academic writing?

QuillBot is good for rewording a sentence you wrote yourself, especially if English is not your first language. It is weaker on academic sources: it doesn't find or check references, and a paraphrase can change a technical term, drop a hedge such as 'may', or separate a claim from its citation. Read every rewrite against the original before you keep it.

### Is paraphrasing with AI plagiarism?

Paraphrasing someone else's idea without citing them is plagiarism, whether you reword it yourself or with AI. Paraphrasing with a citation is normal academic practice. Using AI adds two duties: check the paraphrase still says what the source says, and follow your course or journal's rules on disclosing AI use. Rewording your own draft is not plagiarism.

### What is the best free QuillBot alternative?

For quick sentence rewrites, Wordtune's free Basic plan gives 10 rewrites a day. For grammar and clarity everywhere you type, Grammarly Free. For academic English with a Word add-in, Paperpal's free plan. For rewriting a cited passage while keeping citations in place, AI for Research's free paraphrasing tool, which allows 3 runs a day without an account.

### Is Wordtune better than QuillBot?

Wordtune is better if you want a few natural-sounding options for one sentence at a time, inside the browser. QuillBot is better if you want named modes, such as Fluency or Formal, applied to a whole paragraph. For academic work neither checks sources, so the better choice is the one whose rewrites you change least when you compare them to your original.

### How do I paraphrase a source correctly in a paper?

Read the passage until you understand it, close it, and write the idea in your own words and sentence structure. Then compare with the original: the meaning, numbers and hedging must match, and no distinctive phrase should be copied without quotation marks. Add the in-text citation and, in APA, a page number if it helps the reader find the passage.
