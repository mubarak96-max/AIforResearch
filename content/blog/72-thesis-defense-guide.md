# Thesis defense: what happens, how long it lasts and how to prepare

*Last updated: 12 November 2026*

A thesis defense is an oral examination where you present your research and answer examiners' questions about it. Most last one to three hours, begin with a short talk, and end with a private decision. The usual result is a pass with corrections. Prepare by rereading your thesis, predicting questions chapter by chapter, and rehearsing aloud at least twice.

This guide is for master's and PhD candidates in the final months before their defense, and for supervisors who want one page to send their students. It is the pillar of our thesis defense series, which also covers [the questions examiners ask](/blog/thesis-defense-questions) and [how to build the presentation](/blog/thesis-defense-presentation).

## What is a thesis defense?

**A thesis defense is an oral examination in which a degree candidate explains and justifies their thesis to a panel of examiners, who then decide whether it meets the standard for the degree.** In the US it is often called a dissertation defense for doctoral degrees. In the UK and much of the Commonwealth it is called a viva voce, or simply a viva. British English spells the word "defence"; this guide uses the US spelling.

The defense has two jobs. First, it checks that you did the work and understand it. Second, it tests your judgment: can you explain why you made each choice, what the results do and do not show, and where the field should go next?

## Who is in the room at a thesis defense?

The people present depend on the country. In the US you face your own committee. In the UK you face two examiners, one of them from another university. In Finland, Sweden and the Netherlands you face an opponent or opposition committee in front of a public audience.

| | US dissertation defense | UK and Commonwealth viva | Nordic public defense (Finland, Sweden) | Dutch public defense |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who examines | Your committee, usually 3 to 5 faculty, including your advisor | An internal and an external examiner; some universities add an independent chair [3] | An opponent from outside the university, chaired by a custos (Finland) or a chair (Sweden) [5][6][8] | An opposition committee proposed by your supervisor and appointed by the dean [7] |
| Audience | Varies: often an open talk, then closed questions | Usually private | Public; anyone may attend [5] | Public, often livestreamed [7] |
| Length | Commonly 1 to 3 hours <!-- VERIFY: no single national rule; check program handbook --> | At Cambridge, "rarely less than 2hrs" [3] | Helsinki: talk up to 20 minutes, opponent up to 4 hours, whole event up to 6 hours [5] | Leiden: 45 minutes, ended by the beadle saying "Hora est" [7] |
| Thesis approved beforehand? | Partly: the committee must accept the draft as ready to defend [1] | No: the viva is part of the examination | Yes: a preliminary examination grants permission to defend [6] | Yes: the doctorate committee approves the manuscript first [7] |
| Typical outcomes | Pass, pass with revisions, fail | Award, minor corrections, major corrections, resubmit, lower degree, fail [4] | Accept or reject; some faculties grade [6] | Degree awarded at the ceremony |

### The US committee defense

You present for 20 to 45 minutes, then each committee member questions you in turn. Your advisor sits on the committee. At the University of Texas at Austin, a passing report "confirms the committee's unanimous approval" of the dissertation, and any required revisions must be completed to the committee's satisfaction [1]. Other schools allow a pass with one dissenting vote [2]. Many US programs ask you to send the full dissertation to the committee several weeks before; Texas asks for at least four weeks [1].

### The UK viva

The viva is usually a closed conversation between you and two examiners. There is often no formal presentation, though some examiners ask you to summarize the thesis in five minutes. The examiners write reports, and the result is confirmed afterwards by a degree committee [3]. Bristol lists eight possible outcomes, from an unconditional award to a fail, with 28 days for minor corrections, normally six months for major corrections and normally twelve months for a resubmission [4].

### The Nordic and Dutch public defense

**An opponent is an examiner, usually from another university, who leads a public, structured debate with the candidate about the thesis.** At Helsinki you give a lecture of at most 20 minutes (the lectio praecursoria), the opponent questions you, and then the audience may ask questions through the custos [5]. In the Netherlands the ceremony is short and formal: at Leiden the defense runs 45 minutes, you are supported by two paranymphs, and you may submit up to twelve propositions alongside the thesis [7]. Because the thesis is approved before the event, the public defense is closer to a ritual than a test, though the questions are real.

## How long is a thesis defense?

A thesis defense usually takes one to three hours, including deliberation. The extremes are a fixed 45 minutes at Leiden [7] and up to six hours for the whole event at Helsinki [5]. A master's defense is usually shorter: 30 to 60 minutes is common. <!-- VERIFY: master's defense length varies by program -->

A typical US doctoral defense, as an example:

| Part | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Your presentation | 30 minutes |
| Committee questions | 60 to 75 minutes |
| Deliberation (you leave the room) | 10 to 20 minutes |
| Result and next steps | 5 minutes |
| **Total** | **about 2 hours** |

## What happens at a thesis defense, step by step?

Most defenses follow the same order, even when the format differs.

1. **Opening.** The chair introduces you and the examiners and states the rules.
2. **Your talk.** A summary of the problem, method, findings and contribution. See [thesis defense presentation](/blog/thesis-defense-presentation) for a 12-slide structure.
3. **Questions.** Examiners usually start broad ("Summarize your contribution in two minutes") and then go chapter by chapter. Expect questions about why you chose your method, what you would change, and how your results fit the literature.
4. **Audience questions**, at public defenses.
5. **Deliberation.** You leave while the examiners decide.
6. **Result.** You are told the outcome and, if there are corrections, how long you have to make them.

## Can you fail a thesis defense?

Yes, but it is uncommon. Supervisors and committees rarely schedule a defense unless they believe the thesis is close to the standard, and at Kent State, for example, the defense is not held if more than one committee member judges the dissertation not in acceptable final form. <!-- VERIFY: Kent State catalog wording, https://catalog.kent.edu/academic-policies/dissertation-doctoral-degree/ -->

**A pass with corrections is a decision that the thesis meets the standard once you make the changes the examiners list, within a set time.** It is the most common outcome. Treat a list of minor corrections as a pass.

Failure risk rises when:

- the thesis claims more than the data show;
- you cannot explain your own analysis (a common problem when someone else ran it);
- you ignored a major study the examiner knows well;
- you went to the defense against your supervisor's advice.

## How to prepare for a thesis defense: a six-week plan

Six weeks is enough for most candidates working alongside other duties. Shorten it if your defense date is fixed sooner.

1. **Week 6: reread the whole thesis.** Read it as an examiner would, in one or two sittings. Keep a list of typos, unclear passages and claims you would now soften. Bring a short errata sheet if your university allows one.
2. **Week 5: write a one-page summary per chapter.** For each chapter: the question it answers, the main finding, the weakest point. These pages become your revision notes.
3. **Week 5: check what is new.** Search for papers published since you finished your literature review. An examiner may ask about a study that appeared last month.
4. **Week 4: predict the questions.** Write 30 likely questions, grouped by chapter. Use [our list of thesis defense questions](/blog/thesis-defense-questions) as a start. Draft a short answer to each, with the page number where the thesis supports it.
5. **Week 3: build the slides.** Twelve slides for a 20-minute talk is a good rule. Put figures, not paragraphs, on the slides.
6. **Week 2: hold a mock defense.** Ask your supervisor and two peers to question you for an hour. Record it. Note every question you answered poorly and rewrite the answer.
7. **Week 1: rehearse and rest.** Run the talk aloud twice, timed. Check the room, the projector and any video link. Sleep normally the night before.

### Worked example (invented)

Amira is defending a master's thesis on a 12-week peer tutoring program and math anxiety in 9th grade, with 64 students in two schools. Her weakest point, found in week 5, is that the comparison group came from a different school. She prepares a two-sentence answer: the schools were matched on intake scores, and she reports this limit on page 41. She also prepares a backup slide showing the baseline scores (mean 3.1 and 3.0 on a 5-point scale). When the question comes, she answers in 40 seconds and moves on.

## What is the difference between a master's and a PhD defense?

The PhD defense tests an original contribution; the master's defense tests competent research.

| | Master's defense | PhD defense |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main test | Did you carry out sound research and understand it? | Did you add something new, and can you argue for it as a peer? |
| Examiners | Often your supervisor and one or two faculty members | Committee or examiners, often including someone external |
| Length | Often 30 to 60 minutes | Often 1 to 3 hours, longer in some countries |
| Typical hard question | "Why did you use this test?" | "What does your thesis change in the field?" |
| Outcome | Pass, pass with revisions, fail | Same, plus resubmission or a lower degree in the UK [4] |

## What should you wear to a thesis defense?

Dress as you would for a job interview unless your university says otherwise. Public defenses in Finland are formal: Helsinki's guidance lists a dark suit, a tailcoat or a long-sleeved black dress among common choices [5]. Wear comfortable shoes: you may stand for 30 minutes or more.

## FAQ

### What happens at a thesis defense?

You usually give a short talk on your research, then examiners question you on your choices: why this question, why this method, what the results mean and what the limits are. The examiners then deliberate without you and announce a result, most often a pass with corrections. The format differs by country: a committee defense in the US, a private viva in the UK, a public debate with an opponent in the Nordic countries.

### How long does a thesis defense last?

It depends on the country and the degree. A US dissertation defense commonly runs one to three hours. A UK PhD viva rarely lasts less than two hours at Cambridge, and can run longer. A Dutch defense at Leiden is fixed at 45 minutes. At Helsinki, the opponent may question you for up to four hours. A master's defense is usually shorter, often under an hour.

### Can you fail a thesis defense?

Yes, but outright failure is rare, because supervisors and committees rarely let a thesis reach the defense unless it is close to the standard. The common results are a pass with minor or major corrections. In the UK, examiners can also ask you to revise and resubmit, or offer a lower degree. Dutch and Nordic theses are approved before the public defense, so failing there is very unusual.

### What should I wear to a thesis defense?

Wear what you would wear to a job interview in your field, unless your university sets a dress code. Most US and UK defenses expect business or smart casual clothes. Nordic and Dutch public defenses are more formal: the University of Helsinki, for example, lists a dark suit, a tailcoat or a black dress among common choices and asks candidates to avoid hats and prominent jewelry.

### What is the difference between a master's and a PhD defense?

A PhD defense tests whether you made an original contribution to knowledge and can argue for it as a peer. A master's defense tests whether you can carry out and explain a sound piece of research. PhD defenses are longer, use external examiners more often, and probe the contribution and future work. Master's defenses focus on method, findings and whether you understand your own analysis.
