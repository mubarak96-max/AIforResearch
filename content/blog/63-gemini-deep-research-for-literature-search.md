# Gemini deep research for a literature search: what it finds and what to check

*Last updated: 12 November 2026*

Gemini deep research is Google's mode that writes a research plan, browses many web sources and writes a cited report, usually in about 5 to 10 minutes. It is free with limits. For a literature search it is fast and broad, but it reads the open web, not a scholarly database, so paywalled papers are often read as abstracts and there are no study-type filters.

This guide is for students and researchers who want to know whether Google's deep research can start a literature search. It is part of our series on [what deep research AI is and when to trust it](/blog/what-is-deep-research-ai). Facts about Gemini come from Google's own pages, checked November 2026.

## What is Gemini deep research?

**Gemini deep research is an agent mode in the Gemini app that breaks a request into steps, searches and browses sources, analyzes what it finds and writes a report.** Google says it can browse hundreds of websites in one session [2].

Documented behavior, checked November 2026 [1][2]:

- **Plan first.** It shows a research plan that you can review and change before it starts.
- **Sources.** Google Search is the default. You can add Gmail and Drive (when Google Workspace is connected), uploaded files and NotebookLM notebooks. You can turn off Google Search to limit the run to your own sources.
- **Time.** Google says a report "usually takes about 5-10 minutes", longer for complex ones. You can leave and get a notification.
- **Output.** A report in the Canvas panel, which you can share, export to Google Docs, copy, or turn into an Audio Overview.

## What Gemini deep research does well

- **Breadth for the time.** It reads far more pages than you would skim in ten minutes, and it is among the faster deep research modes.
- **An editable plan.** Seeing and changing the plan before the run is the best protection against a report that answers a different question. Few tools offer this.
- **Your own files as sources.** You can upload PDFs or point it at Drive, so papers you already have through your library can be read in full.
- **Google Docs export.** The report lands in Docs with its links, ready to annotate and share with a supervisor.
- **Free access.** Anyone 18 or older who is signed in can try it [1].

## Is Gemini deep research free?

**Yes, with limits that Google does not print as exact numbers.** As checked in November 2026 [1][3][4]:

| Plan | Deep research access |
| --- | --- |
| No paid plan | Available to signed-in users 18+; uses the Thinking model; may be unavailable at times of high demand |
| Google AI Plus | Available; overall usage limits 2x the standard limits |
| Google AI Pro | Available; higher report limits; can use the Pro model for higher-quality reports; overall limits 4x standard |
| Google AI Ultra | Available; highest limits; reports can include charts, diagrams and interactive simulations (not when Gmail or Drive sources are included) |

Google rates deep research as a "High" usage-cost feature, so each report uses more of your allowance than a normal prompt [3]. Prices vary by country; see Google's subscriptions page [4].

## Can Gemini deep research read paywalled papers?

**No, not unless you give it the paper.** Google's help page lists its sources as Google Search, your Workspace content, uploads and NotebookLM [1]. It does not describe access to subscription journals. For a closed article, a web agent usually sees the publisher's landing page: title, abstract and perhaps a figure.

That matters because the abstract rarely has the numbers you need: sample size, effect size, confidence interval, the comparison group. A report sentence such as "the intervention improved outcomes" may rest on one line of an abstract.

The fix is simple: download the key PDFs through your library and upload them, or put them in a Drive folder, and run the report on those sources as well as the web.

## Where it falls short for a literature search

A literature search has needs that general deep research does not meet:

- **No study-type filters.** You cannot ask for randomized trials only, or exclude opinion pieces, except by wording the prompt.
- **No scholarly index.** Results come from web search ranking, so press releases and blogs compete with papers.
- **Not reproducible.** You cannot report or rerun the exact searches in a methods section.
- **Thin reference details.** Citations are links. You still need full references with DOIs.
- **No retraction check.** Nothing in the documented workflow flags retracted papers or preprints.

## A test on one question: the method

To judge it on your own topic, run a structured test. Here is one you can do in about an hour, using the same question as our [ChatGPT deep research](/blog/chatgpt-deep-research-for-academic-work) guide so the two can be compared. *The question and known papers are real; results are left for you to record.*

**Question:** "Does practice testing improve long-term retention compared with rereading in university students? Summarize the evidence from meta-analyses and experiments, with study types and effect sizes."

**Known papers** any good search should find: Dunlosky et al. (2013) [8], Rowland (2014) [9] and Adesope, Trevisan and Sundararajan (2017) [10].

1. Start the run and, before confirming, read the plan. Add a step: "List each source's study type and say whether you read the full text or only the abstract." Note what you changed.
2. Record the date, your plan and the time to finish.
3. Export to Google Docs.
4. Sort the cited sources: peer-reviewed paper, preprint, review, web page or news, other.
5. Record whether each known paper appears.
6. Choose ten factual sentences and open each cited source. Mark supported, partly supported or not supported.
7. Run it again with the three known papers uploaded as PDFs, and repeat steps 4 to 6.

## How to export Gemini deep research

**Open the report in Canvas, choose Share & export, then Export to Docs.** The other options are Copy Contents and a share link; Audio Overview sits under Create [1].

For a literature search, export to Docs and then:

1. Add a column or comment next to each claim: "checked", "partly", "not supported", "abstract only".
2. Delete claims you could not confirm instead of keeping them for later.
3. Keep the doc in your project folder as a record of how you found the papers, not as a source.

## How to move Gemini's findings into your Library

The report is a list of links. Your thesis needs a list of papers. Turn one into the other with this routine:

1. **Pull the papers out.** From the Docs export, copy every cited link that points to a paper (a DOI, PubMed, arXiv, a journal page). Ignore news and blogs, or trace them back to the paper they describe.
2. **Find the record.** Search each title or DOI in a scholarly index to get the full reference.
3. **Check them in bulk.** Paste the list into a citation checker to confirm each paper exists and is not retracted.
4. **Save to your Library** or reference manager, tagged with the question you were working on.
5. **Get the full texts** you need through your library, and read the methods and results of the papers you will rely on.
6. **Search the gaps.** Use the key terms from the report to run your own search, then follow the citations of the best papers forward and backward.

## FAQ

### Is Gemini deep research free?

Yes, with limits. Google's help page, checked November 2026, says anyone 18 or older who is signed in can use Deep Research in Gemini Apps. Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers get higher report limits and can use the Pro model for higher-quality reports. Google does not print exact daily numbers on that page, and free use may be unavailable at times of high demand.

### Can Gemini deep research read paywalled papers?

Not on its own. Google's help page lists Google Search, Gmail and Drive, uploaded files and NotebookLM notebooks as sources, and says nothing about subscription journals. In practice it reads what is publicly readable, which for a closed article is usually the abstract. If you have the PDF through your library, upload it so the run can read the full text.

### How do I export a Gemini deep research report?

Open the report in the Canvas panel and choose Share & export. From there you can export it to Google Docs or copy the contents. Google also offers an Audio Overview from the Create menu. Export to Docs is the most useful option for a literature search, because you keep the links and can annotate each claim as you check it.

### Is Gemini deep research better than ChatGPT deep research?

Neither is better for every question. Gemini lets you edit its research plan before it runs, usually finishes in 5 to 10 minutes and exports straight to Google Docs. ChatGPT lets you restrict a run to sites you choose and downloads reports as Word or PDF. Both search the open web, so both need the same source checks.

### Can I use Gemini deep research for a systematic review?

Not as the search. A systematic review needs searches you can report and rerun, in named databases, with screening by people. Gemini deep research picks its own queries and sources, so the search cannot be documented to that standard. Use it to find key terms and seed papers, then build and run the formal search in bibliographic databases.
