# IEEE citation format: how to cite in IEEE, with examples

*Last updated: 14 November 2026*

To cite in IEEE style, number your sources in the order you first cite them and put the number in square brackets in the text, for example [1]. The reference list at the end, titled References, lists the sources in that numerical order. Each entry gives authors as initials and surname, the article title in quotation marks, the abbreviated journal or conference name in italics, then volume, issue, pages, date and DOI [1].

IEEE style is used in electrical engineering, computer science, electronics and related fields. This guide follows the *IEEE Reference Guide* [1] and the *IEEE Editorial Style Manual* [2]. It is part of our citation styles series; for how numbered and author-date lists compare, see [how to format a reference list](/blog/how-to-format-a-reference-list).

## IEEE at a glance

| Feature | IEEE rule |
| --- | --- |
| In-text citation | Number in square brackets: [1] |
| Order of the list | Order of first citation, not alphabetical |
| Repeat citation | Reuse the original number |
| Author names | Initials then surname: C. E. Shannon |
| Author limit | All authors up to six; for seven or more, first author and et al. may be used |
| Article and chapter titles | Quotation marks, sentence case |
| Journal, book and conference titles | Italics; journal and conference names abbreviated |
| Labels | vol., no., pp., Art. no., ch., ed., Ed./Eds. |
| Dates | Abbreviated month and year: Jul. 1948 |
| DOI | At the end: doi: 10.xxxx |

## How do in-text citations work in IEEE?

**An IEEE in-text citation is the source's number in square brackets, placed inside the sentence before any punctuation: "as Shannon showed [1]."** The first source you cite is [1], the second new source is [2], and a source cited again keeps its first number [1], [2].

| Situation | Correct | Incorrect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One source | ... a channel with noise [1]. | ... a channel with noise.[1] |
| Several sources | [1], [3], [7] | [1, 3, 7] |
| A range | [4]–[7] | [4-7] |
| A page or figure | [1, p. 381] or [1, Fig. 2] | [1] p. 381 |
| The number as a noun | In [1], Shannon defines ... | In reference [1], Shannon defines ... |
| Starting a sentence | Reference [1] defines ... | [1] defines ... |
| Naming authors | He et al. [3] show ... | He et al. (2016) show ... |

Two habits cause most IEEE errors. First, writers copy an author-date habit and add a year after the number. IEEE never puts the year in the text. Second, after moving a paragraph, the numbers no longer follow the order of first citation. Renumber the whole list after every edit, or let a reference manager do it.

**Starting a sentence and the word "reference."** The *IEEE Editorial Style Manual* treats the bracketed number as a noun, so "in [1]" is fine; it asks you to avoid "in reference [1]" in the middle of a sentence and to write "Reference [1]" only at the start of one [2]. <!-- VERIFY: exact wording in the current IEEE Editorial Style Manual on "Ref." and "Reference [n]" -->

## IEEE reference list rules

**The list is titled References and gives each source once, in numerical order, with the number in square brackets flush left and the entry indented after it** [1].

1. **Authors:** initials first, then surname: C. E. Shannon. Two authors: A. Author and B. Author. Three to six: A. Author, B. Author, and C. Author (with a comma before "and"). Seven or more: the first author's name and et al. is allowed [1]. <!-- VERIFY: current IEEE Reference Guide wording for the et al. threshold (more than six names) -->
2. **Titles of parts** (articles, chapters, conference papers, web pages) go in quotation marks, in sentence case, with the comma inside the closing quotation mark: "A mathematical theory of communication,"
3. **Titles of wholes** (journals, books, conference proceedings, standards) go in italics. Book titles are in title case.
4. **Abbreviate** journal and conference names (see the table below).
5. **Number labels:** vol. 27, no. 3, pp. 379–423. For online journals that use article numbers instead of pages, write Art. no. 123456.
6. **Dates:** abbreviated month and year (Jul. 1948). IEEE shortens most month names to three letters and a period (Jan., Apr., Aug.) and writes May in full. <!-- VERIFY: IEEE month abbreviations (Jun./Jul. and Sep. vs Sept.) in the current Reference Guide -->
7. **DOI:** at the end, as "doi: 10.xxxx", followed by a period.

## IEEE reference examples

All examples below are real sources; two of them, Shannon [4] and He et al. [5], are also in the Sources list at the end. In the list, the parts in *italics* here are italic.

### Journal article

Pattern:

```text
[n] A. Author, B. Author, and C. Author, "Title of article,"
    Abbrev. Title of Journal, vol. x, no. x, pp. xxx–xxx,
    Abbrev. Month Year, doi: xxx.
```

Example:

> [1] C. E. Shannon, "A mathematical theory of communication," *Bell Syst. Tech. J.*, vol. 27, no. 3, pp. 379–423, Jul. 1948, doi: 10.1002/j.1538-7305.1948.tb01338.x.

Shannon's paper ran in two parts. This entry is Part I; Part II appeared in the October 1948 issue (no. 4) with its own pages and DOI, so cite the part you used.

A second example, the reference used across our style pages:

> [2] J. D. Watson and F. H. C. Crick, "Molecular structure of nucleic acids: A structure for deoxyribose nucleic acid," *Nature*, vol. 171, no. 4356, pp. 737–738, Apr. 1953, doi: 10.1038/171737a0.

### Conference paper

Pattern:

```text
[n] A. Author, "Title of paper," in Abbrev. Name of Conf.,
    (City, State, Country), Year, pp. xxx–xxx, doi: xxx.
```

Example:

> [3] K. He, X. Zhang, S. Ren, and J. Sun, "Deep residual learning for image recognition," in *Proc. IEEE Conf. Comput. Vis. Pattern Recognit. (CVPR)*, Las Vegas, NV, USA, 2016, pp. 770–778, doi: 10.1109/CVPR.2016.90.

The location of the conference is optional in the IEEE Reference Guide; keep it or drop it consistently [1].

### Book

Pattern:

```text
[n] A. Author, Title of Book, xth ed. City, State, Country:
    Publisher, Year.
```

Example:

> [4] C. E. Shannon and W. Weaver, *The Mathematical Theory of Communication*. Urbana, IL, USA: Univ. of Illinois Press, 1949.

Give the edition (2nd ed.) after the title only from the second edition on. For a US city, add the two-letter state code and USA; for other places, the city and country (Berlin, Germany).

### Chapter in an edited book

Pattern:

```text
[n] A. Author, "Title of chapter," in Title of Book, xth ed.,
    A. Editor, Ed. City, Country: Publisher, Year, pp. xxx–xxx.
```

Example:

> [5] Y. LeCun, L. Bottou, G. B. Orr, and K.-R. Müller, "Efficient BackProp," in *Neural Networks: Tricks of the Trade*, G. B. Orr and K.-R. Müller, Eds. Berlin, Germany: Springer, 1998, pp. 9–50, doi: 10.1007/3-540-49430-8_2. <!-- VERIFY: page range 9–50 and DOI of the LeCun et al. chapter (LNCS vol. 1524, 1998) -->

### Website

Pattern:

```text
[n] Author or Organization. "Title of page." Website Name.
    Accessed: Mon. Day, Year. [Online]. Available: URL
```

Example:

> [6] IEEE. "IEEE reference guide." IEEE Author Center. Accessed: Nov. 14, 2026. [Online]. Available: https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/IEEE_Reference_Guide.pdf

<!-- VERIFY: punctuation of the website pattern (periods vs commas after title and site) in the current IEEE Reference Guide, and the PDF URL -->

### Standard

Pattern:

```text
[n] Title of Standard, Standard number, Year.
```

Example:

> [7] *IEEE Standard for Floating-Point Arithmetic*, IEEE Std 754-2019, 2019, doi: 10.1109/IEEESTD.2019.8766229.

<!-- VERIFY: DOI of IEEE Std 754-2019 -->

Cite the version you used. IEEE 754 has editions from 1985, 2008 and 2019, and their content differs.

### Patent

Pattern:

```text
[n] A. Inventor, "Title of patent," Country Patent xxx xxx,
    Abbrev. Month Day, Year.
```

Example:

> [8] A. G. Bell, "Improvement in telegraphy," U.S. Patent 174 465, Mar. 7, 1876.

IEEE separates groups of three digits in a patent number with a space rather than a comma. The date is the date the patent was issued (or the filing date, labeled as such, for an application).

### Dataset

Pattern:

```text
[n] Author, "Title of dataset," Repository or distributor,
    Version (if any), Year. doi: xxx (or [Online]. Available: URL)
```

Example:

> [9] R. A. Fisher, "Iris," UCI Machine Learning Repository, 1936, doi: 10.24432/C56C76.

<!-- VERIFY: IEEE Reference Guide pattern for datasets, and the year (1936 vs 1988) and DOI the UCI repository gives for the Iris dataset -->

### Software

Pattern:

```text
[n] Author or Organization, Title of Software. (Version).
    Publisher, Year. [Online]. Available: URL
```

Example:

> [10] Python Software Foundation, *Python*. (Version 3.12). 2023. [Online]. Available: https://www.python.org/

<!-- VERIFY: IEEE Reference Guide pattern for software and code (punctuation, placement of version) -->

If the software has a paper or a DOI (for example, an archived release), cite the version you ran, with its DOI. Reviewers in computing want to know the exact version.

## IEEE journal and conference abbreviations

**IEEE abbreviates journal and conference names word by word with standard forms, and the abbreviated name stays in italics.** The *IEEE Reference Guide* includes the list; the common ones are below [1].

| Word | Abbreviation | Word | Abbreviation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Transactions | Trans. | Communications | Commun. |
| Journal | J. | Computer, Computing | Comput. |
| Proceedings | Proc. | Engineering | Eng. |
| Conference | Conf. | Electronics | Electron. |
| International | Int. | Information | Inf. |
| Symposium | Symp. | Systems | Syst. |
| Letters | Lett. | Technology | Technol. |
| Magazine | Mag. | Technical | Tech. |
| Annual | Annu. | Society | Soc. |

Applied to real titles:

| Full title | IEEE form |
| --- | --- |
| IEEE Transactions on Information Theory | *IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory* |
| Proceedings of the IEEE | *Proc. IEEE* |
| IEEE Journal on Selected Areas in Communications | *IEEE J. Sel. Areas Commun.* |
| IEEE Communications Magazine | *IEEE Commun. Mag.* |
| Bell System Technical Journal | *Bell Syst. Tech. J.* |
| Nature | *Nature* (one-word titles are not abbreviated) |

For a non-IEEE journal, check the abbreviation the journal itself uses or the ISO 4 abbreviation in a database such as the NLM Catalog or CASSI, and use it consistently.

| Correct | Incorrect | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C. E. Shannon, | Shannon, C. E., | Initials before surname |
| "A mathematical theory of communication," | *A Mathematical Theory of Communication*. | Article titles in quotes, sentence case |
| *Bell Syst. Tech. J.*, | Bell System Technical Journal, | Abbreviate and italicize the journal |
| vol. 27, no. 3, pp. 379–423, | 27(3):379-423 | IEEE uses labels |
| Jul. 1948, | (1948) | Month and year at the end, no parentheses |
| doi: 10.1002/j.1538-7305.1948.tb01338.x. | DOI 10.1002/... | Lowercase "doi:" with a colon |
| List in order of first citation | List sorted A to Z | IEEE is a numbered style |

## IEEE in LaTeX

Most IEEE journals and conferences provide LaTeX templates with the IEEEtran BibTeX style, which formats a .bib file into IEEE references and numbers them for you [3]. Use `\cite{shannon1948}` in the text and let BibTeX build the list. The output is only as good as your .bib entries, so check author names, capitalization (wrap acronyms in braces: `{IEEE}`) and DOIs.

## Common IEEE citation mistakes

- Sorting the reference list alphabetically.
- Giving a cited source a new number the second time.
- Adding years in the text: [1, 2016].
- Using full journal names, or abbreviations that are not italic.
- Putting the bracket after the period: "noise.[1]".
- Leaving out the DOI or the article number for online-only papers.
- Citing a conference paper as if it were a journal article (missing "in" and *Proc.*).

## FAQ

### How do you cite in IEEE format?

Number your sources in the order you first cite them and put the number in square brackets in the text, for example [1], before any punctuation. Reuse the same number each time you cite that source again. At the end, list the sources in numerical order under References, each starting with its bracketed number, with initials before surnames and article titles in quotation marks.

### How do you cite multiple sources in IEEE?

Put each number in its own brackets, separated by commas: [1], [3], [7]. For three or more consecutive sources, use a range with an en dash: [4]–[7]. Do not combine numbers inside one pair of brackets, as in [1, 3]. IEEE keeps a comma inside brackets for adding a page or figure to a single citation, as in [3, p. 12].

### Does IEEE use et al.?

In the reference list, IEEE lists all authors up to six. For seven or more, the IEEE Reference Guide allows the first author's name followed by et al. In the text you normally cite by number, so names are not needed; if you do name a work with three or more authors in a sentence, write the first author's surname and et al., for example He et al. [2].

### Are journal names abbreviated in IEEE?

Yes. IEEE abbreviates journal and conference names using standard word abbreviations, such as Trans. for Transactions, J. for Journal and Proc. for Proceedings, and sets them in italics. IEEE Transactions on Information Theory becomes IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory. The IEEE Reference Guide includes a list of these abbreviations, and IEEE's own titles have set forms you should copy exactly.

### How do you cite a website in IEEE?

Give the author or organization if there is one, the page title in quotation marks, the website name, then Accessed: with the date you viewed it in Mon. Day, Year form, then [Online]. Available: and the full URL. Websites change, so the access date matters more in IEEE than for fixed sources such as a journal article with a DOI.
