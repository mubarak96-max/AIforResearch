AI for social science research should help with both halves of the work: the words and the numbers. AI for Research gives you a Qualitative workbook for coding transcripts with exact quotes, a Thesis Suite module that turns constructs into measurable variables, a Survey builder with validated scales, statistics calculated in the browser, and public economic and social data from the OECD and FRED. Papers are searched across 250M+ scholarly works and cited in APA or any of 10,000+ styles.

## A worked example

*Example (invented for illustration):* a master's student in education studies teacher burnout with 12 interviews and a survey of 140 teachers.

1. The Thesis Suite's operationalization module turns "burnout", "workload" and "school support" into indicators and instruments, and points to the PSS-10 for perceived stress. 3 credits.
2. She builds the survey with the PSS-10 and her own workload items. 0 credits.
3. She uploads three transcripts to the Qualitative workbook, writes a codebook of nine codes, and asks for AI code suggestions on each: 3 credits each, 9 credits. She rejects four suggestions and marks two excerpts as negative cases. The remaining nine transcripts she codes by hand.
4. In Data Lab she checks Cronbach's alpha for her workload items, then runs a linear regression of stress on workload, free. The AI results paragraph costs 2 credits, and the PDF report is free.
5. From Official Sources she adds OECD figures on teachers' working hours for the introduction, 0 credits.
6. She checks the 9,000-word chapter against COREQ 2007: 5 credits.

Total: 19 credits, inside the Free plan's 45 a month.

## Citation styles and reporting standards

APA 7 is the default in most of the social sciences, and the journal formatter has an APA preset with a blank APA template in the editor; Chicago, Harvard and ASA are among the other 10,000+ styles. For qualitative studies the guideline check covers COREQ 2007 for interviews and focus groups and SRQR 2014 for qualitative research in general; for surveys and other observational designs, STROBE 2007. SAGER applies when you report sex or gender, and SAMPL when you report statistics.

## What it does not do

- It does not code a whole dataset for you or decide your themes. Suggestions quote the text; you accept or reject each one.
- It does not transcribe audio. Upload text transcripts.
- It does not include licensed scales, such as the SF-36, the BDI or the MBI.
- It does not search ICPSR, the UK Data Service, national statistics offices or census microdata.
- No structural equation modeling, multilevel models or factor analysis yet.
- Statistics are calculated, not generated, but the choice of test and the interpretation are yours.
