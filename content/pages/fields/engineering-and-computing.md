AI for computer science research has to start where the field publishes: conferences and preprints. AI for Research searches DBLP and arXiv next to OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar, answers questions with citations to those papers, drafts a conference paper to the venue's page limit, and formats it for IEEE, ACM or another style. It works for engineering research too, with Word, PDF and LaTeX in and out.

## A worked example

*Example (invented for illustration):* a PhD student in software engineering is writing a paper on flaky test detection for a conference with an 8-page limit.

1. In Find Papers, DBLP source, he searches "flaky tests" for the last five years and saves 30 papers from the main software engineering venues to his Library. 0 credits.
2. He adds arXiv preprints from the last six months. 0 credits.
3. A Deep Research Summary answer on "which features predict flaky tests, and how are detectors evaluated?" cites twelve of them. 1 credit.
4. He draws the pipeline of his method with Draw with AI and edits the Mermaid code: 1 credit.
5. He compares his detector with two baselines across 20 projects in Data Lab with a Wilcoxon signed-rank test, free.
6. The Conference paper module drafts the paper from his notes, results and Library, 8 pages, with three contribution statements: 15 credits.
7. He runs the journal formatter on the 7,000-word draft with the IEEE style: 3 credits.

Total: 20 credits, inside the Free plan's 45 a month. He moves the text into the venue's official LaTeX template for submission; LaTeX export is on paid plans, or he copies from the Word export.

## Citation styles and reporting standards

IEEE and ACM reference styles are presets in the journal formatter, with blank venue templates in the editor, and both are among the 10,000+ styles for references. Many conferences accept only their own LaTeX class, so keep the official author kit at hand for the camera-ready. Computing has few formal reporting guidelines like those in medicine, but reviewers expect the dataset version, code revision, hardware and evaluation protocol for every result; the Technical research workbook has fields for exactly these, and the SAMPL guidelines help when you report statistical comparisons between methods.

## What it does not do

- It does not search IEEE Xplore, the ACM Digital Library or Inspec. Bring those papers in as PDFs or export files.
- It does not run your code, experiments or simulations, and it does not train models.
- No CAD, finite element analysis or CFD.
- It does not submit to a conference or guarantee acceptance. Use the venue's official template and read its current call for papers.
- Drafts are written from your notes and sources; the contribution and the results must be yours. Check the venue's policy on AI-assisted writing.
