This thesis defense presentation template gives you 12 main slides and 6 backup slides for a 20-minute talk, each with fields to fill, a prompt for what to say and a time budget. Copy it into PowerPoint, Keynote or Google Slides, or let the Slides workspace draft it from your thesis.

## The thesis defense presentation template: 12 main slides (copy this)

Each slide has three fields. **Title** is a sentence, not a label. **On screen** is the most you show. **Say** is the prompt for your speaker notes. Times add up to 20 minutes.

```text
SLIDE 1  TITLE PAGE                                         0:30
Title:     [thesis title]
On screen: [your name] | [degree, program] | [supervisor] | [date]
Say:       Thank the committee. One sentence on what the thesis asks.

SLIDE 2  THE PROBLEM                                        1:30
Title:     [the problem as a claim, e.g. "X costs Y every year"]
On screen: one number, image or case. Nothing else.
Say:       Who is affected and why it matters now.

SLIDE 3  WHAT IS KNOWN, AND THE GAP                         2:00
Title:     [the gap in one sentence]
On screen: 3 to 5 key studies as a timeline or diagram, author-year
Say:       What these studies show, then what none of them did.

SLIDE 4  RESEARCH QUESTION                                  1:00
Title:     Research question
On screen: the question (and hypotheses or sub-questions), alone
Say:       Read it aloud. Say which slide answers each part.

SLIDE 5  DESIGN                                             1:30
Title:     [design in plain words, e.g. "A two-year cohort of 640 students"]
On screen: flowchart: setting, sample, timeline
Say:       Why this design answers this question better than others.

SLIDE 6  DATA AND ANALYSIS                                  1:30
Title:     [the analysis in one line]
On screen: measures or data sources; named tests or approach
Say:       One sentence on each choice an examiner may question.

SLIDE 7  RESULT 1                                           2:30
Title:     [finding 1 as a sentence]
On screen: one chart or table from the thesis, labeled
Say:       Read the chart for them: axes, then the pattern, then the number.

SLIDE 8  RESULT 2                                           2:30
Title:     [finding 2 as a sentence]
On screen: one chart or table
Say:       Same order as slide 7.

SLIDE 9  RESULT 3 OR SYNTHESIS                              2:00
Title:     [finding 3, or how the results fit together]
On screen: one chart, table or summary figure
Say:       Link the findings back to the question on slide 4.

SLIDE 10 WHAT IT MEANS                                      2:00
Title:     [your interpretation as a sentence]
On screen: 2 to 3 points, each tied to a study from slide 3
Say:       Where you agree with the literature, where you do not, why.

SLIDE 11 LIMITS AND NEXT STUDIES                            1:30
Title:     Limitations and future work
On screen: 2 to 3 limits, each paired with the study that fixes it
Say:       Name the limit before the committee does.

SLIDE 12 CONTRIBUTION                                       1:30
Title:     [the contribution in one sentence]
On screen: 1 to 3 contributions, one line each; thank you; contact
Say:       What exists now that did not before. Stop. Invite questions.
```

## Backup slides (copy this)

Backup slides sit after slide 12, numbered B1, B2 and so on, so you can jump to them by number when asked.

```text
B1  Full model or coding framework   [specification, variables, codebook]
B2  Full results table               [every coefficient or theme count]
B3  Sensitivity or robustness check  [the analysis an examiner may ask for]
B4  Sample and attrition             [flow of participants or records]
B5  Instrument                       [survey items, interview guide]
B6  The study you did not do         [why you rejected the obvious alternative design]
```

## Design specs for the thesis defense presentation template

| Element | Setting |
| --- | --- |
| Slide size | 16:9, unless your department's template says otherwise |
| Slide titles | 32 to 40 pt, a full sentence for results |
| Body text | 24 pt minimum; at most 5 bullets |
| Charts | Re-drawn with axis labels at 18 pt or larger |
| Slide numbers | On every slide, so examiners can refer to them |
| Citations | Author and year on the slide; full references on a final slide |
| Colors | One accent color; check contrast for a projector |

For why each slide is there, timing variants for 10, 15 and 30 minutes, and how to rehearse, read the guide to the [thesis defense presentation](/blog/thesis-defense-presentation).

## A filled example (invented)

> **Example only.** The thesis, numbers and names are invented to show how much goes on a slide.

A master's student in engineering education defends a thesis on peer tutoring and first-year retention.

- **Slide 2.** Title: "One in four engineering students leaves after year one." On screen: a single bar showing a 24% first-year dropout rate at the study university.
- **Slide 4.** "Is attending weekly peer tutoring associated with staying in the program into year two, after adjusting for entry grades?"
- **Slide 7.** Title: "Students who attended 6+ sessions were half as likely to leave." On screen: a bar chart of dropout by attendance band (0, 1 to 5, 6+ sessions), with the adjusted odds ratio under it.
- **Slide 11.** "Students chose to attend, so motivation may explain part of the effect. Next: a randomized invitation design."
- **B3.** The same model excluding students who changed programs, because a committee member works on program transfers.

## What this template and Slides do not do

- There is no PowerPoint file of the template to download. Copy the text, or generate a deck and download that.
- Slides does not know your committee's time limit, your department's slide master or your examiners. Set the length yourself and time it in rehearsal.
- Generated charts are not your thesis figures. Replace them, and check every number on screen against the thesis.
- The free plan shows the first 5 slides. PowerPoint download is on Plus, Pro, Team and the Defence Pass.
- No tool rehearses for you. Time yourself at least three times.

## Who it is for

The template is for master's and PhD candidates in the last month before a defense, and for supervisors running mock defenses. At a UK viva, where there is often no talk, it works as the outline of a short opening summary.
