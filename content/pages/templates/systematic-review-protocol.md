This systematic review protocol template follows the fields of PROSPERO, the international register for health-related systematic reviews, so you can copy each answer into the registration form. It is for students, clinicians and review teams writing a first protocol. Each field is explained, and a filled example shows the detail registrars expect.

Write the protocol before you search properly, and register it before data extraction starts [1]. If you are still deciding which kind of review you need, read [what is a systematic review](/blog/what-is-a-systematic-review) and [scoping review vs systematic review](/blog/scoping-review-vs-systematic-review) first.

## What each field asks for

The fields registrars return most often are the eligibility criteria and the synthesis plan.

| Field | What to write | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Review question | One sentence naming population, intervention or exposure, comparator and outcome | A topic ("napping and shift work") instead of a question |
| Eligibility criteria | Include and exclude lines per element, each testable from a paper | "High-quality studies only": quality is assessed later, not screened for |
| Main outcomes | The measure, the scale and the time point | Listing every outcome as "main" |
| Selection process | Number of screeners at each stage, independence, how conflicts are settled | Leaving out the full-text stage |
| Risk of bias | The named tool for each study design (for example RoB 2, ROBINS-I) | One tool for trials and cohort studies alike |
| Data synthesis | The condition for pooling, the model, and the fallback if pooling is not possible | "We will do a meta-analysis" with no criteria |
| Stage of review | What you have actually started or finished | Ticking data extraction as started, which makes the review ineligible |

The criteria builder in [inclusion and exclusion criteria](/templates/inclusion-exclusion-criteria) gives example lines for PICO, PCC and SPIDER, and [systematic review search strategy](/blog/systematic-review-search-strategy) covers the searching fields in depth.

## A filled example (invented)

*This is an invented example for illustration.*

- **Review title:** Planned night-shift naps and alertness in adult shift workers: a systematic review and meta-analysis.
- **Review question:** In adult shift workers, does a planned nap during the night shift improve alertness at the end of the shift compared with no nap?
- **Population:** Include adults (18 or over) working night or rotating shifts in any occupation. Exclude students in simulated shifts with no work duties.
- **Intervention:** Include a planned nap of any length taken during the shift. Exclude naps taken before the shift only.
- **Comparator:** No nap, or usual breaks without sleep.
- **Study designs:** Include randomized and non-randomized controlled studies, including crossover trials. Exclude case series and studies with no comparison group.
- **Main outcome:** Alertness at the end of the shift, measured by the Psychomotor Vigilance Task or the Karolinska Sleepiness Scale.
- **Searches:** MEDLINE via PubMed, Embase via Ovid, CENTRAL, PsycINFO and Web of Science, from inception, no language limits. Reference lists of included studies and two trial registers.
- **Selection:** Two reviewers screen titles and abstracts and then full texts independently; disagreements are settled by discussion, then by a third reviewer.
- **Data extraction:** One reviewer extracts into a piloted form; a second checks every row against the paper.
- **Risk of bias:** RoB 2 for randomized trials, with the crossover variant for crossover trials; ROBINS-I for non-randomized studies.
- **Synthesis:** If three or more studies report the same outcome on comparable scales, a random-effects meta-analysis of standardized mean differences; otherwise a structured synthesis without meta-analysis.
- **Stage:** Pilot searching and screening of 100 records completed; formal screening not started.

## PROSPERO rules to check before you register

PROSPERO's own help pages set conditions that decide whether your record will be published [1][3]:

1. **Register before data extraction.** Records for reviews that have already started extraction are not accepted.
2. **Fill every field in English.** Uploaded protocols and search strategies may be in any language, but the fields must be complete; pointing to a protocol instead is not enough.
3. **Check the review type.** PROSPERO does not register scoping reviews at present, nor training exercises or mini reviews done for a course or module. Use OSF Registries or a protocol journal instead.
4. **Have an ORCID.** PROSPERO now asks everyone who creates a record for an ORCID.

For a full protocol published in a journal or on OSF, check it against PRISMA-P 2015, the reporting checklist for protocols [4]. For reporting the finished review, see the [PRISMA checklist](/templates/reporting-checklists).

## How to fill this template in AI for Research

The Systematic Review workspace holds the question and criteria that a protocol needs, and drafts the protocol from them.

1. Open Systematic Review and choose **New review**. Enter a title and the review question.
2. Pick the **Type of review** (systematic, scoping, rapid, meta-analysis, mapping, qualitative evidence synthesis, realist or living). The framework is set to match: PICO, PCC or SPIDER. You can change it.
3. Fill the framework fields, then **Include when** and **Exclude when**, one criterion per line.
4. On the **Protocol** tab, choose **Draft the protocol with AI** (3 credits). The draft uses PROSPERO's main headings, from review title to timeline, and marks anything you have not decided as [To decide: ...].
5. Edit it in place, switch between **Edit** and **Preview**, and **Save**. Copy each section into the PROSPERO form.

| Workspace draft heading | PROSPERO form group |
| --- | --- |
| Review title; Review question | Review title and type; Question |
| Condition or domain; Participants or population; Intervention(s) or exposure(s); Comparator(s) or control(s); Types of study; Context; Main and additional outcome(s) | Eligibility criteria |
| Searches (databases, dates, sample search string) | Searching |
| Data extraction (selection and coding) | Selection process; Data extraction |
| Risk of bias (quality) assessment | Risk of bias |
| Strategy for data synthesis; Analysis of subgroups or subsets | Data synthesis |
| Timeline | Timeline and stage |

## What this template and the workspace do not do

- **They do not register your review.** You submit the record on PROSPERO yourself, under your own account.
- **The AI draft does not cover every PROSPERO field.** Reporting bias, certainty of evidence, the screening method, language and date limits, team, funding, conflicts of interest and peer review are yours to add.
- **The draft is only as good as your criteria.** It writes from what you entered; it does not choose your outcomes, tools or synthesis model for you.
- **There is no Word or PDF download of this template.** Copy it, or paste it into the editor and export from there.
