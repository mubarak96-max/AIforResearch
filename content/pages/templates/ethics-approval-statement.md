An ethics approval statement is the paragraph in a paper or thesis that names the committee that approved your study, the reference number, and how participants consented. Below are wordings for 10 study types you can copy and adapt, an IRB submission checklist on risk, consent and data protection, and a way to draft the application itself.

## What every ethics approval statement includes

Whatever the study type, a reviewer or journal editor looks for the same five facts. Missing any of them is a common reason for a query before review.

| Element | Example | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Committee name | [University] Research Ethics Committee | Use the committee's full name, not "the IRB" |
| Reference number | Ref. [ABC-2026-041] | Some journals ask you to blind it for review |
| Date of approval | [12 March 2026] | Before data collection started |
| Consent | Written, verbal, online or waived, and by whom | For minors: parental consent and child assent |
| Standard followed | Declaration of Helsinki, local law, ARRIVE | Medical research usually cites Helsinki [1] |

## Ethics approval statement wordings by study type

Replace everything in square brackets. Keep the wording factual; the statement records what happened, so write it in the past tense.

### 1. Human participants, full approval, written consent

> This study was approved by the [committee name], [institution] (reference [number], [date]). All participants gave written informed consent before taking part.

### 2. Exempt determination (US)

> The [institution] Institutional Review Board reviewed this study and determined it exempt under 45 CFR 46.104(d)([category]) (protocol [number], [date]). Participants received an information sheet and consented by [method].

The exempt categories are set out in the US Common Rule [2].

### 3. Medical records, consent waived

> This retrospective study of de-identified records was approved by the [committee] (reference [number]), which waived the requirement for individual consent because [reason, e.g. the data were anonymized before the researchers received them]. The study followed the Declaration of Helsinki.

### 4. Anonymous online survey

> The [committee] approved this study (reference [number]). Participants read an information page and indicated consent by selecting "I agree" before starting the anonymous survey. No identifying data were collected.

### 5. Children or young people

> The study was approved by the [committee] (reference [number]). Parents or guardians gave written informed consent, and each child gave assent in age-appropriate language before taking part. [School or organization] gave permission for recruitment.

### 6. Interviews and focus groups

> Ethical approval was granted by the [committee] (reference [number], [date]). Participants gave written consent, including consent to audio recording. Recordings were deleted after transcription, and transcripts were pseudonymized.

### 7. Clinical trial

> The trial was approved by [committee(s)] (reference [number]) and registered at [registry] ([registration number]) on [date], before the first participant gave consent. All participants gave written informed consent. The trial was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Journals that follow ICMJE recommendations require trial registration at or before the first participant's consent [3].

### 8. Public or secondary data, no approval needed

> This study used publicly available, de-identified data from [source]. The [institution] research ethics office confirmed on [date] that ethical approval was not required [reference, if any].

### 9. Animal research

> All procedures were approved by the [IACUC or animal ethics body] (protocol [number]) and carried out in accordance with [national law]. The study is reported following the ARRIVE guidelines.

ARRIVE 2.0 sets what animal studies should report [4].

### 10. Case report

> Written informed consent was obtained from the patient [or legal guardian] for publication of this case report and any accompanying images.

## A filled example (invented)

> **Example only.** The university, committee and reference number are invented.

> This study was approved by the Northfield University Human Research Ethics Committee (reference HREC-2026-118, 4 February 2026). All participants gave written informed consent, including consent to audio recording. Data were stored on the university's encrypted server and pseudonymized before analysis.

## IRB submission checklist: risk, consent, data protection

Use this before you submit. It is grouped the way committees read an application. For how to write each section, see [how to write an IRB application](/blog/how-to-write-an-irb-application), which also has a checklist of the documents to attach.

### Risk

| Check | Done |
| --- | --- |
| Every risk named: physical, psychological, social, legal, economic, privacy | ☐ |
| Each risk rated for likelihood and severity, with a mitigation | ☐ |
| Distress protocol: what you do if a participant becomes upset, with referral contacts | ☐ |
| Risks to researchers covered (lone fieldwork, sensitive topics) | ☐ |
| Deception, if any, justified with a debrief plan | ☐ |

### Consent

| Check | Done |
| --- | --- |
| Information sheet in plain language, at a reading level your participants can follow | ☐ |
| Key facts first: purpose, what taking part involves, time, risks, right to withdraw | ☐ |
| Withdrawal: until when, and what happens to data already collected | ☐ |
| Separate consent for recording, quotes and future use of data | ☐ |
| Assent and guardian consent for minors; capacity plan for adults who may lack it | ☐ |
| Payment or incentive amount, and that it does not pressure people to take part | ☐ |
| Contact for complaints that is independent of the research team | ☐ |

### Data protection

| Check | Done |
| --- | --- |
| What personal data you collect, and why each item is needed | ☐ |
| Where it is stored, who has access, and how it is encrypted | ☐ |
| When identifiers are removed (anonymized or pseudonymized) | ☐ |
| How long data are kept, and how they are destroyed | ☐ |
| Transfers outside your country or to cloud tools named | ☐ |
| Data sharing plan matches what the consent form says | ☐ |

## What this page and the tool do not do

- This is not legal advice, and neither the wordings nor the checklist replace your institution's rules. Your IRB or ethics office decides what applies.
- The tool does not submit to any board, check compliance or produce an approval. Never state an approval, reference number or exemption you have not received.
- It does not know your institution's form. Copy the content into the form your committee uses.
- Clinical, patient and legal content needs review by a qualified person at your institution before submission.
