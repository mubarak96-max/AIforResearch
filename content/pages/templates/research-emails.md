A cold email to professor for research has one job: get a reply from a busy person who does not know you. These six templates cover it and the emails that follow: asking about a research position, contacting a possible PhD supervisor, booking a meeting, requesting data, inviting a co-author and following up. Each is under 200 words.

## Five rules for every email to a professor for research

1. **Subject line with the topic and the ask.** "Prospective PhD student: soil carbon modeling, fall 2027" gets opened. "Inquiry" does not.
2. **One screen.** Under 200 words. Put the ask in the first three sentences.
3. **One specific detail.** Name a paper, dataset or method of theirs and say what you would do with it. This is the sentence that shows you are not mass-mailing.
4. **One clear request.** A 20-minute call, a file, a yes or no. Not "any advice".
5. **Attachments named.** "Lastname_CV.pdf", not "CV final v3.pdf". Mention them in the text.

## Email to professor for research: the six templates

Square brackets are what you replace. The notes under each say which line matters most.

### 1. Asking a professor about a research position (undergraduate or master's)

```text
Subject: [Year] [program] student interested in your [topic] research

Dear Professor [Lastname],

I am a [year] [program] student at [university]. I read your [year] paper
"[title]" and was interested in [one specific finding or method].

I would like to ask whether your group has a place for a research assistant
this [semester or summer]. I have [one relevant skill: e.g. R, lab technique,
interviewing] from [course or project], and I can offer [hours] a week.

I have attached my CV and transcript. Would you have 15 minutes for a call or
a visit during your office hours in the next two weeks?

Thank you for your time.
[Full name] | [Program, year] | [university email]
```

*Note:* the second paragraph is the ask. Professors take students who can name what they will do.

### 2. Contacting a possible PhD supervisor

```text
Subject: Prospective PhD student, [topic], [start term]

Dear Dr [Lastname],

I am finishing a [degree] in [field] at [university] and plan to apply for
the PhD in [program] for [start term]. Your work on [topic], especially
[paper], is close to the question I want to study: [one-sentence question].

My [master's thesis or project] used [method] to [result in one line].
I have attached a two-page proposal and my CV.

Are you taking new PhD students for [start term]? If so, could we talk for
20 minutes about whether my project fits your group?

Best regards,
[Full name] | [Current position] | [link to profile or thesis]
```

*Note:* attach a short proposal. The [research proposal template](/templates/research-proposal) has the structure, cut to two pages.

### 3. Setting up a meeting with your supervisor

```text
Subject: Meeting request: [chapter or decision], [week]

Hi [Name],

Could we meet for 30 minutes before [date]? I need your view on [one
decision, e.g. whether to drop the second survey wave].

Before we meet I will send [draft or summary] by [day]. The options I see:
  1. [option A, its cost]
  2. [option B, its cost]

I am free [three time slots]. A call works if that is easier.

Thanks,
[Name]
```

*Note:* a meeting with a decision on the agenda gets scheduled faster than a general catch-up.

### 4. Requesting data from an author

```text
Subject: Data request: [short paper title] ([journal], [year])

Dear Dr [Lastname],

I am a [position] at [institution] working on [project]. Your paper
"[full title]" ([journal], [year], doi:[DOI]) reports [the data you need].

Would you be willing to share [exact variables, files or time period]?
I would use them to [purpose], store them on [secure location], use them
only for this project, and cite your paper and dataset in any output.
[My ethics approval, reference [number], covers secondary analysis.]

I am happy to sign a data use agreement or to offer co-authorship if your
input goes beyond sharing the files.

Kind regards,
[Full name] | [Institution] | [email]
```

*Note:* check the paper's data availability statement and repositories first. Asking for exact variables gets more yeses than "the dataset".

### 5. Inviting a co-author

```text
Subject: Invitation to co-author: [working title]

Dear [Name],

I am writing a paper on [topic] for [target journal], using [data or
method]. Because of your work on [their expertise], I would like to invite
you as a co-author, contributing [specific role: e.g. the analysis plan,
the discussion of policy implications].

Timeline: draft by [date], submission by [date]. Expected time from you:
about [hours]. Authorship order: [proposed order, or "to agree together"].

The draft and data are in a shared project I can add you to. If this is not
a good time, no problem at all.

Best,
[Name]
```

*Note:* stating the role and hours up front prevents authorship disputes later.

### 6. Following up after no reply

```text
Subject: Re: [original subject]

Dear Professor [Lastname],

I am following up on my email below about [one-line ask]. Since then,
[one new fact, e.g. I completed the course in Bayesian statistics].

I understand you are busy. If you are not able to take this on, I would be
grateful for the name of a colleague who might.

Best regards,
[Name]
```

*Note:* send it once, one to two weeks later, in the same thread.

## How to do the groundwork in AI for Research

The email itself is short. What makes it work is the specific detail, and that comes from reading the person's work.

1. Search their topic in **Find Papers**, filter to the last few years and open their recent papers. No credits.
2. Save one or two papers to the **Library** and ask a question of the PDF (1 credit per question) to check you understood the method you mention.
3. Open the **Writing Studio** and choose **Research email** under **Correspondence**. Pick the kind (co-author invitation, meeting invitation, data request, paper request, prospective-supervisor enquiry or follow-up), then fill in the recipient's role, the purpose, the study and any deadline. You get a short draft with [placeholders] for what only you know. 1 credit.
4. Replace every placeholder and write the one sentence about why you chose this person yourself. Ask the **editor** for a shorter rewrite (1 credit) or run a tone check (2 credits) if you want.
5. For a co-author or supervisor, share a **Project** with them by email as can edit, can comment or can view, so the draft and papers sit in one place.

## What these templates and the tool do not do

- AI for Research does not send email, find email addresses or track replies. You send from your own account.
- No template replaces the sentence about why you chose this person. Write that yourself.
- Data requests do not override a paper's license or a participant consent form. The author decides what can be shared.
