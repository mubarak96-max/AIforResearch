The statistics are calculated in your browser, not generated by the AI, and they cost nothing. ## How to read a Bayes factor

BF10 is the evidence for the alternative over the null; BF01 is its reciprocal, the evidence for the null. A value of 1 means the data do not favor either. Many researchers describe the size of a Bayes factor with labels like these, adapted from Jeffreys:

| BF10 | BF01 | Common label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 3 | 1 to 3 | Anecdotal or weak |
| 3 to 10 | 3 to 10 | Moderate |
| 10 to 30 | 10 to 30 | Strong |
| above 30 | above 30 | Very strong or extreme |

<!-- VERIFY: label thresholds against Lee and Wagenmakers (2013) / Jeffreys (1961) as used in the bayes-factor-explained post -->

The labels are a convention, not a rule. Report the number itself, and say which prior produced it. A credible interval is read directly: given the prior and the data, there is a 95% probability that the true value lies inside a 95% credible interval. That is the statement many people wrongly make about a confidence interval.

## What it does not do

- It does not fit full Bayesian models. No Bayesian regression, ANOVA, hierarchical models or MCMC sampling.
- It does not choose a prior for you beyond the default. If your field has an informed prior, note it and run the analysis in a package that accepts it.
- It does not replace a statistician or statistical software. The tests are calculated, not validated or certified for regulatory use.
- It does not tell you what to conclude. A Bayes factor is evidence for one hypothesis relative to another, under the prior you used; report both.
