A research project management tool keeps one research job in a place other people can open. In AI for Research, a project is a shared folder for a thesis, a literature review or a grant: it holds the reports, reviews, drafts and analyses you ran, and shares them with a supervisor or co-authors with the right access.

<!-- SCREENSHOT: the Projects page with two project cards showing the member count and the viewer's role -->

## What a project holds

Every job you run in AI for Research is saved as a task in your History. A project is a set of those tasks, shared. It can hold work from any workspace:

| Workspace | What goes into the project |
| --- | --- |
| Deep Research | Summary answers and Deep or Detailed reports, with their sources and follow-ups |
| Reviewer, Claim check, Citation check | Reviews of a thesis, proposal or manuscript, and their results |
| Writing Studio, Thesis Suite, Proposal Studio | Drafts, outlines, methodology, gaps, timelines and proposals |
| Systematic Review | The whole review: protocol, searches, screening, extraction and PRISMA counts |
| Citation Map and Landscape | Saved maps with their working set, and field overviews |
| Data Lab and Slides | Analyses with their results section, and slide decks |

Each item opens in its own workspace, where a member can read the full result.

## How it works, step by step

1. **Create a project.** Open Projects and press New project. Give it a name ("MSc thesis: sleep and memory") and, if you like, a line on what it is about.
2. **Run your work as usual.** Ask a research question, run a review, write a chapter outline. Each job is saved in your History.
3. **Move work into the project.** In History, each row has a project menu. Choose the project and the task moves in. You can move it out again the same way.
4. **Start a systematic review inside the project** by choosing it in the Project field when you create the review. Editors on the project then screen as second reviewers.
5. **Invite people.** On the project page, type an email under Invite by email, choose a role and press Invite. They get an email with the link and see the project when they sign in with that address.
6. **Change who has access.** The owner removes a member with the × next to their name. A member can leave on their own the same way.

## The three roles

| Role | What they can do | Typical person |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can edit | Read everything, run research and move their own work into the project | Co-author, lab colleague |
| Can comment | Read everything; cannot run new work or change existing work | Supervisor, committee member |
| Can view | Read only | Second marker, a colleague who wants to follow along |
| Owner | All of the above, plus invite, remove and delete | You |

## History: every task, saved

History is the other half of keeping research in order. Every report, review, draft and analysis you run is saved there automatically. You can:

- **Search** your History by title and summary.
- **Filter** by kind: Deep Research, Reviewer, Claim check, Citation check, Thesis Suite, Proposal Studio, Writing Studio, Systematic Review, Data Lab, Citation Map, Landscape, Slides and Chat with PDF.
- **Star** the items you come back to, and show only starred ones.
- **See what each job cost**, in credits, next to its date.
- **Reopen** any item in its workspace and continue it: ask a follow-up, change a draft, run the next snowball round.
- **Move** an item into a project, or delete it.

## Example: a master's thesis with a supervisor

*This is an example of how a project is set up, not a real account.*

Amira is writing a master's thesis on sleep and memory. She creates a project called "MSc thesis: sleep and memory" and invites her supervisor as Can comment and her lab partner as Can edit. Over a term she moves in a Deep report on sleep and consolidation, a Citation Map of the two key papers, a thesis outline from the Thesis Suite, and a Reviewer run on her literature review chapter at Master's level. Before each supervision, her supervisor opens the project and reads the latest work without needing a copy by email. On the free plan this uses her 1 project and 1 invited person, so she moves to Plus to add the lab partner.

## What it does not do

Projects are a research project management tool for keeping and sharing work, with these limits:

- **It is not a task manager.** There are no deadlines, assignments, Gantt charts or boards. For a plan of phases and milestones, the Thesis Suite has a Research timeline.
- **Your Library and editor documents stay in your account.** A project holds tasks from History; saved papers and PDFs are not shared through it.
- **Members cannot add work to a project they can only view or comment on.** Only owners and editors can move work in.
- **New work does not go into a project automatically.** Move it in from History after it runs (a systematic review is the exception: you pick its project when you create it).
- **People must sign in with the invited email address** to see the project.
