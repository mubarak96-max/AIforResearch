Proposal Studio is the research proposal generator AI in AI for Research. It is for master's and PhD students writing a thesis proposal, and for researchers applying for a grant or ethics approval. You give it a topic or a short description of your study. It searches the literature, then drafts the proposal with citations to real papers.

## What you can draft

Proposal Studio has four modules. Each one is a form with a few fields, and each costs a fixed number of credits shown on the Run button before you start.

| Module | What you give it | Options | Searches papers | Credits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Research proposal | Topic or working title, degree, notes | Write the whole proposal, or one part: title, aims and objectives; background and significance; literature review; methods; timeline; budget outline; ethical considerations | Yes | 15 whole, 3 per part |
| Grant proposal | Project idea, the funding call (paste or upload), notes | Funder format: generic, NIH, NSF, ERC, Wellcome. Whole proposal, or summary or specific aims; significance and innovation; approach and work plan; impact and dissemination; budget justification | Yes | 15 whole, 3 per part |
| Ethics application | A description of your study (paste or upload) | Board: generic, US IRB, UK REC, Australian HREC. Participants: adults at low risk; children or vulnerable groups; patients or clinical data; online or secondary data | No | 15 |
| Data management plan | Your data: what you collect, formats, size, personal data | Format: generic, NIH Data Management and Sharing Plan, NSF, Horizon Europe, UKRI | No | 3 |

The degree setting (undergraduate, honours, master's or PhD) changes the scope and tone of a research proposal. High quality mode, on the Pro plan, uses a stronger model and writes longer answers for three times the credits.

## How it works, step by step

1. Open Proposal Studio and pick a module from the list on the left.
2. Fill in the form. Upload fields take PDF, Word, Markdown or plain text, so you can drop in a funding call or a study summary instead of pasting it.
3. For research and grant proposals, choose where to search: All papers, Medical, Preprints, Web or My Library, with filters for year, study type and journal tier. Under "Your own sources" you can attach files or papers from your Library, and the draft can cite them.
4. Set your role, keep "With headings" on or off, and pick a citation style: APA 7, MLA 9, Chicago or Harvard.
5. Press Run. The tool plans several searches from your topic, reads the papers it finds and writes the draft as it streams in. The cited papers appear in a panel beside the text.
6. Press Open in editor to finish the proposal with its citations and reference list, or Copy to paste it elsewhere. Under "Build on this" you can carry the result into a thesis outline, a gap analysis, slides or a systematic review.

## What the output contains

A whole research proposal has these sections: title, aims and objectives, background and significance, literature review, methods, a timeline table, a budget outline table, ethical considerations and expected outcomes. A whole grant proposal follows the funder format you chose. For NIH, that means Specific Aims (problem, long-term goal, objective, central hypothesis, two or three aims, payoff) and a Research Strategy with Significance, Innovation and Approach, including pitfalls and alternatives for each aim.

Anything only you know is left as a [placeholder] in square brackets: budget amounts, team members, your sample size, dates. The tool is told never to invent data, approvals or institutions.

The ethics application covers a plain-language summary, participants and recruitment, procedures, risks and how they are reduced, benefits, informed consent with a short draft consent statement, confidentiality and data protection, withdrawal and conflicts of interest. The data management plan covers data description, documentation and metadata, storage and security, personal data, sharing and access, preservation, and roles and costs, and names repositories that fit the field, such as Zenodo, Dryad or OSF.

## A worked example

*Example (invented for illustration):* a master's student in nursing plans a study of telehealth follow-up after heart surgery.

1. Research proposal, whole, degree Master's, sources Medical: 15 credits. The draft cites papers on telehealth follow-up and readmission and leaves the sample size as a placeholder.
2. Her supervisor asks for a stronger methods section. She reruns Research proposal with Write set to Methods and adds her notes on the hospital site: 3 credits.
3. Ethics application, Board US IRB, Participants "Patients or clinical data": 15 credits.
4. Data management plan in the NIH format: 3 credits.

Total: 36 credits, inside the Free plan's 45 a month. She then opens each draft in the editor, fills the placeholders and checks every cited paper.

## What it does not do

- It does not know your university's or funder's current template, page limits or deadlines. Paste the call or guidelines so the draft follows them, and check the final layout yourself.
- The ethics application and data management plan do not search the literature. They work only from what you describe.
- It does not submit anything to an ethics board, funder or PROSPERO, and a draft is not an approval. Your institution's committee decides.
- It cannot judge whether your idea is new or fundable. Use Research gaps in the Thesis Suite and talk to your supervisor or program officer.
- Citations are real papers, but the tool can still summarize a paper too broadly. Read the papers you cite.
- Budget figures are placeholders. Costs come from your institution's finance office.
