A scite AI alternative is worth comparing when the question is not "how was this paper received?" but "does my draft say what the evidence says, and are my references sound?" AI for Research answers the second question with Claim Check and Citation Check, for students, academics and anyone checking a manuscript before submission.

## What scite is best at

**Scite is a research platform built around Smart Citations, which classify each citation statement as supporting, contrasting or mentioning, so you can see how later work has treated a study [2].** It reports more than 1.6 billion citations from over 300 million scholarly sources [1]. Around that core it offers an Assistant whose answers link to their sources, a search with alerts, Collections that alert you to new supporting or contrasting citations and retractions, a browser extension that shows citation badges, and a Zotero plugin [1], [2]. Its Reference Check takes an uploaded manuscript and shows whether references have been retracted, received editorial notices or accumulated contrasting findings [2]. Basic costs $12 a month and Pro $40 a month, billed yearly, after a 7-day trial [1].

If you need to know whether a finding has held up across the citing literature, scite does something no other tool on this site does. Use it for that.

## Where AI for Research differs

AI for Research starts from your text rather than from a paper's citation record.

- **Claim Check** tests the claims in your paragraph. It splits the text into up to 10 claims, finds papers for each from All papers or Medical sources, and labels each claim **Supported**, **Partly supported**, **Not supported** or **No evidence found**, with a short explanation, the papers it relied on and an evidence-strength note (for example, strong when a meta-analysis, systematic review or trial supports it). 2 credits.
- **Citation Check** tests your reference list. Each reference is looked up by DOI at Crossref, otherwise by title in OpenAlex. It flags retractions, corrections and expressions of concern (from Crossref's update notices, with Retraction Watch data), preprints, references it cannot find, and wrong years or first authors. No AI, 0 credits.
- **Sentence check.** An optional pass checks that the sentences citing each source say what that source's abstract says. 2 credits.
- **It sits next to the draft.** Upload PDF, Word, Markdown or text (up to 25 MB) or pick a document from the editor, then fix the problems where you write.

Neither is a verdict on truth. They show whether the papers found support your wording, so you can read them and decide.

## How to check a draft in AI for Research, step by step

1. Open **Reviewer** and choose **Claim check**. Paste a paragraph under **Claims to check**, pick **All papers**, **Medical** or both, and click **Check claims** (2 credits).
2. Read each claim's label, explanation and papers. Open the papers marked as the basis and read them.
3. Rewrite claims that came back partly supported or not supported; narrow the wording or add the missing evidence.
4. Switch to **Citation check**. Paste your reference list, or leave it empty and add the document so the References section is used.
5. Tick **Also check that the sentences citing each source say what its abstract says** if you want the AI pass (2 credits).
6. Fix every flagged reference: replace retracted sources, update preprints to the published version, correct years and authors.

### Example: one paragraph from a discussion section

*Example (invented, for illustration).* A master's student pastes a five-sentence paragraph on caffeine and sleep in adolescents. Claim Check returns: 2 supported (one with strong evidence from a meta-analysis), 1 partly supported because the cited trials studied adults, 1 not supported, and 1 with no evidence found. She narrows one claim to "in adults" and cuts the unsupported one. Citation Check on her 42 references flags 1 retraction, 2 preprints that now have journal versions and 1 wrong year. Total cost: 2 credits.

## How to use them together

1. Draft in the editor, then run Claim Check on the paragraphs that carry your argument.
2. For the 5 or 6 studies your conclusions rest on, look them up in scite to see how they have been cited since.
3. Run Citation Check on the full reference list before you submit.
4. Read every paper behind a changed claim. Neither tool replaces reading.

## What AI for Research does not do

- **No citation statements.** It does not show or classify how other papers cite a study.
- **Abstracts, not full texts,** for Claim Check and the sentence check. A paper's abstract can miss a caveat in its results.
- **Up to 10 claims per run.** Longer texts need several runs.
- **No alerts** on papers you cite.
- **Not a judge of truth.** Labels show whether the papers found support your wording. In medicine and law, a qualified professional must review the result.
