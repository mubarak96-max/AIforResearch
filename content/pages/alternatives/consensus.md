If your work doesn't stop at the answer, this Consensus AI alternative fits. AI for Research answers questions from papers with a consensus line, then lets you filter studies by design, save them, check the claims in your draft and write with live citations. For a fast yes-or-no read, Consensus remains an excellent choice.

## What Consensus is best at

Consensus is an AI academic search engine for peer-reviewed literature. It says it searches 400M+ papers drawn from Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, its own crawl and publisher partnerships, and it only uses AI after it has searched the literature, so every answer cites real papers [1][2]. Its signature is the Consensus Meter: for a yes-or-no question it shows how relevant papers split into yes, possibly, no and, since version 2.0, mixed, with a Snapshot of study designs, journal scores and citations for each position [3]. Its free plan includes unlimited Papers searches [4], which makes it a low-risk first stop. If your question is "does X affect Y?" and you want a read on agreement in two minutes, start with Consensus.

Consensus is also honest about its limits. It says a model can misread a paper and summarize it wrongly, and it estimated that the first version of the meter misclassified about 10% of results [2][5]. Those cautions apply to every tool on this page, including ours. Our full [Consensus AI review](/blog/consensus-ai-review) covers it in more depth.

## Where AI for Research differs

The two products overlap on the first step: a cited answer from papers. They differ on what you can do with the answer.

- **More control over sources.** In the Ask box you choose All papers, Medical (PubMed, Europe PMC), Preprints (arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv), Web or My Library, so a clinical question can run on PubMed records and a draft can be checked against your own PDFs.
- **Filters you can see.** Find Papers filters by year, study type, journal quality tier, citations, open access and peer-reviewed only, and leaves out retracted papers by default. It costs 0 credits.
- **A long report when you need one.** Deep (15 credits) writes a 2,500 to 4,000-word report after screening about 250 papers and reading 12 full texts. Detailed (45 credits, paid plans) writes 5,000 to 8,000 words with a study table from 90+ papers and 25 full texts.
- **The steps after the answer.** Open the result in the editor with live citations, run Claim Check on a paragraph (2 credits), check every reference exists with Citation check (0 credits), get a scored review of the whole draft from the Reviewer, or build a deck in Slides.
- **Systematic reviews.** If the question grows into a review, the Systematic Review workspace handles the protocol, logged searches, dual screening with Cohen's kappa, extraction and a PRISMA 2020 diagram from live counts.

## How to use Consensus and AI for Research together

Many researchers keep both. A practical split:

1. **Scope in Consensus.** Ask your yes-or-no question and read the meter and Snapshot to see whether the evidence is thick or thin.
2. **Export the papers.** Download the results as RIS from Consensus.
3. **Bring them in.** In AI for Research, upload the RIS file into a Systematic Review project, or save the key papers to My Library.
4. **Ask again with your filters.** In the Ask box, pick Deep, choose sources (for example Medical), set study type and years, and run it. The answer streams with citations; open the Cited tab to see each study's label.
5. **Save and write.** Click Save cited papers, then Open in editor. Draft the section with cited autocomplete; citations stay live and the reference list builds itself.
6. **Check before you send.** Run Claim Check on your key paragraph and Citation check on the reference list.

## A worked example

*Example (invented, for illustration).* A master's student asks, "Does mindfulness training reduce burnout in hospital nurses?" Consensus shows most relevant papers leaning yes, which tells her the question is worth a chapter. In AI for Research she runs a Deep report on Medical sources, filtered to randomized trials and systematic reviews from 2015 on. The consensus line reads that 5 of 7 studies that test the question support it. She opens the two that do not, finds both used a four-week program, and makes program length a theme in her review. The Deep report used 15 of her 45 free credits; search, saving papers and the citation check cost nothing.

## What AI for Research does not do

- **It does not weigh evidence for you.** The consensus line is a count of labeled studies, like the Consensus Meter. A case series and a large trial each count as one.
- **It reads abstracts when no open full text exists.** Full texts come through Unpaywall; paywalled papers are read from the abstract.
- **No Google Scholar, Scopus or Web of Science search.** Search those through your library and upload the export into a Systematic Review project.
- **No yes-or-no meter graphic.** If a visual split of positions is what you want, Consensus does it well.
- **Detailed reports and Zotero or Mendeley import need a paid plan.**
