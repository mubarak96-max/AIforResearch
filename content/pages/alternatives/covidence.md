A Covidence alternative is worth comparing if you want the search, protocol and methods write-up next to screening. The AI for Research Systematic Review workspace runs PubMed, Europe PMC and OpenAlex searches, takes uploads from other databases, and carries records through two-reviewer screening, extraction and a PRISMA 2020 diagram. It suits students and small review teams.

## What Covidence is best at

**Covidence is a web-based systematic review tool that handles the screening and data extraction stages of a review, with independent dual screening, blinding and structured conflict resolution as standard [2].** Duplicates are removed automatically before screening, exclusion reasons are captured at full text, extraction templates are built once and reused across the team, and risk of bias assessment runs alongside extraction [2]. It produces a PRISMA flow diagram and keeps an audit trail as you work [2], and you can export inter-rater reliability data after screening [3]. It imports from EndNote, Zotero, Mendeley, RefWorks, RevMan and Cochrane's CRS, or any RIS, CSV or PubMed XML file [1]. Cochrane describes it as complementing RevMan in Cochrane review production [4].

For a funded team running a Cochrane-style intervention review with blinded dual screening and formal risk of bias, Covidence is the safe choice. Many universities already license it.

## Where AI for Research differs

- **Search inside the review.** Build searches for PubMed, Europe PMC and OpenAlex with a live record count, then **Run and add**. Each search is logged with date, string, records found, added and duplicates, ready for your methods and PRISMA flow. Exports from Scopus, Web of Science, Embase and others come in as uploads.
- **Protocol in the same place.** Pick one of 8 review types (systematic, scoping, rapid, meta-analysis, mapping, qualitative evidence synthesis, realist, living), a PICO, PCC or SPIDER framework and criteria. An AI draft in the structure PROSPERO asks for costs 3 credits.
- **AI as a helper, not the decider.** AI suggestions give a decision and reason for each record, 1 credit per 25 records. A solo screener can count the AI as second reviewer; with project editors, people screen.
- **Extraction from abstracts with quotes.** AI fills extraction cells from abstracts and shows the words it used, 3 credits per 10 studies.
- **A methods draft.** Written from your search log, criteria, screening and counts; facts it does not have are marked [To add: …]. 3 credits.
- **The rest of the research.** Add the papers of a Deep Research answer as records, or move from the review to the editor, Library and Reviewer in the same account.
- **A free plan.** One review with up to 50 records, enough to pilot your criteria.

## How a review works in AI for Research, step by step

1. Click **New review**. Enter a title and question, choose the **Type of review** and **Framework**, list **Include when** and **Exclude when** criteria, and pick a Project if colleagues will screen with you.
2. On **Protocol**, draft the protocol from your criteria and edit it.
3. On **Search**, type a string for PubMed, Europe PMC or OpenAlex, watch the count, and click **Run and add**. Use **Upload an export** for other databases.
4. On **Screening**, work through **Title and abstract**, then **Full text**. Press I, E or M. Records you mark maybe at title and abstract go on to full text. Settle disagreements in **Conflicts**.
5. On **Extraction**, fill the table for included studies by hand or with AI, add columns, and rate the three risk-of-bias fields.
6. On **Report**, download the PRISMA 2020 diagram as SVG or PNG, export **All records and decisions (CSV)**, **All records (RIS)** or **Included (RIS)**, and draft the **Methods section**.

### Example: a master's scoping review

*Example (invented, for illustration).* A student and her supervisor review digital tools for medication adherence in older adults. She runs 3 searches (PubMed 412, Europe PMC 268, OpenAlex 190) and uploads a CINAHL export of 133 records: 1,003 records, 297 duplicates removed, 706 screened. Her supervisor, an editor on the Project, double-screens; kappa at title and abstract is 0.71 (substantial). 64 go to full text, 22 are included. The PRISMA diagram updates as they go, and the methods draft takes 3 credits. On Plus, AI suggestions for the first 200 records would cost 8 credits.

## How to use them together

1. Write the protocol and run and log your searches in AI for Research.
2. Export **All records (RIS)** after deduplication and import it into Covidence for blinded screening and risk of bias, if your protocol requires them.
3. Bring the final counts back into your write-up, or screen in AI for Research when blinding is not required.

## What the workspace does not do

- **No blind mode.** Reviewers can see each other's decisions and the AI suggestion on a record.
- **Simple risk of bias.** Three fields with low, some concerns, high, unclear or not applicable; not a full RoB 2 or ROBINS-I tool.
- **PRISMA limits.** Records from other methods are counted in the databases column, the removed-before-screening box shows duplicates only, and full-text "maybe" counts as not yet assessed.
- **Kappa limits.** Calculated between you and one other reviewer (or the AI), across include, exclude and maybe, with no confidence interval.
- **No PDF viewer.** Full-text screening links out to the DOI or PubMed record.
- **No meta-analysis** or forest plot yet.
- **AI extraction reads abstracts only.** Check every cell against the full text. Reviewers decide, not the AI.
