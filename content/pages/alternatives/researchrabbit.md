A Research Rabbit alternative is useful when browsing is no longer enough and you have to show how you searched. Citation Map in AI for Research turns a set of papers into numbered backward and forward snowballing rounds, ranks what your set links to, and exports the result. It is for thesis writers and review teams. Mapping costs 0 credits.

If you want a list of seven discovery tools, read [Research Rabbit alternatives](/blog/research-rabbit-alternatives). This page compares ResearchRabbit with one tool, side by side.

## What ResearchRabbit is best at

**ResearchRabbit is a literature discovery app: you add papers you like to collections and it recommends related work, new authors and emerging topics, building a map as you browse [1], [2].** Its strength is exploration that compounds. Each paper you add changes the suggestions, so a collection becomes a running model of your topic. The free plan is one of the most generous in this category: unlimited searches across 310+ million articles, an unlimited library and collections, shared collections and up to 50 seed articles, at no cost [1]. ResearchRabbit+ adds up to 300 seeds, advanced search controls, multiple projects and Signals alerts [1]. A rebuilt version launched in October 2025 through a partnership with Litmaps [3], after Litmaps announced it was acquiring ResearchRabbit [4].

For open-ended reading in the first months of a PhD, it is hard to beat. Stay with it if that is what you need.

## Where AI for Research differs

- **Rounds, not wandering.** In Citation Map you choose **Backward**, **Forward** or **Both**, and each round reads the whole set at once. Every paper is tagged with its round (R0, R1, R2), so you can say exactly where it came from.
- **Ranked by agreement.** Candidates are ordered by how many papers in your set link to them. A paper cited by 4 of your 6 key studies comes first.
- **Seen papers stay hidden.** **Hide papers already seen** keeps each round to new candidates, which shows you when the search has saturated.
- **Reviews citing your set.** One click lists reviews and meta-analyses that cite your papers.
- **Then the rest of the work.** Export RIS or CSV, upload into a [systematic review](/features/systematic-review) for screening and a PRISMA diagram, or save to your [Library](/features/research-library) to read with page-cited answers and write in the editor.

## How Citation Map works, step by step

1. Open [Citation Map](/features/citation-map) and paste a DOI or title, then click **Map it**. Or start **From my Library** (up to 10 papers) or from the papers of a Deep Research answer.
2. On the Map tab, read the graph: seeds in orange, papers placed by year, circle size by citations.
3. Add papers to your set with the plus button in the References or Citing lists. Filter by year, minimum citations or study type.
4. Run a **Backward**, **Forward** or **Both** round. Read the ranked candidates on the Snowball tab and add the ones that fit your criteria.
5. Click **Find reviews citing the set** to check what published reviews include.
6. Repeat until a round adds nothing new, then export **RIS** or **CSV**.

### Example: a nursing PhD that has to report its search

*Example (invented, for illustration).* A PhD student has a ResearchRabbit collection of 34 papers on nurse staffing and patient falls. Her supervisor asks how she found them. She exports the collection to Zotero, picks the 8 most central papers and starts a Citation Map from her Library. Round 1 (Both) returns 60 candidates; she adds 12. Round 2, with seen papers hidden, returns 60 new candidates; she adds 3. Round 3 adds none, so she stops. Her methods note: "Backward and forward citation searching from 8 seed papers in three rounds (November 2026) identified 15 additional records." She keeps the ResearchRabbit collection for new-paper browsing.

## Which should you pick?

| If you want to... | Pick |
| --- | --- |
| Browse a topic freely and keep collections at no cost | ResearchRabbit |
| Share a live collection with a co-author | ResearchRabbit |
| Hear about new related papers | ResearchRabbit+ (Signals) or Litmaps |
| Report backward and forward citation searching | Citation Map |
| Find reviews that cite your key papers | Citation Map |
| Screen, extract and draw a PRISMA diagram from the same papers | AI for Research |

## What it costs

Citation Map, its exports and Save to Library cost 0 credits on every plan. AI work around it uses credits: a Summary answer in Deep Research costs 1 credit, a Deep report 15, and a question to a PDF in your Library 1. The free plan has 45 credits a month; Plus is $12 a month for 750 credits and Pro $29 a month for 1,500. See [pricing](/pricing).

## How to use them together

1. Explore in ResearchRabbit until your collection settles.
2. Move the core papers into AI for Research through Zotero or Mendeley import (paid plans), a RIS file, or by pasting DOIs.
3. Run Citation Map rounds from that core and record each round.
4. Screen the combined set in a Systematic Review, or read and write from your Library.
5. Keep the ResearchRabbit collection running to catch new work, and add it at your next search update.

## What Citation Map does not do

- **No recommendations feed or alerts.** It finds what is linked to your papers today.
- **No similarity suggestions.** Papers with no citation link to your set will not appear. Pair it with a keyword search in [Find Papers](/features/paper-search).
- **Fixed limits per step.** Up to 10 seeds per map, the first 25 set papers per round, up to 60 candidates per round.
- **No shared live map.** You share an export of the set, not the map.
- **Coverage follows OpenAlex,** which is thinner for books, some humanities work and very new papers.

## Who it is for

PhD and master's students who must defend their search in a viva or methods chapter, and researchers in medicine and health who report citation searching under PRISMA. See [AI for PhD students](/for/phd) and [AI for medical research](/for/medicine).
