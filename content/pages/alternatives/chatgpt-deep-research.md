If you use ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude for deep research and your question is answered by published studies, this ChatGPT deep research alternative is built for that case. AI for Research searches 250M+ scholarly works, filters by study type and year, labels how each study bears on your question and opens the report in an editor with live citations.

## What the general assistants' deep research modes are best at

All three general assistants now run multi-step research and write long reports with citations. Each does something well.

- **ChatGPT deep research** plans searches, reads sources and writes a structured report; OpenAI says a run takes 5 to 30 minutes [1]. You can restrict research to websites you list or prioritize them, connect apps with read-only access, and download the report as Markdown, Word or PDF [2]. Its pricing page lists deep research on every plan, "limited" on Free and Go, "expanded" on Plus and "maximum" on Pro [3].
- **Gemini deep research** shows a research plan you can edit before it runs and can browse hundreds of websites in a session [4]. It is free for signed-in users aged 18 or over, takes about 5 to 10 minutes, can use your uploads, Drive, Gmail and NotebookLM, and exports to Google Docs [5]. Google AI Plus, Pro and Ultra raise the limits [6].
- **Claude Research** runs several searches that build on each other and can use connectors such as Gmail, Google Calendar and Google Docs, which helps when your notes and drafts live there [7]. It is on paid plans: Pro costs $17 a month billed annually or $20 monthly, Max starts at $100 [8].

For a background section that mixes papers with policy documents, statistics and news, these tools are often the better choice. Our guides on [ChatGPT deep research for academic work](/blog/chatgpt-deep-research-for-academic-work) and [Gemini deep research for a literature search](/blog/gemini-deep-research-for-literature-search) go deeper.

## Where AI for Research differs

**The general assistants rank web pages; AI for Research searches paper records first and treats the web as one optional source.** That changes three things.

1. **What it reads.** Sources are All papers, Medical (PubMed, Europe PMC), Preprints (arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv), Web and My Library. Open full texts are found through Unpaywall. Every result is a paper with authors, year, journal and DOI, not a landing page.
2. **How you narrow it.** Filters for year, study type, journal quality tier, citations, open access and peer-reviewed only are applied before anything is read. Retracted papers are left out by default. With a web agent you can only ask in the prompt.
3. **How it cites.** Citation markers that point to no real source are dropped. Each cited study gets a label for how it bears on your question, and a consensus line counts them ("2 of 4 studies that test the question support it"). The Cited, All read and Web tabs show what was used and what was read.

After the report, Save cited papers puts them in your Library, and Open in editor turns the report into a document whose citations stay live in any of 10,000+ styles.

## How to use a general assistant and AI for Research together

1. **Orient with the assistant.** Ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude for a broad report. Note the key terms, authors and the reviews it cites.
2. **Ask the focused question here.** In the Ask box, choose Deep, pick sources (for example All papers and Medical), set study types and years, and start. The cost is shown before you run it.
3. **Compare.** Open All read and check whether the reviews the assistant found appear. If one is missing, find it in Find Papers and save it.
4. **Keep the papers.** Click Save cited papers. Open a key paper in Citation Map to see references and citing papers (0 credits).
5. **Write.** Click Open in editor, draft with cited autocomplete, and run Citation check on the reference list before you submit (0 credits).

## A worked example

*Example (invented, for illustration).* A PhD student asks a general assistant about retrieval practice for adult learners. The report is clear and cites 30 sources, but 11 are blogs and university teaching pages, and two well-known meta-analyses appear only through their abstracts. In AI for Research she asks, "Does retrieval practice improve long-term retention in adult learners?", with All papers, peer-reviewed only, and study types set to meta-analyses and randomized trials. The Deep report (15 credits) cites 24 papers, all peer-reviewed; the consensus line counts how many of the studies that test the question support it, and the Cited tab shows which are meta-analyses. She saves them and opens the report in the editor.

## What AI for Research does not do

- **It does not search Google Scholar, Scopus or Web of Science.** Search those through your library.
- **It reads abstracts when no open full text exists,** just as web agents do for paywalled papers.
- **It does not connect to Gmail, Drive or calendars.** Your own material comes in as PDFs and documents in the Library, or Zotero and Mendeley import on paid plans.
- **The consensus line is a count,** not a weight of evidence.
- **Detailed reports need a paid plan;** the free plan allows 2 Deep reports a day.
