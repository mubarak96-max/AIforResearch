An AI market research tool is only as good as its sources. AI for Research answers market, technology and policy questions from research papers and the open web, cites every claim, and shows how a field has grown over time. It is for R&D, strategy, consulting and policy teams who need the evidence behind a market, not just the headlines.

## Three jobs you can do here

### 1. A cited report on a market or technology question

Ask a question such as "How mature is solid-state battery manufacturing, and what limits scale-up?" Choose **All papers** and **Web**, and **Deep** depth (15 credits). You get a 2,500 to 4,000-word report with figures, built from about 250 papers screened and 12 read in full, plus web sources in their own tab. **Detailed** (45 credits, paid plans) runs 5,000 to 8,000 words with a study table from 90+ papers; you review its plan of sub-questions before it starts.

### 2. Map the research landscape

**Landscape** (free) shows a topic's works per year, top topics, journals, authors, institutions, open-access share and most-cited works, all from OpenAlex. Compare two topics side by side, for example "perovskite solar cells" and "tandem solar cells", to see which is growing faster and which institutions lead.

### 3. Turn the report into a deck and share it

From the result, **Make slides** under Build on this opens Slides (10 credits): a deck with speaker notes and a references slide, downloaded as PowerPoint on paid plans. Move the report and deck into a **project** and invite colleagues as Can edit, Can comment or Can view.

## How it works, step by step

1. **Set your role** to Industry or policy so reports are pitched for a professional reader.
2. **Ask** at Deep depth with All papers and Web. Add Preprints for fast-moving fields. Use filters for years (for example the last five) and peer-reviewed only.
3. **Read the tabs.** Cited lists the sources used, All read lists everything screened, Web lists the web pages. Ask follow-up questions (1 credit each).
4. **Run Landscape** on the same topic to check the report's picture of the field against publication counts.
5. **Build the deck.** Choose Make slides under Build on this, review the deck on screen, and download the .pptx.
6. **Share.** Move the work into a project and invite your team by email.

**Example (invented):** a strategy analyst asks "What is the evidence that plant-based meat reduces greenhouse gas emissions compared with beef?" at Deep depth with All papers and Web. The report summarizes the life-cycle assessments, notes where the studies' system boundaries differ, and lists the industry reports from the Web tab separately from the peer-reviewed studies. Landscape shows publications on the topic by year, and a 12-slide deck goes to the team for a Monday meeting.

## AI-use rules that matter to you

- **Confidential material.** Follow your company's policy on external AI tools. Do not upload documents under NDA, unreleased financials or customer data without approval. AI for Research does not use your documents to train AI.
- **Check before you quote.** Every claim cites a source, but AI text can overstate a finding. Open the source before a number goes into a board deck.
- **Say where it came from.** If a client or regulator asks how a report was made, state that AI drafted it from cited sources and a person checked it.
- **Patents and legal questions** need a professional; this is not a patent or legal search tool.

## What it does not do

- No market sizing data, survey panels or paid databases such as Gartner or Statista. Public economic series from FRED, the OECD and US Treasury are in Official Sources. Patent databases and company filings are not searched.
- The Web source reads open pages; it cannot read paywalled reports or sites behind a login.
- Paywalled papers are read from the abstract.
- It does not forecast. Reports summarize published evidence.
